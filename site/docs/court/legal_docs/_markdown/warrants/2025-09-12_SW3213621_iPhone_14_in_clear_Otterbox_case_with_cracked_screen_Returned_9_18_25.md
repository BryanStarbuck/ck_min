I am familiar with the facts and circumstances set forth herein as a result of a review of records, digital surveillance camera footage, and conversations with other law enforcement officers, and my own investigative efforts, as more fully described below. 

On September 10, 2025, Charles James Kirk was at the Utah Valley University (UVU) campus in Orem, Utah, for a political event. Mr. Kirk is a conservative political activitst who was scheduled to speak at an event in the courtyard area on the UVU campus. The courtyard area is to the west of the Losee Center building. According to video recordings, Charles was wearing a white T-shirt and dark pants, sitting under a white canopy in a courtyard of the campus facing to the east. Charles was holding a microphone and speaking to a crowd. While speaking into the microphone, a loud gunshot was heard at approximately 1223 hours, and Charles appeared to have been hit in the neck. Blood was seen coming out of the left side of his neck as he fell over to his left. Charles was transported to the Timpanogos Regional Hospital in Orem. Charles was pronounced deceased at the hospital. Charles’ body was transported to the Office of the Medical Examiner, where an autopsy was performed on the body. According to information obtained from the police officer who attended the autopsy, Sergeant Barrett with the Utah State Bureau of Investigation, Dr. Guarjado with the Office of the Medical Examiner concluded the autopsy and explained that he was going to certify that Charles Kirk’s cause of death was a homicide caused by a gunshot wound. 

Investigators reviewed the Utah Valley University surveillance cameras. Hereafter, the unidentified individual will be referred to as “Robinson”. Based on surveillance camera footage, Robinson appears to be a white male with dark colored hair, wearing a blue colored hat, sunglasses, a long sleeve black shirt with a picture of an American flag and an eagle

While processing the crime scene, evidence technicians were able to identify the locations where Robinson, through physical contact, had left potential biological evidence. Touch DNA transfers when skin cells, from touch or sweat, are shed onto an object during direct or indirect contact, and it can also transfer from one person to another. This type of DNA evidence comes from epithelials cells, or skin cells, that contain cellular DNA and can be recovered from a wide range of surfaces with the use of forensic DNA techniques. This evidence was collected in order to obtain a potential DNA profile for Robinson. Evidence technicians also identified fingerprints and a smeared palm print in the vicinity of the northeast corner rooftop edge of the Losee Center building where Robinson dropped off the rooftop to the ground below.

Through my training and experience, your affiant knows that cell phones contain historical information including text messages, photographs, device location information, social media activity, emails, and videos among other things. Through my training and experience, your affiant knows that those engaged in criminal activity who use electronic devices have the same type of electronic storage data stored on their electronic devices, such as cellphones. I have investigated many cases where cell phone forensic searches have turned up valuable evidence, including location data, images, video, text messages, social media posts, and other evidence. In my experience those involved in criminal activity frequently communicate about their ideals, plans, motives, and even criminal acts and searches of messaging data often reveal this evidence. 

I know that cellphones keep a historical record of the device’s location, text messages and other communications, images, videos, social media usage and history, websites visited and searches conducted. These materials can be recovered even if deleted through forensic searches. Accordingly, I am requesting authority to seize the cellphone described and conduct a forensic search using forensic tools such as Cellebrite, or other tools used in digital forensics.

The facts to establish the grounds for issuance of a Search Warrant are: 

Your affiant, Agent Justin Bowen, is employed by the Utah Department of Public Safety Sate Bureau of Investigation and is currently assigned to the Major Crimes unit. Your affiant has been given the responsibility to investigate homicides, violent crimes, weapons offenses, fraud, domestic violence crimes, sex offenses, offenses related to vehicle crashes and narcotics offenses among others. Your affiant has been through Peace Officer Standards and Training, and graduated August 2018. Your affiant has received several specialized trainings in different areas, including sex crimes investigations, fraud detection and prevention, domestic violence incidents, and homicides. Your affiant is currently certified as a Certified Fraud Examiner. Your affiant has investigated or assisted in investigations involving homicides, firearms offenses, death investigations, assault offenses, property crimes, crash investigations, and impaired driver investigations. 

Through my training and experience, your affiant knows that cell phones contain historical information including text messages, photographs, device location information, social media activity, emails, and videos among other things. Through my training and experience, your affiant knows that those engaged in criminal activity who use electronic devices have the same type of electronic storage data stored on their electronic devices, such as cellphones. I have investigated many cases where cell phone forensic searches have turned up valuable evidence, including location data, images, video, text messages, social media posts, and other evidence. In my experience those involved in criminal activity frequently communicate about their ideals, plans, motives, and even criminal acts and searches of messaging data often reveal this evidence. 

I know that phones keep a historical record of the device’s location, text messages and other communications, images, videos, social media usage and history, websites visited and searches conducted. These materials can be recovered even if deleted through forensic searches. Accordingly, I am requesting authority to seize the phone described and conduct a forensic search using forensic tools such as cellebrite, or other tools used in digital forensics. 

The following information was obtained by your affiant from police officers, multiple video recordings posted on social media websites, Utah Valley University campus surveillance cameras and those who witnessed the incident. 

On September 10, 2025, Charles James Kirk was at the Utah Valley University campus in Orem, Utah, for a political event. According to video recordings, Charles was wearing a white T-shirt and dark pants, sitting under a white canopy in a courtyard of the campus facing to the east. Charles was holding a microphone and speaking to a crowd. While speaking into the microphone, a loud gunshot was heard at approximately 1223 hours, and Charles appeared to have been hit in the neck. Blood was seen coming out of the left side of his neck as he fell over to his left. Charles was transported to the Timpanogos Regional Hospital in Orem. Charles was pronounced deceased at the hospital.

Investigators received information that a post had been made on “X” (a social media website) that contained threats to Charlie Kirk. The post on “X” was under the account handle “Omar (@NajraGalvz). The post said “Charlie kirk is coming to my college tomorrow I rlly hope someone evaporates him literally”. A later post by the same user read “Lets just say something big will happen tomorrow”. A photo posted by the same “X” handle depicts an individual in what appears to be a “selfie”. We know that the individual in the photo is not the same as the individual associated with that “X” account, described as Omar Najera below. Special Agents with the Federal Bureau of Investigation sent an emergency request to a social media account on “X” for an account associated with “Omar (@NajraGalvz). The “X” account was associated with a phone number 801-473-8004. That phone number is serviced by Verizon Wireless. Another emergency disclosure was served to Verizon for that same phone number. The information obtained from Verizon Wireless showed that the phone number was associated with 385-448-8574, which was listed Diana Galvez as the account holder’s phone number. The address associated with that Verizon Wireless account is 245 West 1730 South Payson, Utah. 

Investigators conducted a records check for a Omar Najera. The results showed that Omar Najera had a registered address of 245 West 1730 South Payson, Utah. This address is the same address associated with the Verizon Wireless account holder. Investigators queried the Utah Valley University’s student records and identified that Omar Najera was a registered student at the Utah Valley University. Investigators obtained historical cell phone location information for the phone number associated with the “X” account, 801473-8004. The historical location information for the device associated with that phone number showed that the device was in the vicinity of the Utah Valley University’s campus on 9/10/2025 from approximately 0900 and departed the campus at approximately 1110 hours. During the approximate time of the shooting (approximately 1222 hours) at Utah Valley University, the device location was located in the vicinity of the home address in Payson. For this reason and based on other surveillance of an individual captured UVU surveillance, we do not believe Omar is the shooter. However, due to the post made from the phone described, we believe the is probable cause that the phone and its contents, including the posts described are evidence of the offense described. 

Investigators with the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) made contact with Omar Najera on 9/11/2025 at his residence in Payson. According to information obtained from the FBI, Najera confirmed that he made the post on “X”. Najera also confirmed that he still had the cellphone in his possession. Najera also explained to FBI investigators that he changed his social media handle on “X” after the incident. Najera’s new “X” handle is “goat…(@reunicluh)”. 

It is not believed that Omar Najera is the suspected shooter. However, due to the threatening nature of Najera’s social media posts on “X”, investigators are seeking a search warrant to seize Najera’s cellphone in connection with the death investigation. 

This affidavit has been reviewed by Ryan McBride of the Utah County DA Office, and it has been approved for presentation to the court.

The undersigned affiant, Agent Justin Bowen of the Utah State Bureau of Investigation, makes application for an order of permission to extend the search warrant sealing time in accordance with Utah Rule of Criminal Procedure 40(m). 

On 11th day of September 2025, the Court issued search warrant 3212881, which included a 90 day sealing time in accordance with Utah Rule of Criminal Procedure 40(i). However, the police investigation is still ongoing and unsealing the search warrant after ninety days would cause a substantial risk of harm to a person’s safety, pose a clearly unwarranted invasion of or harm to a person’s reputation or privacy, or pose a serious impediment to the investigation, based on the following facts: 

- e Cause a substantial risk of harm to a person’s safety e Pose a clearly unwarranted invasion of or harm to a person’s reputation or privacy 

This investigation has garnered global attention. As a result, people associated with the investigation have been identified in the media and on social media. Association with the investigation in any capacity has made individuals the focus of both positive but more importantly negative attention with the potential for individuals to be targeted. Online abuse such as, Doxxing (the search for and publishing of private or identifying information about a particular individual on the internet, typically with malicious intent.), disclosure of personal information resulting in swatting (the action or practice of making a prank call to emergency services in an attempt to bring about the dispatch of a large number of armed police officers to a particular address) and a variety of methods have been used to harass and threaten those involved, including death threats. Out of an abundance of caution and respect for those associated with the investigation the affiant requests that this warrant, that contains identifying information that could be used to target an individual, be sealed for an extended period. 

For the foregoing reasons and pursuant to Utah Rule of Criminal Procedure 40(m) (4), the State respectfully requests the Court allow the warrant to remain sealed until 06/10/2026.

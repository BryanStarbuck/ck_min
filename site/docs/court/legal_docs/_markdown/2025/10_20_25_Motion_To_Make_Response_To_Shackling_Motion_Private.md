IN THE FOURTH JUDICIAL DISTRICT COURT UTAH COUNTY, STATE OF UTAH 

STATE OF UTAH, **MOTION TO CLASSIFY AS “PRIVATE” THE RESPONSE TO** Plaintiff, **DEFENDANT’S MOTION TO APPEAR AT ALL IN-PERSON** vs. **PROCEEDINGS IN CIVILIAN CLOTHING AND WITHOUT** TYLER JAMES ROBINSON, **RESTRAINTS** Defendant. Case No. 251403576 Honorable Tony F. Graf, Jr. 

On October 13, 2025, this Court emailed counsel an Interim Order ordering 

the State and the Utah County Sheriff’s Office (“Sheriff’s Office”) to respond to 

Defendant’s Motion to Appear in Civilian Clothing and Without Restraints. The 

October 13th Interim Order permits the Sheriff’s Office to file its response “as a 

sealed document” if it contains “secure information.” _See_ October 13, 2025 Interim Order. 

The response does include information from the Court’s Security Director, Chris Palmer, regarding specific security measures in this case. The Court should therefore limit access to the response. 

Although the Court’s October 13th Interim Order contemplated classifying as “sealed,” a response that contains “secure information.” The State and the Sheriff’s Office believe that the more apt classification should be “private,” 

because not even counsel for the parties can assess a “sealed” court record without a court order. _See_ Utah R. Jud. Admin. 4-202.03(2). Therefore, pursuant to Rule 4-202.04 of the Utah Rules of Judicial Administration, the State and the 

Sheriff’s Office request that the Court classify the response as “private.” 

On October 14, 2025, this Court entered on the docket a modified Interim Order ordering the State the Sheriff’s Office to respond to Defendant’s motion. The docketed October 14 Interim Order omits the reference to allowing the 

response to be filed under seal. The State and the Sheriff’s Office believe that this omission was a mere oversight. But even if this omission were intentional, the Court should nevertheless classify the response as “private” because it contains secure information that might endanger “personal and public safety” if 

disclosed. _See_ Utah R. Jud. Admin. 4-202(2)(B) (recognizing that interests of 

“personal and public safety” justify restricting access to certain court records).

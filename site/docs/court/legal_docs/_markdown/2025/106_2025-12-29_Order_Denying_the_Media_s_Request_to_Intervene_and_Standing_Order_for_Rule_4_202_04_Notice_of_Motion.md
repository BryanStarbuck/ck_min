Two groups of media outlets seek to intervene in this matter. (See Dkt. 164, 197). “Only the State and the defendant,” however, “are actual parties to a criminal action.” State v. Lane, 2009 UT 35, 4 17, 212 P.3d 529, 532. The press need not be a party to a criminal action to pursue the public’s presumptive right to access public court records. See Soc’y ofPro. Journalists v. Bullock, 743 P.2d 1166, 1173-76 (Utah 1987) (discussing development of standing through denial of access). The press has already been provided with a mechanism through Rule 4-202.04 to protect that right and has already utilized it in this matter. See Utah R. Jud. Admin. 4-202.04(4); (see also Dkt. 150, 164, 171). For this reason, the court denies both requests to intervene. 

The court now reiterates and clarifies its Order Requiring Parties to Serve All Closure or Reclassification Motions on the Media dated November 10, 2025. (See Dkt. 171) as follows:

Again, the parties are ORDERED to serve all closures motions on members ofthe press who have requested notice. The parties must file proofof service with the court. 

A “closure motion” is any motion to classify or reclassify a presumptively public court record under Rule 4-202.02 as any other classification. Compare Utah R. Jud. Admin. 4- 202.02(2) with (3)-(5). The parties need not serve notice on members of the press of<sup>court</sup> records that are presumptively not public. See Utah R. Admin. 4-202.02(3)-(5). 

A “court record” means a record that is prepared, owned, received, or retained by this 

court. Utah R. Jud. Admin. 4-202.01(2). This includes hearings. See id. at (3). Therefore, the parties are ORDERED to serve all motions to close hearings on members ofthe press who have requested notice. The parties must likewise file proofof service with the court. 

Nonetheless, the parties are not required to serve notice on members of the press of motions to /imit electronic media coverage. Compare Utah R. Jud. Admin. 4-401.01(2) (permitting courts to restrict or limit electronic media coverage) with 4-202.04. 

If members of<sup>themediaintendtoobjecttoaclosuremotionandrequestaclosure</sup> hearing, such objections and requests must be filed within seven (7) days of<sup>receivingnotice.To</sup> promote judicial economy and to eliminate unnecessary hearings, the court may decline to hold a closure hearing if no hearing is requested. 

The foregoing should be treated by the parties as a standing order for the duration of<sup>this</sup> 

case.

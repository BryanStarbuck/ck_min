- **II. Mr. Robinson’s Motion to Classify Was Intended to Expedite Obtaining the Transcript and Unseal It So That the Parties Could Review It In Order to Determine if An Interlocutory Appeal Should be Sought.** 

The media fundamentally misunderstands the classification process and Mr. Robinson’s 

motion to classify. This likewise requires clarification. 

As explained in his motion to classify, the October 24, 2025, hearing was closed/sealed. 

_See_ Dkt No. 152. As a result, the records from that hearing, including the audio, were presumptively sealed. This classification prevented the parties from even ordering a transcript for their own litigation purposes. _See_ Utah R. Jud. Admin. 4-202.03(2) (“No one may access a sealed court record except as authorized . . . or by order of the court. A judge may review a sealed court record when the circumstances warrant.”). The court reporter required an order from the Court in order to prepare the transcript and, in order for the parties to review the transcript without violating the rules of judicial administration, the classification of the record needed to be “lowered” from “sealed” to “private.” The State stipulated to Mr. Robinson’s request, recognizing the difficulty in potentially seeking appellate relief without having a transcript from the hearing at issue. 

With the motion, as per normal procedure, Mr. Robinson submitted a proposed order. See Dkt No. 154. Had the Court signed the classification order as filed by Mr. Robinson, this would have been entirely proper. Utah Rule of Judicial Administration 4-202.04(3)(A) anticipates that the reclassification occurs temporarily, instructing that upon filing a motion to classify “as private, protected, sealed, safeguarded” the Court “must deny access to the record until the court enters an order.” Signing an order, temporarily, while awaiting a hearing is exactly what courts are required to do in these circumstances. Nonetheless, the Court properly issued its own order, requiring heightened protections, above that which the “private” designation generally provides,

Although it may be frustrating for the media to have to address factual matters partially unavailable to them, the media’s request violates the rule upon which it relies. 

With these procedural clarifications—that Mr. Robinson sought to broaden access to the transcript and audio—Mr. Robinson turns to the Media’s request to classify the hearing transcript and audio as public. Dkt No. 220, at 16-19.

# **IV. Mr. Robinson’s Only Goal is to Avoid a “Media Circus” Which Deprives Him of His Right to a Fair Trial.** 

As the above should demonstrate, Mr. Robinson has not sought to exclude the Media 

from these proceedings. Mr. Robinson’s constant refrain in responding to the plenary of Media filings is that the Court must make appropriate findings and orders that protect his constitutional right to a fair trial in the midst of what could quickly become (or perhaps already has become) a media circus. Such orders should include establishing clear parameters on the Media’s role and a concrete procedure for the Media to seek the relief permissible under Utah Rule of Judicial Administration 4-202.04. 

Further, Mr. Robinson asks this Court to provide deadlines for media objections to motions to classify or close in order to avoid last minute filings, as was done here. Mr. Robinson served _Deseret News et al_ . and _The Salt Lake Tribune et al_ . with the motion to reclassify on November 13, 2025. Dkt No. 173. Motion deadlines are governed by Utah Rule of Civil Procedure 7, requiring opposing memorandum to be filed within fourteen (14) days. _See_ Utah R. Civ. P. 7(d) & 81.  Accounting for the Thanksgiving holiday, the Media’s opposition should

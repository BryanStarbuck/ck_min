Defendant, Tyler James Robinson, by and through undersigned counsel hereby files this reply in support of his _Motion to Classify Application for Certificate to Secure Out-Of-State Witness_ seeking to classify the transcript of the witnesses’ 1102 statement provided in support of the request for an order permitting Mr. Robinson to subpoena an out-of-state subpoena. Dkt 592. Where the State did not object to Mr. Robinson’s motion, Mr. Robinson’s reply largely responds 

to the issues raised in the _News Media’s Memorandum Opposing Motion to Classify Application_ . 

- **I. The State’s “Mooting” of the Witness Identity Safety and Security Concern by Publicly Revealing Information Currently Classified as Private Should Not Be Tolerated.** 

The Court has provided a process, consistent with the Utah Rules of Judicial 

Administration, to consider the interests of the parties and others impacted by the classification of court records and the information within them. This process was provided to the parties in an order in December of 2025. _See_ Dkt 267; _also_ Dkt 417 (amending opposition deadline). 

As to the present motion to classify, care was taken by the defense to protect the identity and location of an important witness in this highly publicized capital case by privately filing Mr. Robinson’s pleading seeking to procure an order from this Court to begin the process of subpoenaing the witness to the preliminary hearing. Such care was taken because the Utah Rules of Judicial Administration note the “not exhaustive” list of interests served by non-public court records include: protecting personal privacy, Utah R. Jud. Admin. 4-202(2)(A); protecting personal and public safety, _id_ . 4-202(2)(B); and protecting “non-parties participating in the court process, such as victims, witnesses,” _id_ . 4-202(2)(E). 

_Despite the existing classification of the application and its attachments as private_ , even if ultimately a temporary classification, the State chose to file a public opposition to the application identifying the witness. _See_ Dkt 605. In this pleading, the State also described some

portions of the witness’s statements that were attached to the defense pleading and, again, classified as private pursuant to Utah Rule of Judicial Administration 4-202.04(3)(A) (when a motion to classify is filed the “court must deny access to the record until the court enters an order”). Effectively, the State subverted the Court’s order regarding classification issues, shortcutting due process and removing the ability of this Court to determine, after weighing the interests (in this case, the safety and privacy interests of a witness), if certain information should remain private. 

This was entirely inappropriate and such a practice should be discouraged. This Court’s orders apply to the parties equally and the State may disagree with a motion to classify filed by the defense, as it has in the past, by filing an objection to the motion. _See_ Dkt 267, 417. This Court should admonish the State that such conduct will not be tolerated. 

In light of what cannot be undone, Mr. Robinson asks this Court for relief in the alternative, that the defense be permitted to redact the potential location information for the witness from the application and exhibits. 

As discussed further herein, Mr. Robinson also maintains his request to classify Exhibit A to counsel’s declaration in support of the application (i.e. the 1102 interview transcript) as private and also asks this Court to permit redaction of any descriptions of specific statements made by the witness in the interview in the application and other attachments. 

# **II. This Court Should Maintain the Classification of the 1102 Interview Transcript and References Thereto in the Filing as Private.** 

The News Media’s Opposition to Mr. Robinson’s request to classify the 1102 interview 

transcript seeks to parse the language from _State v. Archuleta_ , 857 P.2d 234, 241 (Utah 1993) regarding the physical handling and copying of exhibits to limit the case holding to only what physically happens to an exhibit in the courtroom. 

Page **3** of **6** 

However, the News Media’s reading of _Archuleta_ is not consistent with the realities of a Utah courtroom in 2026. Notably, _Archuleta_ was decided a full twenty years before the Utah Supreme Court Rules Committee adopted Utah Rule of Judicial Administration 4-401.01 in 2013, allowing electronic media coverage in Utah courtrooms. At the time of _Archuleta_ , the concept of “copying” an exhibit was a literal, physical process whereby the exhibit would need to be handled by the public or media. Now, such handling is not necessary—all it takes is a live feed or a click of a camera to reproduce an exhibit to millions of individuals worldwide. The “copying” in _Archuleta_ occurs in myriad ways in the digital age, as this Court likely recognized when prohibiting electronic devices at the preliminary hearing in its _Minute Entry Order Re: Electronic Devices in Courtroom Prohibited During Preliminary Hearing_ (Dkt 632). 

Further, the reasoning underlying _Archuleta_ is what should inform the Court’s decision here, not the literalities promoted by the News Media. As _Archuleta_ reasoned: 

The Press is certainly entitled to attend preliminary hearings in appropriate circumstances and to report all that occurs there. However, allowing blanket inspection of exhibits that must be preserved for a later trial if the defendant is bound over to the district court will hinder, not further, the interests of the criminal process. 

857 P.2d at 242. Allowing access to one of the State’s exhibits will hinder the criminal process. 

The News Media’s argument that the 1102 interview became “something other than” a preliminary hearing exhibit when it was necessary to file it in support of Mr. Robinson’s application to secure an out-of-state subpoena fails for the same reason. The filing of the interview prior to the preliminary hearing does not change what it is (the State’s proposed preliminary hearing exhibit) and importantly, it does not change the interests involved—protecting the integrity of the proceedings, preventing the circulation of evidence supporting elements of the offenses at this stage that will categorically not be admissible at trial, and overall jeopardizing Mr. Robinson’s

right to a fair trial. _See id_ . These interests, paramount in a capital criminal proceeding, overcome the public interest. This is particularly true where the public interest will be satisfied by the testimony and discussion of the exhibit in the preliminary hearing process. _See id_ . 

Mr. Robinson recognizes that the classification of the 1102 interview transcript may 

depend, in part, on this Court’s determination regarding the publication of the exhibit during the preliminary hearing pursuant to the Court’s _Ruling and Order on Defendant’s Objections to Electronic Media Requests_ . Dkt 664, at 11. As such, Mr. Robinson asks that, at a minimum, the 

Court reserve ruling on this issue until the publication determination is made when the State offers the interview as an exhibit during the preliminary hearing and the Court has had the opportunity to 

conduct the individualized assessment it has indicated it intends to do. 

# **CONCLUSION** 

Based upon the foregoing, Mr. Robinson maintains his request to classify portions of the 

application and attachments as private.

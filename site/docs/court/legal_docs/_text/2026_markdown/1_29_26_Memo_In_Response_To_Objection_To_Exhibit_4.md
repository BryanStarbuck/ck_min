         2. The evidence is admissible at this hearing

         Defendant couches his objection in terms of relevance and probative value versus a danger

of unfair prejudice as those concepts apply to the motion at hand. In this respect, the Court’s

inquiry is best considered under Utah Rule of Evidence 104. That is, the question to be decided

here is not one of guilt, but rather whether a conflict exists. This question requires an analysis of

CA’s value and qualification as a witness. The question brings the motion within the province of

rule 104.

            2.1.The evidence is relevant.

         Defense counsel frames his rule 401 and 403 objections on grounds of relevance to the

motion to disqualify, rather than on terms of relevance to the elements of the crimes charged. But

the two issues are tied together. If AC is a necessary witness, the argument that a conflict exists

has more merit. On the other hand, if AC is not a necessary witness, the conflict claim fails.

         The exhibits listed, including Ex. 4.1, are relevant because they demonstrate that AC is not

a necessary witness: AC did not see the most important events of that day while others did. For

example, AC did not see Mr. Kirk get shot, his wound, Mr. Kirk get carried away, the shooter, or

any other number of facts important to prove the elements of the charges. There were hundreds, if

not thousands, of other witnesses who did. There are many recordings of the shooting, of the

wounds, of Mr. Kirk’s emergency transport, and of people in the zone of danger, including the

man speaking with Mr. Kirk at the time of the shooting, his security detail, and people behind his

booth. But so far, proffers and affidavits have not satisfied Defendant. As a result, the State will

produce evidence, including exhibit 4.1., rather than proffer. The videos are relevant because they

circumstances of the case.

             2.2.The Evidence is highly probative with no danger of unfair prejudice.

         The videos are probative because they show the elements of aggravated murder which AC

did not witness: Mr. Kirks’s killing and many others in the zone of danger. While the videos have

a high probative value that Mr. Kirk was murdered and that the shooter placed others at great risk

of death, they have no danger of unfair prejudice, particularly at this stage of the proceeding. First,

there is no jury present so there is no danger of prejudicing them. Second, the State cannot see how

watching the video would prejudice the Court as it makes its determination of whether the UCAO

or Prosecutor A has a conflict. To the contrary, the videos show the key facts of the offense to

which CA was not a witness.

         3. Closing the hearing is a separate issue.

         Defendant’s real concern appears to be that publishing exhibit 4.1 may taint the jury pool,

rather than admissibility under the rules of evidence. This is a separate issue which should be

decided on its merits. The State makes no argument regarding closure of the hearing or publication

                                          CONCLUSION

        The State is content to use the still frames from the videos if Defendant stipulates that there

were thousands of people present when Charlie Kirk was shot, many of them witnessed Mr. Kirk’s

shooting, his wound and emergency removal, that many of them witnessed that others were near

Mr. Kirk when he was shot, and that there are many recordings of these facts. Otherwise, the State

is entitled to prove these points with evidence, including Exhibit 4.1.

        Exhibit 4.1 is relevant because it shows the existence of a recording of Mr. Kirk’s death,

independent of AC. While it is highly probative, in fact dispositive, of that fact, it carries no danger

of unfairly prejudicing a jury because a jury is not present. The facts demonstrated by the exhibits,

including exhibit 4.1, make clear that AC is not a necessary witness. This conclusion undermines

Defendant’s motion to disqualify Prosecutor A and the UCAO.

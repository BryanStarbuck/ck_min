# Defense Reply on Appearing in Civilian Clothing Without Restraints

**Filed by:** Defense — counsel for Tyler James Robinson (Kathryn N. Nester, Richard G. Novak, Michael N. Burt, Staci Visser) · **Date:** 2025-10-22 · **Type:** reply

## What it is
The defense's reply to the Utah County Sheriff's response opposing Robinson's motion to appear at all in-person proceedings in civilian clothing and without visible restraints.

## What it argues
The reply contends Robinson's concerns about prejudicial pretrial publicity are real, not merely "hypothesized," citing widely circulated footage and social-media coverage of his appearance. It argues the constitutional and common-law protections against being displayed in restraints and jail clothing extend beyond a jury trial to all public court appearances, given the risk of tainting the potential jury pool. It rejects the Sheriff's suggestion that the concern could be cured by requiring Robinson to waive his right to be present at proceedings in this capital case. The defense agrees with the Sheriff that video and photographic coverage of proceedings should be limited so that Robinson's physical appearance is no longer a subject of media interest.

## The other side
The Utah County Sheriff filed the response this reply answers; that response was classified as private. The Court issued its ruling on the underlying motion on October 27, 2025.

## Why it matters
Concerns whether a defendant may be shown to the public and prospective jurors in shackles and jail attire, implicating both the presumption of innocence and the right to a fair trial before an impartial jury.

# Objection to the States Motion to Amend or Clarify the Publicity Order

**Filed by:** Defense (Nester / Burt / Novak / Visser) · **Date:** 2025-11-20 · **Type:** Objection

## What it is
A defense objection to the State's motion to amend or clarify the court's Pre-Trial and Trial Publicity Order, urging the court to deny the State's motion.

## What it argues
The defense disputes the State's claim that the Publicity Order is overbroad or void for vagueness. It argues the order is narrowly directed at the ethical duties of counsel: its operative language tracks Utah Rules of Professional Conduct 3.6(a) and 3.8(e) almost verbatim, simply applying those duties equally to both prosecution and defense. The order, the defense says, does not subject non-attorneys to the court's contempt power; it only requires lawyers to take "reasonable care" to prevent those associated with the case from making prejudicial extrajudicial statements. Responding to the State's complaint about a lack of findings, the defense argues the record already shows the risk of prejudicial publicity tainting the jury pool — pointing to the court's own September and October 2025 findings about "arguably unprecedented" media interest and the danger of manipulated images. It also argues the State lacks standing to assert the First Amendment rights of lay witnesses or the media, and that the order is not a prior restraint because it limits nothing the media may publish.

## The other side
The State moved to amend or clarify the order (docket 167), calling it overbroad and "vague for voidness" and arguing it improperly restricts lay witnesses and the media.

## Why it matters
It shapes the gag/publicity rules governing what lawyers and witnesses can say publicly, and thus the flow of information about the case.

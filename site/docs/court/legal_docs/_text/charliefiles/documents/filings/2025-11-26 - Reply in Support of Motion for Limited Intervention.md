# Reply in Support of Motion for Limited Intervention

**Filed by:** Media intervenors (Parsons Behle — C. Michael Judd) · **Date:** 2025-11-26 · **Type:** reply

## What it is
The media's reply brief answering the State's and the defendant's responses to their intervention motion, narrowing the dispute to two remaining requests.

## What it argues
The media note that the court's November 13, 2025 order already requires future record-classification motions to be served on the press, and that both parties agreed to abide by it — resolving the threshold notice issue. That leaves two "modest requests": (1) an order formally recognizing the media as limited-purpose parties, which they say promotes efficiency, tracks Utah practice, and could matter on appeal; and (2) an order confirming the notice-and-service rule applies not just to record classification but to all attempts to limit access, including hearing closures and limits on electronic coverage. They cite Bullock, F.L. v. Court of Appeals, and Angilau that limited intervention is the preferred approach.

## The other side
The State does not oppose the relief and agrees the media may appear as limited-purpose parties. The defendant agrees to serve reclassification motions but opposes formal party status, preferring the media re-establish standing event-by-event — a position the media call "the wrong side of this argument."

## Why it matters
It sharpens the transparency fight to its real remaining question: whether the media get durable, formally recognized standing to police access, or must fight closure motions one at a time.

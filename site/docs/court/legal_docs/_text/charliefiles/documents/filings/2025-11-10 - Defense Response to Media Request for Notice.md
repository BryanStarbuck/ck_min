# Defense Response to Media Request for Notice

**Filed by:** Defense — counsel for Tyler James Robinson (Kathryn N. Nester, Richard G. Novak, Michael N. Burt, Staci Visser) · **Date:** 2025-11-10 · **Type:** response

## What it is
Robinson's response to the Request for Notice of Motions to Close Judicial Proceedings and Records filed by Deseret News and other media entities on October 30, 2025 (Doc 150).

## What it argues
The defense does not object to providing the notice required by law but argues the media's request is overly broad and burdensome. It reads Utah Rule of Judicial Administration 4-202.04 narrowly: the rule requires only that a party filing a motion to reclassify a record serve press representatives who have requested notice, and that notice can be satisfied by publicly filing motions (subject to redaction), which Utah's easily accessible online docket already makes available. It contends the rule does not require notice of motions concerning limitations on electronic media coverage. Citing Society of Professional Journalists v. Bullock and Kearns-Tribune v. Lewis, it argues Robinson should not be burdened with service beyond normal filing, and reiterates that the public right of access must give way to his fair-trial rights.

## The other side
Deseret News et al. sought broad advance notice and an opportunity to be heard before any closure. A separate group of media entities later sought fuller "intervenor" status (Doc 164).

## Why it matters
Defines the scope of the notice news organizations receive and how much service burden falls on the parties, an early skirmish in the media-access dispute.

# State Opposition to Motion to Show Cause for Contempt

**Filed by:** State of Utah — Utah County Attorney (Gray / Grunander / Ballard as applicable) · **Date:** 2026-04-30 · **Type:** opposition

## What it is
The State's opposition to the defense motion asking the court to hold the prosecution in contempt for public statements about the ATF ballistics result, and to compel related discovery.

## What it argues
The State says the defense, in its motion to continue the preliminary hearing, wrote that "the ATF was unable to identify the bullet recovered at autopsy to the rifle allegedly tied to Mr. Robinson" and suggested it might call the ATF analyst as exculpatory. The State calls this a misleading material omission. It quotes the ATF Summary Report verbatim: "The Exhibit 6A bullet jacket fragment could not be identified or excluded as having been fired from the Exhibit 1 rifle based on an agreement of all discernable class characteristics and neither sufficient agreement nor sufficient disagreement of individual characteristics. The result of the comparison was inconclusive." The State documents the resulting "media frenzy" — Daily Mail, USA Today, People, CBS, NY Post, PolitiFact, plus Candace Owens and Marjorie Taylor Greene — falsely reporting the bullet did not match. It argues Rule 3.6(c) expressly permits a lawyer to respond to unduly prejudicial publicity not initiated by that lawyer, that expressing confidence in its evidence is not an opinion on guilt, and that the defense already has the only responsive discovery.

## The other side
Answers the defense Motion for an Order to Show Cause for alleged contempt and to compel discovery, which accused the prosecution of violating the publicity order.

## Why it matters
Central to how the "inconclusive" ATF finding was framed publicly, and to the limits of prosecutorial speech under the court's publicity order.

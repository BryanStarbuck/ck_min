# Memo Opposing Motion to Classify January Filing

**Filed by:** Media intervenors (Parr Brown — Jeffrey J. Hunt, David C. Reymann, Carissa A. Uresk; and Parsons Behle — C. Michael Judd) · **Date:** 2026-01-15 · **Type:** memo

## What it is
A memorandum opposing the defendant's motion to classify as non-public a motion he filed January 9, 2026, and asking the court to vacate its January 12, 2026 order that had granted the classification without giving the media a chance to be heard.

## What it argues
The News Media raise both a procedural and a substantive objection. Procedurally, the court granted the classification within one business day without the seven-day opposition window promised in its December 29, 2025 standing order, and did so without the case-by-case findings Rule 4-202.04(6) requires even when parties stipulate — an abuse of discretion under Supernova Media. Substantively, they attack the defendant's premise that pretrial suppression and exclusion hearings are inherently non-public: citing Richmond Newspapers, Waller v. Georgia, and numerous cases, they argue the First Amendment right of access applies fully to suppression hearings, which "often are as important as the trial itself." Because the defendant signaled he will keep making this argument, the media brief it to correct the misimpression.

## The other side
The defendant moved to classify his January 9 motion, apparently reasoning that motions concerning evidence he will argue is inadmissible are necessarily non-public. The court initially granted the motion.

## Why it matters
It challenges both a specific sealing and the pattern of rapid, unbriefed closures — insisting the presumption of open suppression proceedings holds even in a high-profile case.

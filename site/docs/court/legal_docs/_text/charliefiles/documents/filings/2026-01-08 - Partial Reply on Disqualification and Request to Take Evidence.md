# Partial Reply on Disqualification and Request to Take Evidence

**Filed by:** Defense (Nester / Burt / Novak / Visser) · **Date:** 2026-01-08 · **Type:** Reply

## What it is
A short defense reply to the State's opposition to the motion to disqualify the Utah County Attorney's Office, giving notice that the defense will call witnesses and asking the court to take evidence at the January 16, 2026 hearing.

## What it argues
The defense argues that a motion to disqualify a prosecutor is a "fact-intensive inquiry" that requires an evidentiary hearing, citing decisions from Arizona, Nevada, and the Tenth Circuit. It notes it has already served subpoenas on four witnesses, all of whom accepted service. The reply attacks the State's opposition for resting on unsworn factual assertions — fourteen numbered paragraphs, several without any affidavit or declaration — about County Attorney Jeff Gray, the conflicted prosecutor, and that prosecutor's relative who was present at the shooting. The defense says it must be allowed to probe how the prosecutor came to know his relative's experience, what communications occurred within the prosecution team, and the timing and content of the real-time texts. It also argues the UCAO has a vested interest in the outcome and therefore should not be litigating its own disqualification — suggesting the Attorney General or another agency should have handled it. The defense asks to supplement its briefing after the hearing.

## The other side
The State's opposition (docket 279, filed Jan. 5, 2026) had itself acknowledged an evidentiary hearing might be necessary and placed the burden of proving the conflict's risk on the defense.

## Why it matters
It sets up a live, witness-driven hearing on prosecutorial impartiality rather than a paper ruling.

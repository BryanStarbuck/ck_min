# Order on Evidence Preservation

**Filed by:** Fourth District Court — Judge Tony F. Graf, Jr. (stipulated order) · **Date:** 2025-11-04 · **Type:** stipulated order

## What it is
A stipulated order that sets aside the court's earlier October 20, 2025 evidence-preservation order and replaces it with agreed language granting the defense's Motion to Preserve Evidence in part.

## What it argues / orders
Given the parties' stipulation on wording, the court sets aside the October 20 order and orders the Utah County Attorney's Office, its agents, and any law enforcement agencies involved to preserve all evidence in their custody or control. The office may still release or dispose of an item only after notifying defense counsel, who then has 14 days to object. Likewise, before any potentially destructive testing or examination — defined as testing likely to consume or change the character of an item — the office must notify defense counsel, who again has 14 days to object.

## The other side
The order resolves the State's Motion to Set Aside (Dkt. #116) and the defense's Motion to Preserve Evidence (Dkt. #75) by agreement, so there is no unresolved opposing position.

## Why it matters
It governs the chain of custody and testing of the physical evidence — the rifle, casings, DNA, and more — and the notice-before-destructive-testing rule directly bears on the defense's later ballistics and discovery disputes.

# State Response to Motion for Limited Intervention by Media

**Filed by:** State of Utah — Utah County Attorney (Gray / Grunander / Ballard as applicable) · **Date:** 2025-11-19 · **Type:** response

## What it is
The State's response to a group of media outlets seeking to intervene as limited-purpose parties and to receive advance notice of any motion to close, seal, or reclassify records or hearings.

## What it argues
The State largely agrees with the media. It concedes that under Utah Rule of Judicial Administration 4-202.04(4) and Society of Professional Journalists v. Bullock, the outlets may appear as limited-purpose parties on the discrete access issues they raise, and that the parties must serve them with copies of any motion to limit access to court documents. Going further than the defense, the State agrees the outlets are also entitled to notice of motions to close hearings, and that merely filing such a motion is not enough — media who have formally requested notice must be personally served. The State stresses, however, that it preserves its own right to argue for limiting access when a compelling interest justifies it, noting the First Amendment right of access is not absolute and that the defendant's fair-trial rights may supersede media access.

## The other side
Responds to the Media Outlets' Motion for Limited Intervention and Request for Notice, and corrects the defense's narrower reading (in the defense Response to Request for Notice) that filing alone satisfies the notice requirement.

## Why it matters
Sets the ground rules for press access, notice, and standing in a case with intense public interest, while preserving the State's ability to seek closure.

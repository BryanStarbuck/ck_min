# State Opposition to Motion to Disqualify the County Attorney

**Filed by:** State of Utah — Utah County Attorney (Gray / Grunander / Ballard as applicable) · **Date:** 2026-01-05 · **Type:** opposition

## What it is
The State's memorandum opposing the defense motion to disqualify a deputy county attorney and the entire Utah County Attorney's Office over an alleged personal conflict of interest.

## What it argues
The State argues the deputy attorney has no disqualifying conflict because his 18-year-old child, who attended the Turning Point USA event at UVU, is neither a material witness nor a victim. The child had no line of sight to the shooter, did not see Charlie Kirk shot, saw no gun, and learned of the death from others — so any testimony would be cumulative hearsay available from thousands of other attendees. Because there is no personal conflict, the State contends there is none imputed to the Office, and even a personal conflict would not require office-wide screening of the kind reserved for cases involving a defendant's confidential communications. The State further asserts that the same-day death-penalty notice was driven by the strength of the evidence and County Attorney Gray's campaign commitment, not the child's attendance.

## The other side
Answers the defense Motion to Disqualify the Utah County Attorney's Office, which claimed the deputy's family connection to the event created a concurrent conflict tainting the whole prosecution.

## Why it matters
Goes to whether the elected prosecutor's office may continue to lead a capital case, and whether personal ties to victims or attendees compromise prosecutorial impartiality.

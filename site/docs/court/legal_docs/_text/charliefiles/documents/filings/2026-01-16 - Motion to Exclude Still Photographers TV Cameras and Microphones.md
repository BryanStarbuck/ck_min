# Motion to Exclude Still Photographers TV Cameras and Microphones

**Filed by:** Defense (Nester / Burt / Novak / Visser) · **Date:** 2026-01-16 · **Type:** Motion

## What it is
A defense motion — filed together with a request for permission to file it over-length (262 pages) — to exclude still photographers, television cameras, and microphones from the courtroom.

## What it argues
The over-length request, which is the portion available here, explains that the underlying motion details numerous alleged violations, or threatened violations, of Robinson's constitutional rights: due process, a fair and impartial jury, right to counsel, freedom from unreasonable searches and seizures, and freedom from cruel and unusual punishment under the Fourth, Sixth, Eighth, and Fourteenth Amendments and parallel provisions of the Utah Constitution. The defense argues that in a capital case where the State is seeking death, the intense and "arguably unprecedented" media interest — including cameras capturing Robinson's image in court — creates a serious risk of tainting the jury pool. It contends the sheer breadth of the claims, requiring in-depth briefing on U.S. and Utah Supreme Court precedent and on Utah Rule of Judicial Administration 4-401.01, justifies the extraordinary length. The defense offers the State equal latitude in briefing length and time to respond.

## The other side
The filing here is the defense's own over-length request; it does not include a State response. The defense expressly invites the State to take the same page and time allowances to reply.

## Why it matters
It concerns courtroom access, cameras, and how pre-trial publicity could affect Robinson's right to an impartial jury.

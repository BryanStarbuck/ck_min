# Defense Response to Proposed Media Intervenors Motion

**Filed by:** Defense — counsel for Tyler James Robinson (Kathryn N. Nester, Richard G. Novak, Michael N. Burt, Staci Visser) · **Date:** 2025-11-19 · **Type:** response

## What it is
Tyler Robinson's response to the "Proposed Media Intervenors'" Motion for Limited Intervention and Request for Notice of Motions to Close, Seal, or Reclassify, filed November 5, 2025 (Dkt. 164).

## What it argues
The defense does not dispute that the media has a right, under Utah law, to notice and to request leave to be heard concerning the sealing of records or closure of hearings, and states counsel will comply with the Court's order to serve reclassification motions on media who have requested notice. But it opposes the broader relief sought: formal "intervenor" status. The defense argues that status exceeds what Utah law, the Utah Rules of Court, and the First Amendment require, and that the public right of access is not absolute and must yield where there is a "realistic likelihood of prejudice" to Robinson's right to a fair trial. It urges the Court to deny the request for intervenor status.

## The other side
The State filed its own response to the media intervenors on November 20, 2025. The media entities' underlying Motion for Limited Intervention sought formal party-like status and advance notice rights.

## Why it matters
Touches the ongoing tension between press access and the defendant's fair-trial rights, and defines how much formal standing news organizations will have in this high-profile capital case.

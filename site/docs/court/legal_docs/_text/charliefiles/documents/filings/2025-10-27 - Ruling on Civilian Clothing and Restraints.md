# Ruling on Civilian Clothing and Restraints

**Filed by:** Fourth District Court — Judge Tony F. Graf, Jr. · **Date:** 2025-10-27 · **Type:** ruling and order

## What it is
The court's written ruling on Robinson's motion to strike the Sheriff's response and his motion to appear in civilian clothing and without physical restraints at all pre-trial hearings, following a closed October 24, 2025 hearing.

## What it argues / orders
The court GRANTS the motion to strike the Sheriff's opposition as procedurally improper (the Sheriff is not a party), while still considering its security position as allowed under Rule 3-414. On the main motion, the court GRANTS the civilian-clothing request for all pre-trial proceedings, finding that unprecedented media coverage and the risk of manipulated jail-garb images could taint the Utah County jury pool. It DENIES the request to appear without restraints, citing the seriousness of the capital charges and courtroom safety, but directs the Sheriff to use the least-restrictive restraints and amends the decorum order to bar media from recording or photographing Robinson's restraints or while he enters, exits, or stands.

## The other side
The State, the Sheriff's Department, and the Court Security Director jointly opposed, arguing jail clothing aids identification during disruptions and that no precedent requires civilian dress pre-trial.

## Why it matters
It balances Robinson's presumption of innocence against courtroom security, and its restraint-photography limits feed directly into the ongoing publicity and media-access fight.

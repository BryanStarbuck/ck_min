# State Notice of Intent to Conduct Non-Destructive Testing (Bullet Jacket Fragment)

**Filed by:** State (Deputy County Attorney [[The Prosecution|Christopher D. Ballard]]) · **Date:** 2025-12-22 · **Type:** Notice (evidence-preservation)

> This is the document you'd asked about — *"do we have this doc?"* Now we do. It surfaced as **Exhibit 1** to the defense's [[2026-01-09 - Defense Motion to Prohibit Further Alteration of the Bullet Jacket Fragment|Jan. 9, 2026 motion to prohibit further alteration]], and is split out here as its own page.

## What it is
A four-page notice the State served on the defense under the court's Nov. 4, 2025 **[[2025-11-04 - Order on Evidence Preservation|evidence-preservation order]]** (which gives the defense **14 days to object** before potentially character-altering testing). It tells the defense the **FBI lab** intends to run forensic testing on **one bullet jacket fragment and at least one of four lead fragments** recovered from Charlie Kirk's autopsy.

## What it says
- **Bullet jacket fragment → Virtual Comparison Microscopy (VCM).** A non-contact 3-D optical scan to read any markings the rifle imparted; the scan files can be produced for **independent** defense VCM examination. To capture the surface that contacted the barrel, the examiner **may use pliers to fold back portions of the jacket**; this "could leave marks" and "may alter [its] shape," but "the examiner believes that the item should remain intact. The item is, nevertheless, **fragile**."
- **The shedding admission (the key line).** The ATF previously ran **Light Comparison Microscopy (LCM)** and "may have already folded back pieces of the bullet jacket fragment." After the ATF sent the fragment to the FBI, "an examiner at the FBI lab noted that **when the FBI lab received the bullet jacket fragment, it appeared that a small portion of the fragment was detached from the main portion**," and "it is possible that **additional pieces of the fragment may inadvertently become detached during the VCM analysis**."
- **Lead fragments → metallurgical testing.** A **small scraping** from at least one lead fragment, taken from an area that won't affect later ballistics work and that leaves enough material for an independent defense test.
- **Provenance of the ATF conclusions.** It cites the **Sept. 17, 2025 ATF Lab Report (2025-W-000401), disclosed Dec. 18, 2025 as Bates 011543**, which described the item as a **".30-caliber class deformed/damaged bullet jacket fragment."**

## Why it matters
This is the **primary source for the piece-count mechanism** at the heart of the seven-vs-five fragment discrepancy: the jacket fragment **demonstrably sheds pieces** as it is examined and moved between labs. It documents that the **detachment runs ATF → FBI** (a *rising* count as bits break off) — which is why any fixed "count" is unreliable, but also why detachment alone doesn't explain why *fewer* pieces (5) reached the ATF than the ME photographed (7). It also anchors the **".30-caliber class"** language to a dated, signed State filing rather than testimony alone. See [[The Inconclusive Bullet]] and [[Chain of Custody Problems]].

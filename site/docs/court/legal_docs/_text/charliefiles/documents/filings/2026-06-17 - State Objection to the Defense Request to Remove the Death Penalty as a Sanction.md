# State Objection to the Defense Request to Remove the Death Penalty as a Sanction

**Filed by:** State (Ryan McBride) · **Date:** 2026-06-17 · **Type:** Objection

## What it is
A six-page State objection responding to the defense's request — made **orally at the June 12, 2026 [[2026-06-12 - Contempt Evidentiary Hearing Transcript|contempt evidentiary hearing]]** — that the court **strike the death penalty** as the sanction if it found the prosecution in contempt of the publicity order. This is the written State response that sits between the June 12 oral request and [[2026-06-26 - Ruling on Contempt Order to Show Cause|Judge Graf's June 26 ruling]].

## What it argues
- **The request was premature.** The defense raised it **"for the first time at the evidentiary hearing, before any contempt finding"** — contradicting the defense's own reply, which had said the court should "hear from the parties as to what the appropriate remedies should be" *only* after a contempt finding.
- **No violation occurred.** The prosecution "diligently strove to strictly comply"; its media statements only **clarified** that the ATF toolmark analysis was **"inconclusive"** (not that it was incriminating), a **Rule 3.6(c)** correction of *"false reporting"* that the **defense's own public pleading** had generated (the [[2026-03-27 - Motion to Vacate or Continue the Preliminary Hearing|Motion to Vacate]] said the ATF was "unable to identify the bullet … to the rifle" and floated offering the ATF analyst "as exculpatory evidence").
- **Striking the death penalty is not an available remedy — two structural reasons.** (1) **Separation of powers:** reducing aggravated murder from a capital to a first-degree felony would invade the prosecutor's **exclusive executive charging power** (*Bordenkircher v. Hayes*; *Redondo-Lemos*). (2) **Wrong kind of contempt:** the defense alleged **civil** contempt, but an order reducing the charge is **"fixed and unconditional"** — the hallmark of a **criminal**-contempt remedy (*Von Hake v. Thomas*; *Hicks v. Feiock*). A civil-contempt remedy must be **conditional** (purgeable). And the sanction is **"grossly disproportionate … the drastic and never-before-imposed remedy of reducing the aggravated murder charge."**

## The other side
The defense's position (from the contempt motion and the June 12 hearing): two prosecutors made **extrajudicial statements to the media** about the disputed ballistics before any probable-cause finding, cutting against the presumption of innocence in a saturated venue (99% recognition / 64% already believe guilt, per the defense's Edelman survey); the most serious breach warranted the most serious sanction. See [[Prosecution Problems]].

## Why it matters
It explains **why the death penalty was never realistically strippable as a contempt sanction** — a charging decision is executive, and reducing the charge would be a criminal-contempt remedy the defense hadn't invoked. It also shows the **publicity fight is downstream of the inconclusive-bullet dispute**: the State's whole defense is that it was *correcting* a "case is gutted" narrative the defense's own "unable to identify the bullet" filing had seeded. Graf ultimately **agreed in part** — holding Ballard in civil contempt for a *different* (March "ample evidence") statement while **keeping the death penalty on the table**. See [[The Death Penalty in Utah]] and [[The Inconclusive Bullet]].

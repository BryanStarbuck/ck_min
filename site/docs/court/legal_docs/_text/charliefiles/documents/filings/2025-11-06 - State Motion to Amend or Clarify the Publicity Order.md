# State Motion to Amend or Clarify the Publicity Order

**Filed by:** State of Utah — Utah County Attorney (Gray / Grunander / Ballard as applicable) · **Date:** 2025-11-06 · **Type:** motion

## What it is
The State's motion asking the court to amend or clarify its September 2025 Pre-Trial and Trial Publicity Order (Dkt. #23), which requires lawyers to inform "witnesses" of the order's speech prohibitions.

## What it argues
The State argues the term "witnesses" in the order is vague. It asks the court to confirm that the prohibitions reach only witnesses who are members of the prosecution or defense teams — not the thousands of lay attendees or any person with relevant knowledge. Citing Rule 3.6 (which applies only to lawyers and their associates) and Rule 3.8 (which reaches only the prosecution team), the State says a prosecutor has no authority over independent lay witnesses. If the order is read to cover non-team witnesses, the State contends it is an unconstitutional prior restraint imposed without the required analysis, findings, or notice to the media, and is likely void for vagueness and overbroad. It cites Nebraska Press Ass'n v. Stuart and Spencer v. State to lay out the demanding standard for restraining trial-participant and public speech.

## The other side
Anticipates and pre-empts the defense position (later stated in the defense objection) that broad publicity alone justifies extending the order to all potential witnesses.

## Why it matters
Defines who in a heavily publicized capital case can be silenced by court order, and tests the First Amendment limits on gag orders over non-parties.

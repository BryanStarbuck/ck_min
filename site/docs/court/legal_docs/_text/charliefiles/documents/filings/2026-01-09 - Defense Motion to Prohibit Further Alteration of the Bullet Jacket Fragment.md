# Defense Motion to Prohibit Further Alteration or Testing of the Bullet Jacket Fragment

**Filed by:** Defense (Nester / Burt / Novak / Visser) · **Date:** 2026-01-09 (originally filed under seal; ballistics content unsealed April 2026) · **Type:** Motion

## What it is
A 19-page defense motion (with two exhibits) asking the court to **temporarily prohibit any further testing** of the single **bullet jacket fragment** recovered from Charlie Kirk's autopsy, and to let a **defense expert examine and photograph** it — or, in the alternative, to **observe or video-record** any further testing — *before* the fragment is altered. The two exhibits are the primary sources this vault had been missing:
- **Exhibit 1 — the State's [[2025-12-22 - State Notice of Intent to Conduct Non-Destructive Testing (Bullet Jacket Fragment)|Notice of Intent to Conduct Non-Destructive Testing]]** (Dec. 22, 2025, signed by Deputy County Attorney [[The Prosecution|Christopher Ballard]]).
- **Exhibit 2 — the ATF Firearms Lab Report** (Sept. 17, 2025; report **2025-W-000401**; disclosed Dec. 18, 2025 as Bates 011543) — the source of the ".30-caliber class" and "inconclusive" language.

## What it argues
The **one comparable fragment** in the entire case (Exhibit **6A**, the copper jacket) is fragile and **already shedding pieces**: per Exhibit 1, when the **FBI lab received the fragment from the ATF** it noted **"a small portion of the fragment was detached from the main portion,"** and warned **"additional pieces of the fragment may inadvertently become detached during the VCM analysis"**; the earlier ATF **LCM** exam **"may have already folded back pieces"** of the jacket. The FBI's planned **Virtual Comparison Microscopy** may require using **pliers to fold back** the jacket to scan the surface that contacted the barrel. The defense frames the fragment as **material, apparently-exculpatory evidence** — the ATF report says its individual markings **"include numerous makes and models"** — so any further alteration risks **spoliation**. It invokes *California v. Trombetta*, *Arizona v. Youngblood*, and *Illinois v. Fisher*, plus Utah R. Crim. P. 16 and the court's Nov. 4, 2025 **[[2025-11-04 - Order on Evidence Preservation|evidence-preservation order]]** (14-day objection window), and asks for defense-expert access before the item is "further altered or destroyed."

## The other side
The State's position (in Exhibit 1) is that the testing is **non-destructive** — VCM is 3-D optical scanning where the instrument **does not contact** the item, the scan files can be produced for **independent** defense examination, and the metallurgical scraping leaves enough material for the defense to run the same test. The State says the examiner **"believes the item should remain intact,"** while conceding it **"is, nevertheless, fragile"** and that the process **"may alter [its] character."**

## Why it matters
This is the **documentary spine** of the whole "inconclusive bullet" fight. It (1) puts the **Dec. 22 testing notice on our record** — the document that spells out the fragment's **piece-shedding mechanism** behind the seven-vs-five count discrepancy; (2) supplies the **primary-source ATF conclusions** (".30-caliber class deformed/damaged bullet jacket fragment"; "could not be identified or excluded … inconclusive"; lead fragments 6B–6E "not suitable for microscopic comparison"); and (3) shows the defense is fighting to **preserve and independently examine** the one fragment that matters — a preservation posture, not a claim the fragment "clears" Robinson. See [[The Inconclusive Bullet]].

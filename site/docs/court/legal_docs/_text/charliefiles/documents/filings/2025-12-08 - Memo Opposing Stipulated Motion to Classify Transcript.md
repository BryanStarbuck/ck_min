# Memo Opposing Stipulated Motion to Classify Transcript

**Filed by:** Media intervenors (Parr Brown — Hunt, Reymann, Uresk; and Parsons Behle — C. Michael Judd) · **Date:** 2025-12-08 · **Type:** memo

## What it is
A memorandum opposing the defendant's stipulated motion (joined by the State) to classify as "private" the audio and written transcript of the October 24, 2025 hearing on the defendant's clothing and restraints. A closure hearing on the issue was set for December 11, 2025.

## What it argues
The media contend the stipulated motion fails the rigorous constitutional standard needed to overcome the public's presumptive right of access to court records. They frame this as the first of a likely series of closure requests grounded only in generalized claims that pretrial publicity will prevent a fair trial — "blanket claims" that arise in nearly every high-profile case and that courts routinely reject. They argue the proponent of closure bears a heavy burden, that less-restrictive alternatives like targeted redaction exist, and that the parties' "two-word, unexplained justifications" for redaction fall far short. The brief lays out the governing standards to guide the court going forward.

## The other side
The defendant sought the classification and the State stipulated to it; after the court declined to sign a blanket sealing order and set a hearing, both parties retreated to seeking only limited redactions of certain transcript portions.

## Why it matters
This is the first substantive courtroom clash over sealing in the case — a test of whether generalized fair-trial worries can override the open-courts presumption.

# Defense Motion in Limine to Preclude Rule 1102 Hearsay

**Filed by:** Defense — Kathryn Nester, Richard Novak, Michael Burt · **Date:** 2026-05-06 · **Type:** motion in limine

## What it is
The defense's **Motion in Limine (Docket 525)** for an order precluding the State from using **Article I, § 12 of the Utah Constitution and Rule 1102** to admit **hearsay to establish probable cause** at the preliminary hearing — the underlying motion the court later **denied** ([[2026-06-23 - Ruling Denying Motion to Preclude Rule 1102 Hearsay|June 23]]).

## What it argues / orders
That relying on hearsay to establish probable cause — rather than requiring live, cross-examined witnesses — offends the Utah Constitution and Confrontation principles, so the State should be barred from doing so at the prelim.

## The other side
The State opposed; the [[2026-06-12 - Attorney General Notice Not to Respond to Constitutional Challenge|Attorney General]] was noticed of the constitutional challenge and declined to respond; Graf **denied** the motion, and the defense took it up on **interlocutory appeal** (below).

## Why it matters
This is the **motion at the center of the hearsay fight** — its denial let the [[The Discord and Text Messages|Twiggs statement and digital exhibits]] come in at the prelim, and its constitutional challenge is now before the **Utah appellate courts**. See [[Confessions and Admissions]], [[What Is Genuinely Open]].

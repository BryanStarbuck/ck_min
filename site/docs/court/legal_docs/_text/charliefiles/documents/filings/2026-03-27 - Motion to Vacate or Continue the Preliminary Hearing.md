# Motion to Vacate or Continue the Preliminary Hearing

**Filed by:** Defense (Nester / Burt / Novak / Visser) · **Date:** 2026-03-27 · **Type:** Motion

## What it is
A 69-page defense motion asking the court to vacate or continue the preliminary hearing tentatively set for the week of May 18, 2026, until the State completes forensic discovery and the defense has a reasonable time to review it.

## What it argues
The defense contends the preliminary hearing cannot proceed fairly because the underlying forensic case files have not been produced. The State intends to introduce DNA and ballistics reports as "reliable hearsay" under Utah Rule of Evidence 1102(b)(4) without live witnesses. The defense argues Utah's Constitution (art. I, § 12) permits such hearsay only after "appropriate discovery" is provided. It stresses the ATF summary report stating the ATF was **unable to identify** the recovered bullet to the rifle tied to Robinson — evidence the defense may offer as exculpatory. It also challenges the FBI's and ATF's use of the STRmix probabilistic-genotyping software on tiny, multi-contributor DNA mixtures, citing federal cases excluding STRmix. The defense reports roughly 20,000 files, 61,500 pages, 700+ hours of video, and 12 terabytes of new data still under review, and attaches expert declarations (Dr. Elizabeth Johnson, Dr. Dan Krane) describing months of needed work. It applies the five-factor *Layton City v. Longcrier* continuance test, arguing every factor favors delay.

## The other side
The State set the May date and, per the motion, intends to proceed via report-based hearsay rather than live examiners. The court previously called the date "not set in stone." (No State opposition is contained in this filing.)

## Why it matters
This is the "inconclusive bullet" motion. It frames the ballistics and DNA that anchor the case, and how the defense characterizes the ATF finding.

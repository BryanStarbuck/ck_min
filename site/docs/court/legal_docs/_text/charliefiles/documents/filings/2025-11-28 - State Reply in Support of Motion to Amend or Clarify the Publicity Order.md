# State Reply in Support of Motion to Amend or Clarify the Publicity Order

**Filed by:** State of Utah — Utah County Attorney (Gray / Grunander / Ballard as applicable) · **Date:** 2025-11-28 · **Type:** reply

## What it is
The State's reply answering the defense objection and again urging the court to clarify or amend the Pre-Trial and Trial Publicity Order (Dkt. #23).

## What it argues
The State repeats that the order's use of "witnesses" is vague and ambiguous. Applying the series-qualifier canon, it argues the modifier "or other members of the prosecution or defense teams" limits the whole list — so "witnesses" should mean only team members. It says there is no statute, rule, or case extending a prosecutor's ethical duties (Rules 3.6 and 3.8) to independent lay witnesses, and the defense cited none. If the court instead intends the order to reach all potential witnesses or even all trial witnesses, the State argues the order is an unsupported prior restraint that must be vacated pending briefing, a hearing, and detailed findings. It stresses, citing Nebraska Press, CBS v. U.S. District Court, and the Watergate prosecutions, that even pervasive pretrial publicity does not automatically produce an unfair trial and cannot alone justify restraining speech.

## The other side
Answers the defense Objection to the State's Motion to Amend or Clarify, which argued the extensive media interest supported applying the order broadly to witnesses beyond the litigation teams.

## Why it matters
Sharpens the constitutional dispute over gag-order scope in a high-publicity capital case and whose speech the court may restrict.

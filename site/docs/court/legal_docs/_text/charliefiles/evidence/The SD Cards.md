# The SD Cards

This is the rare claim where the **underlying act is real** — which is exactly why it needs a careful, sourced treatment rather than a flat "false."

## What the video shows
In the immediate aftermath, a man is filmed **using Kirk's chair to reach a camera positioned above where Kirk had been speaking** and **removing what appears to be an SD/memory card**, then handing it off. The clip went viral and drove the "someone tampered with / hid the footage" theory (Candace Owens devoted an episode to it: *"Who Moved The Camera Right Above Charlie's Head?"*).

## Who it actually was
It was **Kirk's own TPUSA A/V team**, not police or an outsider:
- The multi-camera event rig was run by TPUSA A/V contractor **Terryl Farnsworth / Visual Impulse**, whose sworn **Voluntary Statement is Plaintiff's Exhibit 8.1** in this case. His statement describes a **three-angle setup**: one camera on Kirk, one on the questioner, and a wide crowd angle.

![[farnsworth-sd-cards-statement.jpg]]
*Plaintiff's Exhibit 8.1 — Terryl Farnsworth's Voluntary Statement (in the case record).*

- TPUSA producer **Andrew Kolvet** and COO **Tyler Boyer** said their **head of tech** grabbed the cards so the footage "wouldn't be stolen," and that **all footage was turned over to the FBI** (who now hold the cards).

## The honest verdict
The **act happened**; it was **Kirk's own media team**; the footage went to the **FBI**. Nothing in the record shows the cards were pulled to hide anything. But because the explanation comes from **Kirk-affiliated spokesmen** — not an independent investigator, and not (so far) sworn testimony on the *card handling* — the **chain of custody is a fair thing to test on cross-examination**, which is exactly what critics are demanding. That is why it sits on [[What Is Genuinely Open]].

Note this is a **separate** body of footage from the **UVU campus surveillance**, which law enforcement has held all along — see [[Kirk Team Seized the Security Footage|UVU footage claim check]] and [[The Surveillance and the Gait]].

> **Day 2 (light) — the Visual Impulse footage is in the State's hands.** Day 2 did **not** cover the card-pulling act or the "cover-up" question. It did establish, on Hull's cross, that **Visual Impulse provided four videos / multiple camera angles** to investigators, of which only **one** (a Bates-numbered clip via the Utah County Attorney's Office) was shown at the prelim — "there's the original footage," and other angles "not shown here" [02:01:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=7276s)–[02:02:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=7337s). That is consistent with the honest verdict here — the event footage went to law enforcement — while leaving the **handling/chain-of-custody of the cards themselves** exactly as open as before: still not tested under oath.

# The Unattributed Holster

One of the small, genuinely unresolved threads — and Day 2 **complicated its central premise.** In Day-1 testimony, an officer described **"an empty pistol holder sitting just on top of the grass"** in the courtyard ([hearing 01:40:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6fq2kCMVMXI&t=6056s)). On cross, he confirmed it was never accounted for: *"What happened to that holster? I have no idea. Did you ever take custody of it? Nope… fingerprinted? I have no idea"* ([02:53:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6fq2kCMVMXI&t=10393s)). It was seen again near the Fugal Building ([04:07:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6fq2kCMVMXI&t=14867s)).

## Day 2: the "empty holster" is now contested
On **Day-2** cross ([[2026-07-07 - Preliminary Hearing Day 2|Day 2 Legal Review]]), the defense confronted the State's case agent, [[David Hull|Agent Hull]], with **Exhibit Hull-13** — a report by Agent **Davis** relaying an interview of an **off-duty trooper, Noah Gonzales**, who was on scene. Per that report, *there was a **gun found in the holster*** in the amphitheater. Hull, who had not surfaced this on direct, confirmed it once shown the document: **"Trooper Gonzales included in his report that there was a gun, yes"** [01:46:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=6397s)–[01:48:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=6538s).

So the Day-1 "empty holster" framing is **now contradicted by the record**: on the hearsay-through-two-reports account, the holster was **not** empty. That deepens rather than resolves the thread — because everything downstream is still open:
- **What kind of gun was it?** A **handgun.** Asked directly on Day 2 what type of firearm the other on-scene weapon was, Agent Hull answered **"That was a handgun"** [01:46:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=6374s). That matters for the ballistics: a **handgun cannot fire a .30-06**, so the courtyard sidearm is the **wrong caliber class** to be the source of the [[The Inconclusive Bullet|bullet-jacket fragment recovered at autopsy]] — whose class characteristics *agreed* with the .30-06 **rifle**. It cannot be the murder weapon; on the current record it is a loose end, not a second shooter's gun.
- **Whose gun was it?** Nothing ties it to Robinson or to the [[The Rifle|rifle]] (the murder weapon was a **rifle recovered along the rooftop-to-north escape route**, not a courtyard sidearm).
- **Was it ever collected or printed?** The Day-1 cross established the *holster* was never taken into custody or fingerprinted; the record still doesn't show the **gun** being logged, matched, or attributed to anyone. See [[Chain of Custody Problems]] and [[Gaps in the Investigation]].
- **It is double-hearsay** — Gonzales's belief, relayed in Davis's report, read to Hull — so its weight is a trial question. Davis is expected to testify later.

## What it is — and isn't
- **It is** a loose end — and a **sharper** one after Day 2: an armed second weapon in the courtyard, its owner unknown, apparently never collected or matched. It stays on the honest [[What Is Genuinely Open|open-questions]] list.
- **It still isn't** evidence of a [[The Shot Came from the Courtyard|second shooter]]. This was an **open-carry event** with police and private security present; a lawfully armed attendee or off-duty/responding officer with a holstered sidearm is **unremarkable** at a chaotic mass-casualty scene, and the fatal shot came from the **roof**, not the courtyard.

The prosaic reading: a lawfully armed attendee or responder left a holstered sidearm behind in the panic. The fair-question reading: a second gun sat in that courtyard and — on the current record — **nobody collected, printed, or attributed it**, so we can't say whose it was. Both can be true, and neither implies a courtyard gunman.

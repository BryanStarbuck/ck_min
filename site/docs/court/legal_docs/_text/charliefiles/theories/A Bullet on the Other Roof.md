# A Bullet on the Other Roof

> **The claim:** A bullet was found on a *different* rooftop — not the shooter's position — which would suggest a second shot or a second firing position.

**Verdict: OPEN** — the "bullet on another roof" now has an innocent explanation on the record, but it opened a *new* loose thread: the round's whereabouts are unknown. A genuine chain-of-custody gap, not evidence of a second shot.

## Day 2 gave it a name — and lost track of it
On **Day-2** cross ([[2026-07-07 - Preliminary Hearing Day 2|Day 2 Legal Review]]), the defense put the same question to the State's case agent, [[David Hull|Agent Hull]] — and this time it got an answer that cut both ways. Hull recalled that **"an unfired bullet had been found on another building"** on September 10, then, pressed, revised it mid-answer to **"an ejected cartridge from an officer who had cycled his rifle"** — a round chambered and ejected when an officer "made his rifle ready" [01:43:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=6203s)–[01:43:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=6232s).

**Was it a rifle round? What caliber?** On Hull's account it would be a **rifle cartridge** (an officer's chambered round) — and, being *unfired*, a live **cartridge**, not a fired projectile. But the **caliber was never established.** Asked directly *"Do you remember what type of bullet that was?"* and *"Was it a 30-o[6]?"* — i.e., the same round as the [[The Rifle|murder rifle]] — Hull answered **"I don't"** to both, and could not say **when** it had been accounted for [01:44:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=6251s)–[01:44:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=6265s). So it was **never confirmed** to be a .30-06, and **no comparison** tied it to any weapon. The scene was **not separately preserved** as its own item.

- **The innocent read (State's).** With **hundreds — "if not a thousand"** — officers converging on the campus that day [02:15:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=8111s), a stray round from a responding officer making his rifle ready is unremarkable, and does not point to a second firing position.
- **The loose end (defense's).** The label **moved** ("unfired bullet" → "ejected cartridge") on a shifting recollection, the "building" is unremembered, its **caliber was never established** (not confirmed as a .30-06), and it was **not separately preserved or compared** — an unaccounted-for piece of ballistic evidence. That is a real [[Chain of Custody Problems|chain-of-custody]] and [[Gaps in the Investigation|investigation]] gap even if it is *not* a second shooter.

So Day 2 both **deflates the "second shot" inference** (an officer's cycled round has an innocent origin) *and* **adds a genuine loose end** (whatever it was, its caliber, timing, and handling were never pinned down on the record).

## Day 3 (July 8) — the caliber is answered: a .223 live round, no line of sight
On **Day 3**, case agent [[Brian Davis|Agent Brian Davis]] finally gave the round a **caliber and a location**, relaying the findings of Agent **Ben Schneider** (see [[2026-07-08 - Preliminary Hearing Day 3|Day 3 Legal Review]]):
- It was a **.223-caliber round**, found **on top of the Computer Science Building** (east, slightly south of the Losee) [01:00:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szsmK2Ul9XY&t=3624s)–[01:00:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szsmK2Ul9XY&t=3646s).
- It was a **live (unfired) round** — "still had the projectile… the primer had not been [struck]… not dented up and fired" [01:00:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szsmK2Ul9XY&t=3635s)–[01:00:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szsmK2Ul9XY&t=3659s) — a **dropped cartridge, not a fired bullet**.
- There was **no line of sight** from that roof "to where the tent was or mister Kirk was" [01:00:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szsmK2Ul9XY&t=3614s)–[01:00:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szsmK2Ul9XY&t=3622s).

**Why this all but closes the "second shot" reading.** The murder weapon is a **[[The Rifle|.30-06 Mauser]]**; **.223 (5.56)** is the standard **AR-15 / police patrol-rifle** caliber — so the round on the Computer Science Building is the **wrong caliber to have come from the killing rifle**, and a **live, unfired** round dropped where there was **no line of sight to Kirk** is exactly what a **responding officer** leaves, matching the Day-2 "officer who cycled his rifle" account. The caliber question Day 2 left open ("never confirmed as a .30-06") is now **answered: it is a .223, not a .30-06.**

> 🎥 **Forbes Breaking News:** the investigator cross-examined about the **.223 bullet recovered on a UVU rooftop** after the shooting.

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rPIrvNeheCc)

> **Key exchange** (Prosecution ↔ [[Brian Davis|Agent Davis]], relaying Agent Ben Schneider): **Q —** *"Did you learn from agent Schneider what caliber that bullet was?"* **Davis —** *"Yes… It was a two-twenty-three caliber."* **Q —** *"…was [it] a live round?"* **Davis —** *"Yes… still intact… the primer had not been [struck]… still had the bullet projectile on the end."* **Q —** *"…would there have been a line of sight [from that roof to the tent]?"* **Davis —** *"No… there was not a line of sight from there to where the tent was or mister Kirk was."*

**Caveat (still a loose end).** Davis got this **from Agent Ben Schneider**, not personal observation [01:01:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szsmK2Ul9XY&t=3666s) — so it is **conduit hearsay** at this stage, and the round's own chain of custody has not been examined live. But the second-shooter reading of this item is now, on the record, **effectively dead** (wrong caliber, unfired, no sightline).

## What was said on Day 1
This comes from the **Day-1 cross-examination** of Officer [[Chris Bagley|Bagley]]. After establishing there was **no casing on the shooter's roof** ([[No Casing or Rifle on the Roof]]), the defense asked:

> **DEFENSE** ([04:11:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6fq2kCMVMXI&t=15113s)): *"…Are you aware of there being a bullet found on another roof?"*
> **BAGLEY** ([04:12:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6fq2kCMVMXI&t=15123s)): *"I'm not."*
> **DEFENSE** ([04:12:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6fq2kCMVMXI&t=15125s)): *"Were you aware of another bullet being found on the scene, not on the roof of the Losee Building?"* — again, no.

## How to read it
- **On Day 1 it was a defense question, not evidence.** A leading question on cross **asserts** a fact to the witness; it does not prove one. Bagley — the *first* officer on the shooter's roof — simply had **no knowledge** of any such bullet.
- **Day 2 supplied the innocent explanation** — an officer's cycled round, not a second shot — so this **no longer meaningfully feeds** [[A Second Shooter from a Lower Angle]] or [[The Shot Came from the Courtyard]]. What survives is narrower: a piece of evidence that was found, reclassified on a shifting recollection, and **never identified by caliber or compared to any weapon**.
- **What's left to resolve** is the provenance of that round — which officer, its caliber, when accounted for, and whether any record documents it. On the **current record** it is a documentation/loose-end question, not a second-shooter one.

## Bottom line
On Day 1 this rested on a single unanswered cross-examination question. **Day 2** gave the "bullet on another roof" an innocent, prosaic origin (a responding officer's cycled round) but left its **caliber unestablished**. **Day 3 answered the caliber:** it was a **.223 live (unfired) round** on the **Computer Science Building** roof, with **no line of sight** to Kirk (via Agent Schneider) [01:00:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szsmK2Ul9XY&t=3614s)–[01:00:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szsmK2Ul9XY&t=3646s) — i.e., the **wrong caliber for the .30-06 murder rifle** and consistent with a **responding officer's dropped round**. It stays on the honest [[What Is Genuinely Open|open list]] only as a **conduit-hearsay / chain-of-custody loose end** (Davis relayed it from Schneider); the **"second shot" reading is effectively closed.** See [[Defense Strategies and Degrees of Liability|the reasonable-doubt posture]].

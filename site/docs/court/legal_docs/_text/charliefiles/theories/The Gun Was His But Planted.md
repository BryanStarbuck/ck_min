# The Gun Was His But Planted

> **The claim:** Robinson wasn't the shooter and wasn't on that roof. The rifle really *is* his family's gun — so of course his DNA and prints are on it — but someone else used it, and the weapon and casings were **planted** along the escape route to frame him.

**Verdict: UNSUPPORTED** — but this is the most *coherent* innocence construction available, so it's worth taking seriously and showing exactly where it breaks.

## Why this is the sharp version
Most "framed" theories stumble on the DNA: Robinson's DNA is on the **trigger, casing, and towel** ([[The Casing Match and DNA]]). This theory is clever because it **concedes the gun is his** and thereby makes the DNA *innocent* — you'd expect a person's DNA on their own family rifle. It then leans on the genuine soft spots in the identification:

> **Day-2 update — the record now cuts both ways on this exact move.** The FBI DNA report (Exhibit 31) puts Robinson as the **major contributor** on the towel wrapping the rifle and on the rooftop screwdriver — likelihood ratios of **1.7 octillion** (towel) and **30 quintillion** (screwdriver) [05:11:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=18662s). Examiner **Amanda Bakker** gave the defense two footholds for the "innocent DNA" premise: she **cannot say *when* or *how* the DNA was deposited** — "DNA on an item doesn't mean the person touched it" [05:31:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=19864s) — and she confirmed the **towel was found in a bush at the university, not in Robinson's home** [07:38:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=23906s). But the same day cut against "planted by another user": the rifle was **recovered in the wooded area Hull saw Robinson enter** [02:20:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=8441s) — the very location his messages describe as his "drop point" — and roommate **Lance Twiggs came back a likelihood ratio of 1, "uninformative"** [07:20:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=26404s), so the DNA does not point to an obvious alternate user. See [[2026-07-07 - Preliminary Hearing Day 2|Day 2 Legal Review]], [[The Casing Match and DNA]], and [[The DNA Didn't Match]].

- The rooftop figure was **never identified** by face, clothing, height, or shoes on Day 1 ([[They Have the Wrong Person]]).
- **No casing and no rifle on the roof** ([[No Casing or Rifle on the Roof]]) — the weapon was recovered *elsewhere*, where it could in theory have been placed.
- The [[The Inconclusive Bullet|bullet fragment was inconclusive]], and the defense floated [[A Bullet on the Other Roof|a bullet found on another roof]].
- The rifle is described in the messages as **"grandpa's rifle"** — a family gun, which is *why* his DNA on it is unremarkable.

## Where it breaks
The DNA-is-innocent move only works if you can also erase Robinson's **own words**, and you can't:
- His [[The Discord and Text Messages|messages]] put the rifle **in his hands, at the scene, that day**: *"I gotta grab my rifle still,"* *"I had planned to grab my rifle from my drop point,"* and *"if I am able to grab my rifle unseen, I will have left no evidence."* He is describing **retrieving the weapon he used** — the same escape-route location where it was found.
- The [[The Keyboard Note|keyboard-note confession]] is first-person and pre-written: *"I left the house this morning on a mission … I had the opportunity to take out Charlie Kirk, and I took it."*
- The [[The Surveillance and the Gait|gait surveillance]] tracks a person to the prone rooftop position and the [[The Surrender|voluntary surrender]] follows.
- **His own home tied him to this ammunition.** A search of his residence turned up a **shell casing with the *same* engravings** as the rifle's rounds and **several targets with bullet holes** (per [[2025-09-16 - The Information and Charges|the Information]]) — his own physical evidence linking him to the specific hand-engraved cartridges and to shooting practice, hard to square with a stranger "planting" a weapon along a route. See [[The Engraved Casings]].

So "his gun, planted, he wasn't there" doesn't stay a **forensics** theory — it collapses into the **full-fabrication** scenario, where the messages, the letter, the surveillance, and the surrender are *also* faked or coerced. That is the scope problem laid out in [[A Hypothetical Cover-Up]].

## Bottom line
As a way to **neutralize the DNA in isolation**, it's the defense's best forensic argument, and a real reasonable-doubt seed if a jury ever discounted the confession and messages. As a factual account, it is **unsupported**: his own contemporaneous words place the gun in his hands at the scene. This is the heart of **Layer 1** in [[Defense Strategies and Degrees of Liability]].

# The Limp Proves an Impossible Timeline

> **The claim:** The suspect's limp appears and disappears in the footage — proving the timeline (or the identification) is impossible.

**Verdict: UNSUPPORTED.**

## What fuels it
The suspect's **distinctive gait** shows up on the approach but is **absent during the rooftop run** — read as a contradiction.

## What the evidence shows
- A visible gait on a **walk** and its absence during an **adrenaline-driven run** are **consistent**, not contradictory.
- If anything, the distinctive gait **aids identification**, because it recurs across **multiple separate cameras** on the approach. See [[The Surveillance and the Gait]].
- **Day 2 put this on the record.** Case agent **David Hull** walked the court through the surveillance, identifying the figure by the **same shoes** and a **limp/gait** recurring across cameras (Day 2 [00:51:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=3085s), [00:51:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=3109s)), and acknowledged the limp is **not visible at every moment** (e.g., the roof egress, Day 2 [02:24:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=8642s)). That variability is the point, not a contradiction.
- The current X framing — *"he could bend his leg freely, so the 'rifle down his pants' story is absurd"* — aims at a **concealment** inference, but the **identification does not depend on it**: the limp is used as a recurring **identifying feature** (with the shoes, vehicle, DNA, and surrender), not as the proof he carried the rifle. How the rifle was moved is a separate question the physical evidence speaks to — the **towel-wrapped rifle recovered on the escape route** ([[The Rifle]]).

> **Reasonable-doubt read (for the defense):** the limp point itself is weak, but the **broader identification doubt** — no facial or feature ID of the roof figure — is real and stands on its own. See [[Insufficiency of the Evidence]] and [[They Have the Wrong Person]].

## Bottom line
People move differently sprinting under stress than strolling. A limp that's visible walking and muted running doesn't break the timeline — it's one more distinctive feature the surveillance uses to track the same person.

# The Body Camera Conveniently Failed

> **The claim:** An officer's body camera "conveniently" failed at the key moment — a deliberate gap.

**Verdict: UNSUPPORTED.**

## What fuels it
Day-1 testimony that an officer's **body-cam battery died** after he reached the roof ([hearing 04:12:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6fq2kCMVMXI&t=15141s)), with footage ending **before** the crime-scene tape went up.

## What the evidence shows
- The camera **died after he had already reached the roof** — not at the instant of the shot.
- **Battery failure on long overtime shifts is routine**, and is verifiable against the device logs.
- There are **hundreds of hours of other video** of the scene and the investigation. See [[The Surveillance and the Gait]].

It is a real gap in **one** officer's footage — the defense fairly flags it (see [[2026-07-06 - Preliminary Hearing Day 1|Day 1 Legal Review]]) — but "a gap exists" is a long way from "the gap was engineered."

> **Reasonable-doubt read (for the defense):** even if not deliberate, it is a **real gap** in the one objective record of the scene, at a bad moment — and the State should have to account for it. "Unsupported" speaks to *intent*, not to whether the gap matters. See [[Insufficiency of the Evidence]].

## Bottom line
A dead body-cam battery on a long shift is ordinary, it happened *after* the roof was reached, and it's one small window inside an enormous video record. Nothing suggests it was deliberate.

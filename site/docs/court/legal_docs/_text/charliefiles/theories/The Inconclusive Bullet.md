# The Inconclusive Bullet

> **The claim:** The bullet was "inconclusive" as coming from the rifle — so ballistics don't actually tie the weapon to Robinson.

**Verdict: PARTLY TRUE** — but "inconclusive" is not "excluded," and the case file settles how it's being misused.

## What fuels it
A **real** lab result, amplified by headlines. The defense's [[defense_motion-to-vacate-or-continue-prelim.pdf|Motion to Vacate/Continue]] characterized the ATF finding as being "**unable to identify** the bullet," which media then ran as "the bullet didn't match."

## What the evidence shows
The **State's own court filing** quotes the ATF conclusion **in full** — and the full sentence is the key, because the defense motion quoted only its first half:
> "The Exhibit 6A bullet jacket fragment **could not be identified or excluded** as having been fired from the Exhibit 1 rifle **based on an agreement of all discernable class characteristics** and **neither sufficient agreement nor sufficient disagreement of individual characteristics.** The result of the comparison was inconclusive."

![[stateopp-atf-quote-crop.png|700]]
*The State's [[state_opposition-to-motion-to-show-cause.pdf|opposition]] (p. 4) quoting the ATF Summary Report's **complete** conclusion — including the clause the "didn't match" headlines dropped: the class characteristics **agreed**; only the *individual* match was inconclusive.*

Key points from the [[state_opposition-to-motion-to-show-cause.pdf|State's opposition]]:
- The defense motion **omitted the words "or exclude,"** which turned *"unable to identify the bullet"* into something misleading.
- The **four lead fragments** were unsuitable for comparison (it is the copper **jacket** fragment that carried the class-level read).
- **Inconclusive bullet results are common** (~20% of cases) with **fragmenting rifle rounds**.

## What "inconclusive" actually means here — the caliber was *not* in doubt
This is the part the headlines drop. A firearms comparison looks at two layers, and the ATF split on them:
- **Class characteristics — *agreed.*** Class characteristics are the general features a barrel stamps on every bullet: **caliber, rifling type, land-and-groove count, twist direction.** They are what separate a **.30-caliber rifle** bullet from, say, a **9 mm pistol** bullet. The ATF found **"an agreement of all discernible class characteristics"** — so the jacket fragment **is consistent with the .30-06 rifle** at this level. The **round type / caliber was therefore *not* inconclusive.**
- **Individual characteristics — *insufficient.*** Individual characteristics are the microscopic striations unique to **one specific barrel.** Here there was **"neither sufficient agreement nor sufficient disagreement"** — not enough detail to say *this barrel and no other*, nor to exclude it. **That** is what made the comparison "inconclusive."

So the honest translation is: **the fragment matches a .30-06 rifle by class; it just can't be individualized to *the* recovered rifle.** "Inconclusive" is a statement about the *individual* match, **not** about the caliber.

## Does this rule out the second gun (the courtyard holster)?
On the current record, **yes — the holster weapon is effectively excluded as the source of this fragment.** The second firearm found on scene was a **handgun**, recovered at **ground level in the amphitheater/courtyard** (see [[The Unattributed Holster]] — a pistol holster on the grass, recharacterized on Day 2 as holding a gun per Trooper Gonzales's report). A **handgun cannot fire a .30-06** — that is a rifle cartridge with rifle-class characteristics. Because the fragment's **class characteristics agree with the .30-06 rifle**, a pistol is the **wrong caliber class** and is excluded at the class level — before even reaching the geometry (a ground-level courtyard sidearm vs. a downward shot from the [[Line of Sight and Shot Geometry|rooftop 140+ yards away]]). **Caveat:** because the *individual* match is inconclusive, ballistics have not individualized the fragment to any one weapon — but the class read points to a **.30-06 rifle**, not the courtyard pistol.

And crucially — the ballistic link doesn't rest on that fragment:
- The **fired casing was positively matched** to [[The Rifle|the rifle]].
- **Robinson's DNA** was on the **trigger, casing, and towel**. See [[The Casing Match and DNA]].

## What the fragment is — and what's still pending
The disputed item is the **bullet *jacket* fragment (Exhibit 6A), recovered during Charlie Kirk's autopsy** — a damaged piece of the round's copper jacket, which is exactly the kind of evidence that defeats a clean comparison. Two wrinkles from the [[2026-03-27 - Motion to Vacate or Continue the Preliminary Hearing|discovery motions]]:
- The defense also filed a **Motion to Prohibit Further Alteration or Testing** of that fragment — a fight over *preserving* it, since comparison testing can consume or alter the sample.
- As of that motion, **comparative bullet analysis and bullet-lead analysis were still in progress at the FBI**, and the ATF casefile/protocols and the FBI's **STRmix** DNA analysis were still being produced in discovery. The ballistics picture was **incomplete, not closed** — part of why the defense sought to *continue* the preliminary hearing.

## Day 2 — still not reached
Day 2 confirmed the picture is **incomplete, not resolved**. Chain-of-custody witness Sgt. Faumuina testified the towel-wrapped **Mauser 98** rifle was routed to the **ATF lab** [05:04:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=18268s), but the hearing reached the **DNA** report only — **no ballistics/tool-mark comparison of the rifle to the recovered round was put in the Day-2 record**, and it ended mid-hearing with the State's forensic case not rested. So the ATF comparison remains outstanding as of Day 2. See [[2026-07-07 - Preliminary Hearing Day 2|Day 2 Legal Review]].

## Day 4 (July 9) — the State's own ATF examiner confirms "inconclusive" on the record
Day 4 turned the ATF finding into live sworn testimony — from the **State's own examiner**, [[Samantha Karner]], who ran the comparison "at the request of the state of Utah" and authored the report (Exhibit 32) [06:24:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=23099s). Called by the **defense**, she confirmed the exact two-layer split described above:
- **The murder bullet-jacket fragment (Item 6A) is INCONCLUSIVE** — it "could not be identified or excluded" as fired from the rifle [07:11:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=25864s), and "may or may not have originated from the same source" [07:14:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=26042s). The single most-quoted line in the case is **now confirmed on the record by the person who did the test.**
- **But the cartridge case IS a positive ID** — Karner "identifies the cartridge case as being fired in the firearm" [06:31:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=23487s). The inconclusive result attaches to the *fragment*, not the casing — precisely the distinction the talking point erases. See [[The Casing Match and DNA]].

The defense also used her to attack the *discipline*, not just this one result: she conceded she cannot claim uniqueness, individualization, infallibility, a zero error rate, or a "reasonable degree of scientific certainty" [06:30:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=23441s)–[06:33:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=23612s); that AFTE identification "is subjective in nature" [07:04:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=25469s); that **NAS (2009)** faulted the field for a "lack of a precisely defined process" [07:25:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=26736s) and **PCAST (2016)** found firearms analysis "falls short … of foundational validity" [07:57:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=28643s); and that her own **2023 bullet proficiency test carried a 20.4% error rate** [08:03:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=28990s). On State cross, McBride rehabilitated with an **ANAB-accredited**, proficiency-tested lab whose conclusions were **verified by a second analyst** [07:51:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=28313s) and a method "accepted by courts across the country … routinely admitted" [08:04:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=29088s).

**Effect on the verdict — unchanged, but sharper.** The "inconclusive" half of the claim is now **confirmed by the State's own examiner**, which is why the fragment stays on the [[What Is Genuinely Open|open list]]. But the claim's second half — "so ballistics don't tie the weapon to Robinson" — is **contradicted by the same witness**, who positively matched the **casing** to the rifle. **PARTLY TRUE** holds against the current record. See [[2026-07-09 - Preliminary Hearing Day 4|Day 4 Legal Review]] and [[2026-07-09 - Preliminary Hearing Day 4 Transcript|Day 4 Transcript]].

## What was actually recovered — and the "seven fragments" discrepancy
The Day-4 record finally itemizes the projectile evidence, and it surfaced a **chain-of-custody discrepancy** the earlier filings did not: **the medical examiner appears to have documented up to seven fragments, but only five reached the ATF.**

**What the ATF received and tested (Exhibit 6 = 6A–6E).** Reading the firearms report, Sgt. [[Jennifer Faumuina]] testified that **"Exhibit six consists of one thirty-caliber class deformed damaged bullet jacket fragment and four lead fragments"** [05:31:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=19861s) — the **"bullet jacket fragment as well as some other fragments that were recovered from Mr. Kirk at the time of autopsy"** [05:30:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=19844s) — and that **"Exhibit 6B through 6E lead fragments are not suitable for microscopic comparison"** [05:31:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=19915s). So of the five pieces, **only the single copper jacket fragment (6A) was comparable; the four lead fragments (6B–6E) were not.**

**Who did what.**
- **The medical examiner (Utah OME)** recovered and documented the fragments from Kirk's body at autopsy — but **never testified live**; the autopsy reached the hearing only secondhand (through Agent Hull on Day 2 and Faumuina/Karner on Day 4), and the **autopsy report itself was not published**.
- **[[Samantha Karner]] (ATF)** is the examiner who actually **received the sealed evidence, documented and prepared 6A, test-fired the rifle, and ran the comparison**: she "was asked to test fire the firearm and compare the test fires to the cartridge case and the bullet jacket fragment" [06:26:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=23163s), confirming she got **"one bullet jacket fragment, and four lead fragments"** [06:26:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=23160s).

**The discrepancy, on the record.** On direct, **Burt** walked Karner straight into it:
> **Burt:** "Were you informed at some point that there was a **discrepancy between what you were examining and what had been documented by the medical examiner** in terms of the number of bullet fragments?" — **Karner:** "I was, yes." [06:37:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=23832s)
> **Burt:** "was it your understanding that there were **originally seven bullet fragments but you ended up with four**?" — **Karner:** "I was **alerted that there was potentially seven fragments from the medical examiner in a photo just a few months ago.**" [06:37:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=23853s)

Three things make this a real, not cosmetic, gap:
- **The numbers don't reconcile.** The ATF worked from **five** pieces (jacket 6A + four lead), the medical examiner's photo suggests **seven** — leaving **roughly two fragments unaccounted for** between the autopsy table and the ATF bench. (Burt's "ended up with four" frames it against the four *lead* fragments; either way the count fell.)
- **No one was tasked to explain it.** Asked whether she was "asked to see if maybe the **ATF had lost certain portions of the evidence**," Karner answered **"No, I was not"** [06:37:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=23849s) — and she learned of the seven only **"a few months ago," from a photo**, long after her exam.
- **Her own chain was clean from receipt.** Karner said the evidence reached her **sealed** in "a small individual Manila envelope" [06:37:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=23879s), and page 14 of her report shows the **"as-received condition of Exhibit 6 in its entirety… 6A all the way through 6E"** [06:38:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=23925s). So the shortfall — if real — happened **before** the ATF sealed and logged it (i.e., in the handoff from the autopsy), not in Karner's custody.

**Contradictions worth flagging.** Day 2's Agent Hull, who was **not present at the autopsy**, loosely called the recovered items **"some fragments of a bullet casing"** and, asked **"how many fragments,"** answered **"I don't"** know (Day 2 [02:04:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=7488s)) — imprecise on both the nature (these are **bullet/jacket** fragments, not casing) and the count. The precise itemization only arrived on Day 4, and with it the seven-vs-five mismatch. This is a **chain-of-custody / completeness** problem for the defense, not proof the fragment "didn't match" — but it compounds the reliability attack: the one comparable fragment is **inconclusive**, the smaller fragments are **useless for comparison**, and the **piece-count between the autopsy and the lab doesn't cleanly reconcile** (7 vs. 5 — see the documented shedding below). See [[Chain of Custody Problems]], [[Gaps in the Investigation]], [[The Medical Examiner and the Wound]].

**Was it explained — on cross or redirect? No.** The discrepancy surfaced on the **defense's own direct** of Karner (Burt); the **State never addressed it.** During Karner's testimony McBride mostly **objected on relevance/scope** grounds (reliability is a *trial* question, not a probable-cause one — [07:07:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=25625s)), and **no witness reconciled the count.** Karner was **not asked whether the ATF lost anything** and had learned of the seven only **"in a photo just a few months ago."** So on the current record the **7-vs-5 gap stands unexplained.**

**The fragment *does* shed pieces during handling — and it's documented.** The jacket fragment really is fragile enough that its piece-count drifts, and the reason is spelled out in the **State's [[2025-12-22 - State Notice of Intent to Conduct Non-Destructive Testing (Bullet Jacket Fragment)|Notice of Intent to Conduct Non-Destructive Testing, dated December 22, 2025]]** (signed by Deputy County Attorney [[The Prosecution|Christopher Ballard]], given under the [[2025-11-04 - Order on Evidence Preservation|Nov. 4 evidence-preservation order]]'s 14-day-to-object rule — surfaced as Exhibit 1 to the defense's [[2026-01-09 - Defense Motion to Prohibit Further Alteration of the Bullet Jacket Fragment|Jan. 9, 2026 preservation motion]]). Per that notice: after the **ATF** examined the jacket fragment and **sent it on to the FBI lab**, *"an examiner at the FBI lab noted that when the FBI lab received the bullet jacket fragment, it appeared that **a small portion of the fragment was detached from the main portion**,"* and that *"it is possible that **additional pieces of the fragment may inadvertently become detached during the [VCM] analysis**"* — and the prior **LCM examination "may have already folded back pieces of the bullet jacket fragment"** to expose its surface. So the mushroomed jacket (its **"petals... crumpled in on themselves,"** per Karner [06:39:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=23975s)) **demonstrably crumbles and sheds pieces as it is examined and transported** — which makes any fixed "count" inherently unreliable and supports the innocent read that the ME's *seven* and the ATF's *five* are the **same crumbly material counted at different handling stages**, not two vanished fragments.

**Two honest caveats.** (1) The *documented* shedding runs **ATF → FBI** (the count *rising* as bits detach), whereas the ME-to-ATF step ran the *other* way (seven **down** to five) — so detachment explains why the number won't sit still, but not, by itself, why **fewer** pieces reached the ATF than the ME had photographed (the likeliest remaining explanation: the ME counted tiny specks/petals the ATF later consolidated, or that were never submitted). (2) **None of this reached the preliminary-hearing record** — Karner was never asked to reconcile it — even though the **Dec. 22 notice is now in our document set** (it was filed as Exhibit 1 to the [[2026-01-09 - Defense Motion to Prohibit Further Alteration of the Bullet Jacket Fragment|Jan. 9, 2026 defense preservation motion]], alongside **Exhibit 2, the ATF's own Sept. 17, 2025 Lab Report 2025-W-000401**). So the *mechanism* is real and squarely sourced to a signed State filing; the exact **7↔5** accounting was simply never laid out under oath. See [[Chain of Custody Problems]].

**The defense's preservation fight (Jan. 9, 2026).** Worth flagging what the defense *did* with all this: rather than argue the fragment "clears" Robinson, it moved to **stop the FBI from altering the one comparable fragment** until a defense expert could examine and photograph it — or at minimum observe/video the testing. Its [[2026-01-09 - Defense Motion to Prohibit Further Alteration of the Bullet Jacket Fragment|motion]] casts Exhibit 6A as **material, apparently-exculpatory evidence** (the ATF report says its individual markings "**include numerous makes and models**") whose further alteration would be **spoliation** under *California v. Trombetta*, *Arizona v. Youngblood*, and *Illinois v. Fisher*. That posture is telling: the defense treats the fragment as **worth preserving and independently testing**, not as a silver bullet — consistent with **inconclusive ≠ exculpatory.**

## The diameter reads under .308 — because it's a deformed jacket *fragment*, not the caliber
A separate talking point — amplified from a *Daily Mail* item and repeated by commentators like Candace Owens — is that the recovered bullet **"measured smaller than .308,"** implying it wasn't the .30-06. The testimony shows what that number actually is. On direct, Karner gave the **diameter *range* of the Exhibit 6A bullet *jacket fragment*** — **".286 to .301 inches"** — read from **page 13** of her report [06:43:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=24232s). Three points:
- **It's the *fragment's* dimension, not the gun's caliber.** This is a **deformed, mushroomed copper *jacket* fragment** (petals crumpled), measured at multiple points — hence a *range*, not a single value. A fired, expanded, fragmented jacket routinely reads **below the nominal .308″** of an intact .30-06 bullet; it is **not** a bore-diameter measurement.
- **The ATF still placed it in the .30-caliber class.** The report calls 6A a **"thirty-caliber class"** fragment with **"six-right conventional rifling"** [06:41:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=24093s), and found its **class characteristics *agreed*** with the Exhibit 1 **.30-06** rifle — *"an agreement of all discernible class characteristics"* [05:31:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=19882s). And **caliber *is* a class characteristic** [06:43:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=24199s) — it was **not** in doubt.
- **So "smaller than .308" is a category error.** It conflates a **deformed fragment's measured diameter range** with the **caliber of the weapon**. The reading is consistent with a **.30-caliber** bullet (which is exactly why the fragment could not be *excluded*). A genuinely different caliber — e.g., a **.223 (.224″)** pistol/rifle round — would have been **excluded at the class level**; this fragment was not. What remains inconclusive is only the *individual* match to **this** barrel, not the caliber. See the class-vs-individual split above and [[The Casing Match and DNA]].

## Bottom line
The **individual** match is genuinely inconclusive — that part is true, and it stays on the [[What Is Genuinely Open|open list]]. But two things the talking point hides: **inconclusive ≠ excluded**, and the "inconclusive" applies only to individualizing the fragment to *the* rifle — **the caliber/class was not in doubt**; the fragment's class characteristics *agreed* with the .30-06 rifle (which also excludes the courtyard [[The Unattributed Holster|handgun]]). And the casing match plus DNA carry the ballistic tie regardless. The talking point works only by dropping two words — and then a whole clause.

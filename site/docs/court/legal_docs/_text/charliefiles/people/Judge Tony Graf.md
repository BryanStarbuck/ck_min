# Judge Tony Graf

![[judge-graf.png|260]]

**Hon. Tony F. Graf Jr.** presides over *State of Utah v. Tyler James Robinson* in the Utah **4th District Court**. (The initial no-bail order, Sept. 12, 2025, was entered by Judge **Shawn R. Howell**.)

- **Appointed** to the 4th District by **Gov. Spencer Cox in 2025** ([Utah Bar announcement](https://www.utahbar.org/gov-spencer-cox-appoints-tony-f-graf-to-the-fourth-district-court/)).

## His rulings cut both ways
The record does not read like a judge tilting for either side:
- He **excluded** a State-annotated compilation video (12.1) because the person who altered it could not authenticate it — the defense's biggest evidentiary win of Day 1. See [[The Surveillance Video Was Doctored]].
- He **held a prosecutor in contempt** (June 26, 2026) over a gag-order violation ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/06/26/us/charlie-kirk-tyler-robinson-contempt-ruling)).
- He has **also ruled against the defense** — admitting reliable hearsay at the prelim, and denying live testimony from Lance Twiggs.

## Day 2: holding the prelim standard, both ways
Day 2 reinforced the mixed record:
- On the **motive exhibit (5.1)**, he **provisionally admitted** the TPUSA statement as relevant to the political-expression enhancement, **expressly reserved** whether it connects to Robinson's state of mind, and **declined to publish** it to the courtroom or media — and separately **sustained** the objection to the prosecutor reading the unadmitted exhibit aloud [03:42:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=13354s), [03:10:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=11419s). A careful, split ruling.
- On the **DNA fight**, he repeatedly held to the **probable-cause** standard — the magistrate **does not weigh** the evidence or choose between competing inferences (citing *State v. Ramirez*, *Drosbeck*, *Merrill*) — and kept **DNA reliability a trial (Rule 702) issue**, not a prelim one [06:03:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=21839s), [07:13:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=25986s). That cuts against the defense's reliability attack at this stage while preserving it for trial. See [[2026-07-07 - Preliminary Hearing Day 2|Day 2 Legal Review]].

That mixed record is why [[Judge Graf Is Bought Off|Judge Graf claim check]] is rated **FALSE**: there is no evidence of bias or corruption, and his rulings demonstrably go both ways.

## Is he taking too long to decide bind-over?
At the close of the 5-day preliminary hearing (July 10, 2026) Graf did **not** rule on probable cause from the bench; he set a **briefing schedule** and **oral argument for Sept 1, 2026**, then a ruling. That delay is **within the normal range for a Utah capital case** — the near-identical **Ryan Bate** case (2 Tremonton officers, capital, different judge/district — bind-over **decision set for Dec. 18, 2026**, ~6 months after its June prelim) and the **Michael Jayne** case (Santaquin officer, capital, *same Utah County office* — bind-over **argued June 18, 2026**) both took bind-over **under advisement with post-hearing briefing**. Robinson's Sept 1 argument (~8 weeks out) is on the faster end. Utah R. Crim. P. **7B** requires the magistrate to make a probable-cause finding and enter written findings, but sets **no deadline** and does not require a bench ruling; the *Robinson* briefing was **defense-requested and mutually agreed**. The unusual features are the **length of the hearing and the publicity**, not the timeline. Full comparison and sources: [[The Death Penalty in Utah]].

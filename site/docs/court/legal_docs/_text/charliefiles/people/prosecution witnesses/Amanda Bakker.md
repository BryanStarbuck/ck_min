# Amanda Bakker

![[amanda-bakker.png|260]]

**Amanda Bakker** — **FBI forensic DNA examiner**, FBI Laboratory (Quantico) — authored the DNA report in this case. She is the **FBI's own analyst**, but on **Day 2** the **defense called her out of order** (Michael Burt on direct) to test the reliability and limits of the DNA comparison (video `y6ofpz6ReuE`).

> **Procedural note.** This happened **during the State's case-in-chief — the State has not rested, and the defense case has not begun.** The State chose to put its DNA in through a **conduit** (Sgt. Faumuina reading Exhibit 31) rather than call Bakker live; **by stipulation** ([05:13:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=18822s)) the defense was allowed to interrupt and take her **out of order** ([05:14:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=18856s)) — a scheduling accommodation for an FBI examiner in from Quantico. Order of exam: **defense direct (Burt) → State cross (McBride) → defense redirect (Burt).** Faumuina's own cross remains reserved.

## Why she matters
Bakker signed **State's Exhibit 31**, the report tying [[Tyler Robinson]]'s and [[Lance Twiggs]]'s DNA to the [[The Rifle|towel]] and [[The Rifle|screwdriver]]. By calling the government's examiner herself, the defense turned the State's headline forensic result into a running seminar on what STR DNA can and cannot establish. See [[The Casing Match and DNA]] and [[The DNA Didn't Match]].

## What she testified to (what the defense drew out)
- **STR measures length, not sequence.** Standard STR typing (the FBI's GlobalFiler kit, 21 loci plus 3 sex-determining) counts how many times a short unit repeats — the "boxcar" count — and **compares only the *length* of the repeats, not the DNA sequence within them**. Two people can differ in sequence yet read the same by length ([05:36:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=20182s), [05:37:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=20268s)).
- **A partial profile on the screwdriver.** Item 7 (towel) gave a **full profile**, but **item 8 (screwdriver) was only a partial profile** — data was missing at some loci, so no comparison could be made there ([05:40:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=20456s)).
- **Both were low-level mixtures.** Both samples were **mixtures with a minor contributor under 20%** (item 7 as low as 5%; item 8 about 11%) — exactly the category the **PCAST report** flagged as needing more validation ([05:52:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=21179s), [05:53:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=21207s)).
- **The statistics come from STRmix — and are not reproducible run-to-run.** The reported likelihood ratios came from **STRmix** probabilistic software; Bakker agreed that running the same sample **never gives the same answer twice** (she said within about a factor of 10) ([05:52:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=21144s), [06:10:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=22236s), [06:11:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=22261s)).
- **The Twiggs result was "uninformative."** For [[Lance Twiggs]], STRmix returned a **likelihood ratio of one — "uninformative,"** not even limited support for inclusion. Bakker nonetheless **treated Twiggs as an assumed/elimination contributor** based on her "own knowledge and training" and a visual read, then conditioned Robinson's statistic on that assumption ([07:21:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=26467s), [07:22:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=26538s), [07:22:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=26574s)). See [[The DNA Didn't Match]] and [[Lance Twiggs Was More Involved|Twiggs claim check]].
- **The towel wasn't found where Twiggs's DNA would be expected.** She agreed she was told the **towel was found outside, in a bush at the university** — not in Robinson's home — yet still used Twiggs as an elimination sample on the investigators' assumption it came from the home ([06:38:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=23906s), [06:38:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=23920s)). See [[The Unattributed Holster]] and [[Chain of Custody Problems]].
- **Experience is not validity.** Confronted with PCAST's line that professional practice "cannot substitute for actual evidence of scientific validity," and that STRmix was validated largely by "the people selling the product," Bakker **disagreed** ([08:09:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=29381s), [08:11:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=29476s)).

## What the State scored back
On McBride's cross, Bakker held the line for the reliability of her lab's work — the balance the record actually reflects:
- Her lab is **accredited**, her methods are **validated internally and developmentally**, and she is **proficiency-tested** ([07:53:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=28429s), [07:55:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=28512s)).
- In response to PCAST, **31 laboratories** pooled internal validation data on STRmix ([08:11:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=29486s)).
- Crucially, she agreed her lab's **validation is itself a "ground-truth" test** — you compare an analyst's conclusion against the known true answer of the study sample ([08:14:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=29642s)).

## Bottom line (on the current record)
For the State, the DNA still places Robinson's profile on the screwdriver and towel at astronomical likelihood ratios from an accredited, validated lab. For the defense, those numbers rest on **STR length-only typing**, a **partial profile**, **sub-20% mixtures**, **non-reproducible software output**, and an **"uninformative" Twiggs result the examiner overrode by judgment**. This is a probable-cause hearing; the reliability fight Bakker previewed is a trial question, and the defense may yet call its own DNA expert. See [[Gaps in the Investigation]] and [[They Have the Wrong Person]].

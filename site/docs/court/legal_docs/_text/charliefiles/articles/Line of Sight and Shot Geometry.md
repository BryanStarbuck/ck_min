# Line of Sight and Shot Geometry

**The concern:** could the shot really be made from that roof — and wasn't the tent in the way?

This is one of the most common good-faith doubts, and it deserves a real answer rather than a dismissal. Two things resolve it: an aerial photo showing the sightline exists, and a to-scale drawing putting numbers on it.

## The aerial
![[line-of-sight-croy-x-post.jpg]]
*Aerial via Reuters (shared publicly by @Mitchell_croy). The white event tent (top) is plainly visible from the rooftop prone position (bottom) across an open lawn.* — **Image note:** this is a licensed news photo, used here for commentary; see [[About This Project]].

## The to-scale geometry
![[kirk-shot-geometry-to-scale.svg]]

Putting numbers on the same sightline:

- **~430 ft** (≈143 yards) horizontal distance · **~45.6 ft** vertical drop.
- A shallow **~6.1° downward angle** (estimates range 4–8°).
- The bullet path clears the near canopy eave by only **~16 inches** to reach a seated neck — tight, but clean.

## What this establishes
For a **scoped .30-06**, 143 yards at ~6° down is an ordinary shot — not a trick shot, not impossible. The geometry also explains *why* the doubt exists: people picture the tent as a **barrier**, when the math shows a person seated at the canopy's front edge was **exposed, with clearance**.

In the preliminary hearing, an officer noted that the Sorenson Building obstructs *part* of the roof's view — but **not** the prone edge position that actually had the line ([hearing 01:21:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6fq2kCMVMXI&t=4272s)).

## What Day 2 added to the geometry
Day 2 (July 7) put the firing position and timing on the record — see [[2026-07-07 - Preliminary Hearing Day 2|Day 2 Legal Review]]:
- **The shot was at 12:23:28, from the Losee roof's *southwest* corner.** The State's case agent, Hull, traced the figure over the railing and **prone at the SW corner**, then the shot [00:54:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=3244s)–[00:55:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=3327s) — the specific edge position the geometry above assumes.
- **The tent banner is opaque — no line of sight either way.** On redirect, Hull confirmed the event-tent banner **blocks the view in both directions**, so no one behind it could see the roof and the shooter could not see through it [02:18:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=8306s)–[02:19:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=8346s). This cuts both ways: it explains why bystanders did not spot the prone figure, and it means the sightline ran to the **exposed seated position at the canopy's front edge**, not through the banner.
- **~15–30 seconds prone before the shot.** Hull *estimated* the figure was prone for roughly **15–30 seconds** before firing [01:56:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=7014s) — a short establish-and-fire window the defense will time against the physical evidence.

**Confirmed:** the ~430 ft distance and the rooftop prone position. **Estimated:** the ~50 ft muzzle height and exact eave clearance (the to-scale drawing is a reconstruction, not a survey).

## Why it matters for the theories
The geometry is the quiet answer to a whole family of claims — that the trajectory "doesn't match the roof," that there must have been [[A Second Shooter from a Lower Angle|a lower-angle second shooter]], or that [[The Shot Came from the Courtyard|the shot came from the courtyard]]. A near-horizontal **neck** wound is exactly what a shallow ~6° downward shot from 143 yards produces.

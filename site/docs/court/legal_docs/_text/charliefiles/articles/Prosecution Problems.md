# Prosecution Problems

> **What this is.** A running catalogue of the problems on the **State's** side of the ledger — a claimed conflict of interest, prosecutors' out-of-court statements to the media, a death-penalty decision that ran ahead of the disclosed evidence, discovery that arrived late, and evidence the State could not authenticate. Read **in the light most favorable to the defense**, as the presumption of innocence requires: Robinson is **charged, not convicted**, and the **State carries the entire burden.**
>
> **Caveat both ways.** Most of these are **trial-admissibility, publicity, or penalty-phase** issues, not reasons a bind-over fails — and where the defense asked for the strongest remedies (disqualifying the office, striking the death penalty), the court so far **declined**. But taken together they bear on the **fairness and reliability** of the State's case, which is what the presumption of innocence exists to protect. This is commentary, **not legal advice**; the case is **ongoing.**

## 1. A prosecutor's own child was at the scene — the disqualification fight
The defense moved to **disqualify the entire Utah County Attorney's Office** on the ground that one of the case's **lead prosecutors — Chief Deputy County Attorney Chad Grunander**, referred to in the closed portions of the record as "Prosecutor A" — has an **adult child who was in the crowd** at the Turning Point USA event at UVU when Kirk was shot: roughly **85 feet away**, who heard the shot and fled with everyone else. The argument: a prosecutor whose family member is effectively a **victim/witness** of the very crime carries a **personal stake** that can taint the whole office's charging and trial decisions, and the office **cannot fairly litigate a conflict allegation against itself.**

The court held a **two-day evidentiary hearing** — [[2026-01-16 - Disqualification Hearing, Part One Transcript|January 16]] and [[2026-02-03 - Disqualification Hearing, Part Two Transcript|February 3, 2026]]:
- The defense (Novak) asked the court to **strike the office's self-authored opposition** and **refer the motion to the Utah Attorney General** (citing *State v. Jimenez* and Utah Code § 17-68-304), arguing the office cannot be judge in its own cause. The court **declined to refer on the briefing alone**, finding the defense had not yet made the threshold showing, and let the evidentiary hearing proceed (Jan 16 [00:56:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NU3AfEth-og&t=3369s)).
- County Attorney **Jeffrey Gray** was called as a witness and examined about the conflict; the defense told him, *"You've made yourself a witness to evidence that you've used to try to advocate to the court"* (Jan 16 [02:29:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NU3AfEth-og&t=8947s)) — the office defending its own conflict from the witness stand.
- On Feb 3 the court heard testimony from **Gray**, **Grunander** himself, and an **SBI agent**, plus closing arguments, then **reserved its ruling** (Feb 3 [03:52:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZukNzYKgcYM&t=13924s)).
- On **February 24, 2026**, the court **denied** the motion — the office stayed on the case.

**The defense's strongest reading:** an office whose own leadership has a personal, familial connection to the crime scene should have been **walled off or handed to the AG**; leaving it to prosecute — and to seek death — is a structural conflict a later "no harm" finding cannot fully cure.
**The Other Side:** after a full hearing the judge found **no disqualifying conflict**; the child was a **bystander, not a charged-case witness**, and Utah sets a **high bar** for disqualifying an elected prosecutor's entire office. The ruling is a **defense loss**, preserved for appeal.

> _Editorial note: the hearings referred to the prosecutor as "Prosecutor A" to protect his child's identity, and part of the identifying testimony was taken in a **closed session**. Because Grunander is a senior deputy in an elected office who was examined **by name in open court**, this page names him — but, like the court, it does **not** name or further identify the child._

## 2. Trying the case in the press — the contempt / order-to-show-cause
Separately, the defense brought an **[[2026-04-10 - Defense Motion for Order to Show Cause for Contempt and to Compel Discovery|order to show cause]]** (backed by a sworn [[2026-04-16 - Declaration of Counsel Supporting the Contempt Motion|declaration of counsel]]) accusing two prosecutors — **Christopher Ballard** and **Jeffrey Gray** — of making **extrajudicial statements to the media** about the disputed **bullet-fragment / ballistics** evidence, in violation of the court's pretrial-publicity order. At the [[2026-06-12 - Contempt Evidentiary Hearing Transcript|June 12, 2026 evidentiary hearing]], the defense walked Ballard through his own quotes — a **TMZ** article (June 12 [01:09:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ws3jBiVuh5U&t=4198s)) and remarks picked up by **USA Today, PolitiFact, and _Fox &amp; Friends_** — and pressed that a prosecutor telling the public *"the evidence that we detail…"* (June 12 [02:13:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ws3jBiVuh5U&t=7992s)) before any probable-cause finding **cuts against the presumption of innocence** (June 12 [02:57:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ws3jBiVuh5U&t=10652s)). As a **sanction**, the defense asked the court to **strike the death penalty** — a request it made **orally at the June 12 hearing**, which the State attacked as **premature** in a written [[2026-06-17 - State Objection to the Defense Request to Remove the Death Penalty as a Sanction|June 17 Objection]]. That objection is worth noting because it explains *why the death penalty was never realistically strippable this way*: reducing aggravated murder from a capital to a first-degree felony would (1) invade the prosecutor's **exclusive executive charging power** (*Bordenkircher v. Hayes*), and (2) be a **criminal**-contempt remedy ("fixed and unconditional," per *Von Hake v. Thomas*) when the defense had alleged only **civil** contempt — whose remedies must be conditional/purgeable.

The court had **granted** the show-cause motion on **June 1** (setting the June 12 hearing) while **denying** a defense motion to compel discovery ([[2026-06-01 - Ruling Granting Order to Show Cause and Denying Compel Discovery|ruling]]); on **June 12** it also **denied** the defense's motion to **stay the preliminary hearing pending its Utah Supreme Court interlocutory appeal** ([[2026-06-12 - Ruling Denying Stay Pending Interlocutory Appeal|ruling]]), heard the **hearsay-at-preliminary-hearing** challenge, and took the contempt matter **under advisement**. After a **June 22–23** interval, **Judge Graf ruled on June 26** in a **20-page order** ([[2026-06-26 - Ruling on Contempt Order to Show Cause|the ruling]]): he held **Christopher Ballard in civil contempt** — not for clarifying the inconclusive ballistics result (that was **protected** as a response to publicity the defense's own filing had generated), but for a **March statement to TMZ** touting the State's *"ample evidence"* to *"overcome"* Robinson's presumption of innocence, which the court found *"possessed a substantial likelihood of materially prejudicing the proceedings by communicating the prosecutor's assessment of the defendant's guilt."* Critically, Graf **declined the defense's headline sanction and kept the death penalty on the table**, imposing lesser remedies instead (measures at jury selection; the defense may recover its contempt-related fees) ([CNN, June 26, 2026](https://www.cnn.com/2026/06/26/us/charlie-kirk-tyler-robinson-contempt-ruling)). See [[Judge Graf Is Bought Off|Judge Graf claim check]].

**The defense's reading:** the State tried its case in the press — spinning an **inconclusive** ballistics result (see [[The Inconclusive Bullet]]) as damning — and **tainted the jury pool.**

> **The publicity has measurable reach.** Litigating the defense's motion to bar courtroom cameras, its trial-consultant expert (Dr. Bryan Edelman) surveyed **200 Utah County residents** and found **99% recognize the case and 64% already believe Robinson is guilty** based on media coverage — the defense's evidence that pretrial publicity has saturated the venue. Judge Graf nonetheless **denied** the categorical camera ban ([[2026-05-08 - Ruling Denying Motion to Exclude Cameras|May 8 ruling]]), keeping cameras in the courtroom under a standing order. The 64% figure is the empirical spine of any future **change-of-venue / fair-trial** argument. See [[What Is Genuinely Open]].
**The Other Side:** prosecutors are permitted **some** public comment (the charges, the burden of proof), and the State argued Ballard's remarks stayed within bounds (citing a 2026 out-of-state decision); it also noted that **defense counsel herself** had given a media interview in an unrelated case. Whether any of it warrants sanctions was left for the court to decide.

## 3. The death penalty was announced before the evidence was disclosed
The State announced it would seek **death at the time of arrest**, yet months later — at the **December 11, 2025** hearing — the defense pressed that the State had **"not provided" the aggravation basis** and that **thousands of discovery files** were still outstanding (see [[Gaps in the Investigation]]). Deciding to seek execution first and disclose the supporting evidence later is, the defense argues, the process running **backwards.**

## 4. Discovery that arrived late — or not at all
The disclosure record has repeated gaps: **"significant discovery materials that have not been received"** (Dec 11), and **ATF lab reports produced only "late last week"** before the June 22 hearing, against which the defense lined up **seven live forensic examiners** (see [[Chain of Custody Problems]]). Late, rolling disclosure of forensic material is a recurring **problem for the reliability** of what the State ultimately presents.

## 5. Evidence the State could not authenticate
On **Day 1** of the preliminary hearing, the State's marquee **compilation video (Exhibit 12.1)** — assembled by a County Attorney staffer with zooms, blurs, and circles — was **excluded** because the State **could not produce an unaltered version** or the person who made the alterations (see [[The Surveillance Video Was Doctored]] and [[Chain of Custody Problems]]). Offering an **annotated, unauthenticated** exhibit as evidence is exactly the shortcut the rules exist to catch.

## 6. Proving the DNA through a conduit, not a live examiner
On **Day 2** of the preliminary hearing, the State put its central DNA conclusion in through **Sgt. Jennifer Faumuina** — the chain-of-custody witness — who **read the FBI's numbers off a report she did not author** (Exhibit 31, signed by examiner Amanda Bakker) [05:07:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=18430s), [05:11:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=18662s). She is, in other words, a **conduit** for a forensic conclusion she cannot defend on the merits, and the defense **reserved her cross** [05:13:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=18801s). *(In fairness, the actual examiner, Bakker, did testify — but out of order, called by the **defense** by stipulation to challenge reliability, not by the State to prove its case.)* See [[Chain of Custody Problems]] and [[2026-07-07 - Preliminary Hearing Day 2|Day 2 Legal Review]].
**The Other Side:** in Utah, **reliable hearsay is admissible at a prelim** (Rule 1102), so reading an FBI report through a conduit is permitted at this stage; the numbers still have to be defended live at trial, and the examiner was available.

## 7. The Exhibit 5.1 motive overreach — provisionally admitted, not published
Also on Day 2, the State moved **Exhibit 5.1**, a TPUSA statement describing the group's "**Orthodox Christian views** on sexuality, marriage, gender," to seat a **motive** theory and the victim-targeting enhancement [03:06:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=11160s). The defense (Novak) argued it says **nothing about Robinson's state of mind** and risks telling the jury pool "this is a case about religion" — which the State does not even allege. The court **provisionally admitted** it, **expressly reserved** whether it connects to Robinson's state of mind, **declined to publish** it, and **sustained** the objection to the prosecutor reading the unadmitted exhibit aloud [03:42:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=13354s). The defense reads this as the State reaching for a religion-motive frame the record does not yet support.
**The Other Side:** the prosecutor **telegraphed** a legitimate motive theory — Robinson's "gender persuasion" put him on the other side of Kirk's political expression, to be proven through other witnesses and Robinson's own statements [03:27:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=12443s) — so the enhancement fight is **deferred, not defeated**, and the exhibit is "in" for now.

## 8. Day 4 (July 9) — the State's own witness undercuts the motive enhancement
Read favorably to the defense, the State's Day-4 case handed the defense its best point on **motive** — even as the day's other rulings went **against** the defense. See [[2026-07-09 - Preliminary Hearing Day 4|Day 4 Legal Review]] and [[2026-07-09 - Preliminary Hearing Day 4 Transcript|Day 4 Transcript]].

- **The immunized roommate cut against the victim-targeting enhancement.** In the recorded statement the State itself played, [[Lance Twiggs]] said he had **"never heard [Robinson] talk about Charlie Kirk before specifically"** and recalled **no LGBTQ / gender-identity discussions** [02:30:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=9052s). The victim-targeting enhancement (§ 76-3-203.14) requires that Robinson selected Kirk **because of his political expression** — yet the State's closest-witness account supplies **no** such political or identity motive. This is the same soft spot flagged in §7 above (the Exhibit 5.1 "Orthodox Christian views" overreach): the State keeps reaching for a motive its own proof does not seat. See [[Beating Some Charges Losing Others]] and [[Confessions and Admissions]].

> 🎥 **Forbes Breaking News (Day 4):** Robinson's roommate asked point-blank whether Robinson had ever talked about Charlie Kirk.

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wMQHCYeANnE)

- **The publicity fight went against the defense.** The court **overruled** the defense's objection to **publishing** the confession exhibits, **distinguishing *Rideau*** ("no inherent or presumed prejudice") [01:27:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=5263s)–[01:28:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=5336s) and adopting a three-tier admit/show/publish method [00:36:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=2192s). The material the defense fought to keep from the jury pool was **published with targeted redactions** — a **defense loss**, of a piece with the earlier disqualification and stay denials, preserved for later challenge.
- **The enhanced-video compromise.** The enhanced surveillance video (Exhibit 12.1) — the exhibit **excluded on Day 1** for authentication — is to be shown to the **Kirk family in the courtroom only, not televised** [08:22:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89ZKQIHaJpM&t=30155s): a middle path that neither publishes it broadly nor keeps it entirely out. (On **Day 5** it was published in-courtroom with the **livestream switched to audio-only** — [[2026-07-10 - Preliminary Hearing Day 5|see below]].)

## 9. Day 5 (July 10) — the camera breach and the broadcast sanction
The Day-4 publication order was **breached the next morning**. A **gallery-only** side-by-side image of the [[The Discord and Text Messages|Robinson–Twiggs messages]] (Exhibit 16) — which the court had said could be shown in the room but **not broadcast** — was briefly **caught by the media camera and pushed to the livestream**. The defense pressed it as a **fair-trial-rights** violation: the barred image "**has been published and is even able to be purchased as photographic prints**," and **Richard Novak** moved to **bar the news-media camera** for the rest of the hearing and for the Sept. 1 argument [01:19:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r3OUG7n37po&t=4787s).
- **The court found its order violated** [01:33:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r3OUG7n37po&t=5629s) — but, weighing the **constitutional fair-trial rights of Robinson and Erika Kirk** against **public transparency**, **denied** the camera ban (defense motion 305) and the alternative all-electronics ban [01:34:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r3OUG7n37po&t=5650s)–[01:34:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r3OUG7n37po&t=5671s), imposing instead a **proportional sanction**: the media "**shall no longer capture or broadcast any exhibits displayed by the parties during today's proceedings**" [01:34:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r3OUG7n37po&t=5691s). Both the media (**Michael Judd**) and the State (**McBride**) agreed the image should not have aired, though McBride noted the **text** was already public in law-enforcement filings.
- **Net:** a **defense-flavored** result on the publicity axis — the court agreed its order was breached and its remedy tightened the broadcast — but the camera **stayed on**, and the recurring theme (this record leaking into the jury pool) is again **preserved** rather than resolved. See [[2026-07-10 - Preliminary Hearing Day 5|Day 5 Legal Review]], [[Judge Tony Graf]].

## The Other Side
None of this is **innocence**, and most of it does not stop a **bind-over** (a preliminary hearing needs only probable cause). The disqualification motion was **denied**; the contempt motion **succeeded only in part** — a prosecutor was held in civil contempt, but the **death penalty stayed on the table**; late discovery and an excluded exhibit are **trial** problems, not proof of a frame; and the Day-2 conduit and motive-exhibit issues are **hearsay-and-relevance rulings a prelim tolerates**, deferred to trial. But a presumption of innocence is, at bottom, a rule about **how the State must behave** — and this is the running tally of where the defense says it has fallen short. See [[Insufficiency of the Evidence]], [[Chain of Custody Problems]], [[Gaps in the Investigation]], and [[Defense Strategies and Degrees of Liability]].

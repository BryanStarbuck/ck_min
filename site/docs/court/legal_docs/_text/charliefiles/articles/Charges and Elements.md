## How to read the checklists
A conviction requires the State to prove **every** element of a count — miss one and the count fails. Below, each **☐ box is one element** the prosecution must establish. At this stage (a **preliminary hearing**) the State need only show **probable cause** on each; at **trial** it must prove each **beyond a reasonable doubt** (see [§ the standards ↓](#the-legal-standard-a-preliminary-hearing-is-not-a-trial)). The single element that runs through *every* count — and the heart of the defense — is **identity**: that the person who did these things was **Robinson**. See [[How Robinson Was Identified]].

## Count 1 — Aggravated Murder · [§ 76-5-202](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title76/Chapter5/76-5-S202.html)

> **Statute (verbatim).** "An actor commits aggravated murder if the actor **intentionally or knowingly causes the death of another individual** under any of the following circumstances… **(iii) the actor knowingly created a great risk of death to another individual other than the deceased individual and the actor**." — § 76-5-202(2)(a).

**Elements the State must prove:**
- [ ] **Act + causation** — the actor *caused the death* of another individual (Charlie Kirk).
- [ ] **Mental state (mens rea)** — the actor acted **intentionally or knowingly**.
- [ ] **Identity** — that the actor was **Tyler Robinson**.
- [ ] **The aggravating circumstance (the capital trigger)** — the actor **"knowingly created a great risk of death to another individual other than the deceased individual and the actor"** — i.e., the bystanders under and around the canopy. § 76-5-202(2)(a)(iii).

**Why it's capital.** Aggravated murder is a **capital felony *only if* the prosecutor files a notice of intent to seek the death penalty** ([§ 76-5-202(3)(a)](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title76/Chapter5/76-5-S202.html)); otherwise it is a noncapital first-degree felony (3)(b). The notice must be filed **within 60 days after arraignment** absent stipulation or good cause (3)(c). **Defeat the aggravator and it is no longer a death case** — a murder conviction without the (2)(a)(iii) circumstance is not death-eligible. That is the highest-value target in the case; see [[Beating Some Charges Losing Others]].

**Two statutory off-ramps that reduce aggravated murder to murder** (each, if found, drops the conviction one step, per § 76-5-202(3)(f) and (4)):
- **Special mitigation** (extreme emotional disturbance / delusion, § 76-5-205.5) — proven by a preponderance → judgment entered for **murder**, not aggravated murder.
- **Imperfect self-defense / affirmative defense** (§ 76-5-202(4)) — a *reasonable but mistaken* belief in legal justification → judgment for **murder**.

**Merger.** An aggravating circumstance that is itself a separate offense (e.g., the felony discharge) **does not merge** — Robinson can be convicted of *both* aggravated murder and the separate offense (§ 76-5-202(5)).

## Count 2 — Felony Discharge of a Firearm · [§ 76-11-210](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title76/Chapter11/76-11-S210.html)

> **Statute (verbatim).** "An actor commits felony discharge of a firearm if… **the actor discharges a firearm in the direction of an individual, knowing or having reason to believe that an individual may be endangered by the discharge of the firearm**." — § 76-11-210(2)(a). "A violation… that causes **serious bodily injury** to an individual is a **first degree felony**." — § 76-11-210(3)(c).

**Elements the State must prove:**
- [ ] **Act** — the actor **discharged a firearm**.
- [ ] **Direction** — the discharge was **in the direction of an individual**.
- [ ] **Mental state** — **knowing or having reason to believe** a person may be **endangered** by it.
- [ ] **Identity** — the shooter was **Robinson**.
- [ ] **Result that sets the degree** — the discharge **caused serious bodily injury** → **first-degree felony** (here, the fatal wound).

> **Recodification note.** This offense was **renumbered in 2025** from the old § 76-10-508.1 to **§ 76-11-210** (Utah's weapons statutes moved into Title 76, Chapter 11). The elements are unchanged; some early filings/coverage cite the old number.

## Counts 3 & 4 — Obstruction of Justice · [§ 76-8-306](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title76/Chapter8/76-8-S306.html)

> **Statute (verbatim).** "An actor commits obstruction of justice… if the actor, **with intent to hinder, delay, or prevent the investigation, apprehension, prosecution, conviction, or punishment of any person** regarding conduct that constitutes a criminal offense… **(c) alters, destroys, conceals, or removes an item or other thing**." — § 76-8-306(2). A violation is a **second degree felony if the underlying conduct would be a capital or first degree felony** — § 76-8-306(3)(a).

**Elements the State must prove (each count):**
- [ ] **Specific intent** — acted **with intent to hinder, delay, or prevent** an investigation/prosecution "regarding conduct that constitutes a criminal offense."
- [ ] **A qualifying act** — **altered, destroyed, concealed, or removed an item** (§ 76-8-306(2)(c)).
- [ ] **Identity** — that **Robinson** did it.
- **Count 3** applies the act to **the rifle** (hidden/concealed on Sept. 10). See [[The Rifle]].
- **Count 4** applies the act to **the clothing** he wore (discarded ~Sept. 11). See [[The Clothing Was Never Recovered]].

> **Degree tracks the underlying crime.** Because the underlying offense (aggravated murder) is a **capital felony**, each obstruction count is a **second-degree felony** (§ 76-8-306(3)(a)). Note also that obstruction is a **general-intent-to-hinder** crime — the State need not prove the underlying murder to prove obstruction, only that Robinson acted to impede an investigation "regarding conduct that constitutes a criminal offense."

## Counts 5 & 6 — Tampering with a Witness · [§ 76-8-508](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title76/Chapter8/76-8-S508.html)

> **Statute (verbatim).** "An actor commits tampering with a witness if the actor **(a)(i) believes that an official proceeding or investigation is pending or about to be instituted; or (ii) intends to prevent an official proceeding or investigation; and (b) attempts to induce or otherwise cause another individual to… (ii) withhold testimony, information, a document, or an item**…" — § 76-8-508(2). "A violation… is a **third degree felony**." — § 76-8-508(3).

**Elements the State must prove (each count):**
- [ ] **Belief/intent** — the actor **believed a proceeding or investigation was pending or about to begin** (or intended to prevent one).
- [ ] **The attempt to induce** — **attempted to induce or cause another individual** (his roommate, [[Lance Twiggs]]) to **withhold** information or an item (or testify/inform falsely, elude process, or be absent).
- [ ] **Identity** — that **Robinson** made the attempt.
- **Count 5** = the instruction to **delete the text messages**. See [[The Discord and Text Messages]].
- **Count 6** = the instruction to **stay silent / not speak to police**.

> **Note.** The offense is complete on the **attempt to induce** — the State need not prove the witness actually complied. It **does not merge** with other offenses committed in the course of it (§ 76-8-508(4)). Both counts live almost entirely in the **[[The Discord and Text Messages|messages]]**, which is where the defense will contest **attribution** and the **"instruction" reading**.

## Count 7 — Violent Offense in the Presence of a Child · [§ 76-3-203.10](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title76/Chapter3/76-3-S203.10.html)

> **Statute (verbatim).** "**In the presence of a child** means… **(i) in the physical presence of a child younger than 14 years old; and (ii) having knowledge that the child is present and may see or hear the commission of a violent criminal offense.**" A person violates the section if the person **"commits or attempts to commit criminal homicide… against a third party in the presence of a child."** — § 76-3-203.10(1)–(2). The base offense is a **class B misdemeanor** (3).

**Elements the State must prove:**
- [ ] **Predicate violent act** — committed (or attempted) **criminal homicide against a third party** (Kirk).
- [ ] **A child physically present** — a **child younger than 14** was in the **physical presence** of the offense.
- [ ] **Knowledge** — the actor **knew the child was present** and may **see or hear** it.
- [ ] **Identity** — that the actor was **Robinson**.

> **Degree note.** The base offense under § 76-3-203.10(3) is a **class B misdemeanor**, but the Information charges Count 7 as a **class A misdemeanor** — it pleads the offense under **§ 76-3-203.10(2) *and* the victim-targeting enhancement § 76-3-203.14(3)(a)(ii)**, which raises a class B one step to a class A. This is the **lowest-stakes** count and a plausible jury off-ramp.

## The aggravators and enhancements — the prosecution's "extra" components

Beyond the elements of each count, the State pleads two things that **escalate exposure**: the **capital aggravator** inside Count 1, and a **sentence enhancement** attached to the case.

### 1. The capital aggravator — "great risk of death to another" · § 76-5-202(2)(a)(iii)
This is the **element that makes the case a death case**. It is proven by the **shot's trajectory and geometry**: that firing a .30-06 across a crowded courtyard **knowingly created a great risk of death to people other than Kirk**. It is contested on the **line-of-sight / trajectory** evidence — see [[Line of Sight and Shot Geometry]] and [[A Bullet on the Other Roof]]. Defeating it does not defeat the homicide, but it **removes the death penalty**.

### 2. The victim-targeting penalty enhancement · [§ 76-3-203.14](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title76/Chapter3/76-3-S203.14.html)
> **Statute (verbatim).** "'Personal attribute' means… **(k) political expression**…" and "A defendant is subject to enhanced penalties… if the defendant **intentionally selects the victim of the criminal offense because of the defendant's belief or perception regarding the victim's personal attribute**." — § 76-3-203.14(1)–(2).

- [ ] **Intentional selection** — the actor **intentionally selected Kirk** as the victim.
- [ ] **Because of a personal attribute** — specifically Kirk's **political expression** (an enumerated attribute, (1)(k)).

**How it enhances.** For **most offenses** the enhancement **bumps the degree up one step** (§ 76-3-203.14(3)(a)). But for a **first-degree felony**, it does **not** bump the degree — instead the selection "**shall [be] consider[ed]… as an aggravating factor**" by the sentencing judge or the Board of Pardons and Parole (§ 76-3-203.14(3)(b)). In this Information the enhancement is **pled against Counts 1, 2, and 7**: against **Counts 1 and 2** (a capital and a first-degree felony) it operates as a **sentencing/parole aggravating factor** rather than a degree bump; against **Count 7** it bumps the base class B misdemeanor up to a **class A** ([§ 76-3-203.14(3)(a)(ii)](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title76/Chapter3/76-3-S203.14.html)). It is what supplies the case's **"political assassination"** framing.

**Two built-in limits the defense will press:**
- **The free-speech savings clause** — the section "**does not… affect or limit any individual's constitutional right to the lawful expression of free speech**" (§ 76-3-203.14(4)(c)), and expressly does not create a protected class beyond the penalty enhancement (4)(d).
- **The proof problem** — it requires proving Robinson's **motive** (*why* he selected Kirk). On **Day 2**, the State's proof for it — **Exhibit 5.1**, a Rule 1102 statement about TPUSA's mission and Kirk's "Orthodox Christian views" — was only **provisionally admitted and not published**, the court **reserving** whether it even bears on Robinson's state of mind ([Day 2, 02:52:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=10370s); court's handling [03:22:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=12132s)). See [[Beating Some Charges Losing Others]].

### 3. The notice of intent to seek death · § 76-5-202(3)(c)
Not an element, but the **procedural switch** that keeps the case capital: the prosecutor's **death-penalty notice** (filed within 60 days of arraignment, absent good cause). Without it, aggravated murder is a **noncapital first-degree felony**. The State **is** seeking death here.

## LOVID — the prosecutor's foundational checklist

Beyond the statutory elements of the specific crime, prosecutors work from an old trial-advocacy mnemonic — **LOVID** — for the **foundational facts every criminal case must establish** to make out a *prima facie* case and to fix the court's power to hear it. If the State fails to put any of these in the record, the case can be dismissed **before the elements are even reached**:

| Letter | Stands for | What it establishes | In *this* case |
|--------|-----------|---------------------|----------------|
| **L** | **Location** of the offense | *where* the crime physically happened | The **Losee Building rooftop** at Utah Valley University, shot fired into the **courtyard** below. |
| **O** | **Offense** (its elements) | *that a crime occurred* — each element of the charged offense | The elements of **aggravated murder** and the six other counts, above. |
| **V** | **Venue** | *jurisdiction* — the town/city, **county**, and **state** where it may be tried | **Utah County**, State of **Utah** → **Fourth Judicial District Court**, Provo. |
| **I** | **Identification** | *who did it* — the in-court identification of **this** defendant as the actor | The single most contested link: that **Robinson** is the shooter/texter. See [[How Robinson Was Identified]]. |
| **D** | **Date (and time)** | *when* it happened — for statute-of-limitations, alibi, and jurisdiction | **September 10, 2025**, ~**12:20 p.m.** during Kirk's canopy event. |

**Why it matters here.** Four of LOVID's five points are essentially uncontested (location, the fact of the offense, venue, date/time). The whole defense concentrates on the **"I" — Identification** — which is exactly why the identity element recurs in every count's checklist above. *(LOVID is a practitioner's mnemonic, not a statute; it captures the jurisdictional/prima-facie basics prosecutors are trained not to forget in an opening. Sources: practitioner discussions of the LOVID checklist. See [Justia — Q&A on LOVID](https://www.justanswer.com/traffic-law/2elyx-policeman-gave-ticket-following-close-him.html); [Quizlet — LOVID/B.O.P.](https://quizlet.com/213832004/lovid-bop-flash-cards/).)*

## The legal standard: a preliminary hearing is *not* a trial

### What a preliminary hearing is
A **preliminary hearing** (preliminary examination) is the **screening step** where a **magistrate** decides whether the State has enough evidence to send a felony case to trial. In Utah it is governed by **[Rule 7B, Utah Rules of Criminal Procedure](https://legacy.utcourts.gov/rules/view.php?type=urcrp&rule=7B)** and grounded in the **Utah Constitution, Art. I § 13**. The **State bears the burden and goes first**; the defendant may **cross-examine** the State's witnesses, and after the State rests may **testify, call witnesses, and present evidence** (Rule 7B(a)). The magistrate does **not** decide guilt — only whether the case **crosses the threshold to proceed**.

### The probable-cause / bindover standard
> **Rule 7B(b), verbatim.** "**If from the evidence the magistrate finds probable cause to believe that the crime charged has been committed and that the defendant has committed it, the magistrate must order that the defendant be bound over for trial. The findings of probable cause may be based, in whole or in part, on reliable hearsay.** Objections to evidence on the ground that it was acquired by unlawful means are not properly raised at the preliminary examination."

Utah's appellate courts define how a magistrate applies that standard (**[State v. Clark, 2001 UT 9](https://law.justia.com/cases/utah/supreme-court/2001/clarkandsmith.html)**):
- The magistrate must **view all evidence in the light most favorable to the prosecution** and **draw all reasonable inferences in favor of the State**.
- The quantum of proof is **low** — "**a reasonable belief that an offense has been committed and that the defendant committed it**" — the **same** standard as for an arrest warrant, and **less** than would defeat a directed-verdict motion.
- Critically: where reasonable inferences **cut both ways**, the magistrate has **no discretion to choose** — the conflict is left **for the jury at trial**. That is why competing defense inferences rarely defeat bindover.
- **Reliable hearsay is admissible** (Rule 7B(b); [Utah R. Evid. 1102](https://legacy.utcourts.gov/rules/view.php?type=ure&rule=1102)) — which is how the State can present a case agent's summary and recorded statements (e.g., the [[Lance Twiggs]] interview) **without calling every witness live**.

### Probable cause vs. proof beyond a reasonable doubt

| | **Preliminary hearing** | **Trial** |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard** | **Probable cause** — a reasonable belief the crime occurred and the defendant did it | **Beyond a reasonable doubt** — near-certainty; the highest standard in law |
| **Who decides** | A **magistrate** (judge) | A **jury** (or judge at a bench trial) |
| **Evidence lens** | **Light most favorable to the prosecution**; inferences drawn *for* the State | **Presumption of innocence**; the State must overcome it |
| **Competing inferences** | Go to the **State** (jury question) | A reasonable doubt means **acquittal** |
| **Hearsay** | **Admissible** if reliable (Rule 1102) | Generally **excluded**; live testimony + **Confrontation Clause** |
| **The question asked** | *Is there enough to proceed to trial?* | *Is the defendant guilty to a moral certainty?* |
| **Outcome** | **Bindover** or dismissal | **Conviction** or acquittal |

> **The upshot.** A **bindover is not a finding of guilt** — it means only that the case **survives screening**. Almost all seven counts are likely to bind over on this record, because the standard is so State-favorable. The defense's element-by-element fight is aimed at the **trial**, where the numbers flip: every reasonable doubt now favors Robinson. That gap — *probable cause here, beyond a reasonable doubt there* — is the frame for Part II.

## Count 2 — Felony Discharge of a Firearm
**Prosecution (satisfies):** The shot was fired from the Losee roof **in Kirk's direction**, causing the fatal neck wound — the "serious bodily injury" that makes it first-degree — Day 2 [00:54:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=3244s).
**Defense (contests):** Rides on the **same contested identity**, and the **death round is not matched to the rifle** — the fragment "could not be identified or excluded" ([[The Inconclusive Bullet]], [[The Casing Match and DNA]]).

## Counts 3 & 4 — Obstruction of Justice

### ☐ Count 3 — concealing the rifle
**Prosecution (satisfies):** The **Mauser was wrapped in a towel off-path in the trees** along the escape route — not simply dropped in flight — Day 2 [02:20:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=8441s); the messages describe a rifle **"drop point"** ([[The Discord and Text Messages]], [[The Rifle]]).
**Defense (contests):** Concealment is **equally consistent with flight**, not with a proven **intent to hinder an investigation**; and it depends on identity plus the towel's DNA-reliability fight (Twiggs's own result was "uninformative" — Day 2 [07:20:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=26404s)).

### ☐ Count 4 — discarding the clothing
**Prosecution (satisfies):** Per the Information, Robinson said he **"disposed of the clothes in different areas"** ([[The Surrender]]); surveillance shows a change out of the shooting clothes/disguise ([[Defense Problems]]).
**Defense (contests):** The clothing, cap, and glasses were **never recovered**, so the disposal act is **unproven** — absence is not the same as a documented "ditch," and it doubles as an identity hole ([[The Clothing Was Never Recovered]]).

## Counts 5 & 6 — Tampering with a Witness

### ☐ Count 5 — telling Twiggs to delete the texts
**Prosecution (satisfies):** The Information charges Robinson with directing his roommate to **delete the messages**; the thread came in as **Exhibit 16** — Day 3 [01:13:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szsmK2Ul9XY&t=4396s) ([[The Discord and Text Messages]]).
**Defense (contests):** The "instruction" reading and its **attribution** are contested — the **softest felony to pick off** ([[Beating Some Charges Losing Others]]); the messages were in fact **preserved** (they became the case), so any inducement failed; and [[Lance Twiggs]] is **un-cross-examinable**, leaving a preserved **Confrontation-Clause** objection ([[Chain of Custody Problems]]).

### ☐ Count 6 — telling Twiggs to stay silent
**Prosecution (satisfies):** The Information charges directing the roommate **not to talk to police**, contemporaneous with the "pending investigation" belief evident in the surrender messages.
**Defense (contests):** Twiggs in fact **cooperated** — two recorded interviews (Sept. 12, 2025 and April 20, 2026) — Day 3 [01:03:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szsmK2Ul9XY&t=3832s); both the "belief a proceeding is pending" and the inducement live only in **unconfronted hearsay**, and the record is thin on a discrete quoted instruction — **OPEN**.

## Count 7 — Violent Offense in the Presence of a Child
**Prosecution (satisfies):** The count as charged alleges a **child younger than 14 present**, with **knowledge** the child was present and could see or hear it — [Initial Appearance, 00:10:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BsNojXN6iUw&t=659s). Bystander **Amber (Amanda) Wright** was present with "**her two young children**" — Day 1 [04:56:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6fq2kCMVMXI&t=17784s); an officer canvassing the scene found a **child on the ground next to a wheelchair** (a seizure, uninjured) — Day 1 [01:39:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6fq2kCMVMXI&t=5966s).
**Defense (contests):** The **"knowingly present"** mental state is the weak seam — a shooter ~143 yards away on a roof cannot easily be shown to have **known a specific under-14 child was present**. Low-stakes misdemeanor, a plausible jury off-ramp; record thin on the knowledge element — **OPEN** ([[Beating Some Charges Losing Others]]).

## Enhancement — Victim-Targeting (political expression)
**Prosecution (satisfies):** The note names the specific target — *"take out **Charlie Kirk**"*; the prosecutor telegraphed the theory that Robinson's **"gender persuasion"** put him on the other side of Kirk's political expression, to be proven through Robinson's **own statements** — Day 2 [03:27:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=12443s).
**Defense (contests):** The State's proof — **TPUSA Exhibit 5.1** — was only **provisionally admitted, not published, and its link to Robinson's state of mind expressly reserved** — Day 2 [03:42:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=13354s); the statute lists **"political expression" (k)** separately from **"religion" (m)**, while the exhibit speaks to Kirk's *religious* views and says **nothing about Robinson's** state of mind — Day 2 [02:52:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y6ofpz6ReuE&t=10370s); and § 76-3-203.14(4)(c) preserves the **free-speech** limit ([[Prosecution Problems]]).

## The confessions, mapped to the counts they prove
Robinson's own words — catalogued in [[Confessions and Admissions]] — do much of the State's element-work at once, which is why the confession record and the charge sheet are best read together:

| Robinson's admission | The element / count it proves |
|---|---|
| *"I am, I'm sorry"* (texts, Discord, and in person to [[Lance Twiggs]]) | **Identity** + **the act / causation** — Counts 1 & 2 |
| the note: *"…on a mission… I had the opportunity to take out Charlie Kirk, and I took it"* + texts describing ~a week of planning | **Mens rea — intentional / knowing & premeditation** — Count 1 |
| *"I had enough of his hatred, some hate can't be negotiated out"* | **Motive** → the **victim-targeting enhancement** *(contested — cf. Twiggs's "never talked about Kirk," see [[Prosecution Problems]])* |
| the rifle *"drop point,"* *"wrapped in a towel,"* *"changed outfits"* | **Obstruction — Count 3** (concealing the rifle) |
| *"disposed of the clothes in different areas"* (to his parents) | **Obstruction — Count 4** (discarding clothing) |
| *"delete this exchange"* | **Witness tampering — Count 5** |
| *"if any police ask you questions, ask for a lawyer and stay silent"* (to Twiggs) | **Witness tampering — Count 6** |

**What the confessions do *not* reach.** They go to identity, the act, intent, and the add-on counts — but they do **not**, by themselves, prove **Count 7's** "child *knowingly* present" element or the **capital aggravator's** "great risk to others" geometry. So even a full confession record leaves the **two highest-value defense targets** (the aggravator and the enhancement) to be fought on **non-confession** grounds — see [[Beating Some Charges Losing Others]]. For how each admission is authenticated and why it is hard to exclude, see [[Confessions and Admissions]].

## The two cuts that run through everything

Strip the counts down and the defense's leverage concentrates in **two shared vulnerabilities**, not thirteen separate ones:

1. **Identity.** Every count needs the actor to be **Robinson**. Four of LOVID's five foundations are conceded; the "I" is the entire fight, and it is built on **gait + vehicle + DNA + surrender** rather than a face on the roof. Win reasonable doubt on identity and the whole indictment wobbles at once. See [[How Robinson Was Identified]] and [[Insufficiency of the Evidence]].
2. **The Confrontation Clause.** Counts 3–7 and the enhancement lean heavily on **unconfronted, Rule 1102 hearsay** — the Information's recitals, Exhibit 16, and the [[Lance Twiggs|Twiggs]] recorded statements — reliable enough for **probable cause** but a live confrontation problem at **trial**. See [[Chain of Custody Problems]].

And the single highest-value target is narrower than "not guilty": **defeat the capital aggravator** (§ 76-5-202(2)(a)(iii)) and, even if the homicide stands, **the death penalty is gone** ([[Beating Some Charges Losing Others]]).

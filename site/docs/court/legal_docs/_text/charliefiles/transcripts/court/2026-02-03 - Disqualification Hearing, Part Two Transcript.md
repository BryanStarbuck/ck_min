## Summary
**What happened:** This was Part Two of the evidentiary hearing on the [[The Defense Team|defense]]'s motion to disqualify the entire Utah County Attorney's Office, resumed after the January 16, 2026 session. The core factual issue is that the adult daughter of Chief Deputy County Attorney Chad Grunander ("Prosecutor A") was present in the crowd at Utah Valley University when Charlie Kirk was shot, and Grunander was later assigned to lead the prosecution of [[Tyler Robinson]]. The court first denied the defense's renewed request to refer the disqualification question to the Utah Attorney General, then heard continued cross-examination of elected County Attorney Jeff Gray, direct and cross examination of Chad Grunander, and testimony from State Bureau of Investigation agent Dave Hull about the volume of independent evidence in the case. The defense argued the most senior prosecutor should have been "walled off" and that Gray's casual, undocumented handling created an appearance of impropriety in a capital case; the state argued the daughter is not a material witness, saw nothing others did not, and that no conflict — actual or apparent — exists. The court closed the evidence and closing arguments, reserved its ruling, expressly stated its "mind is not made up," and set a February 24, 2026 Webex hearing to issue an oral and written ruling. (The motion was ultimately denied February 24, 2026.)
**For the prosecution:** The state kept the disqualification question in the county's own hands (the AG-referral request was denied), and put on evidence that Grunander's daughter is a non-material witness among roughly 3,000 attendees and that the case rests on independent DNA, video, and forensic evidence — see [[Chain of Custody Problems]] and [[Gaps in the Investigation]].
**For the defense:** The defense built a record that Grunander was never screened off, that no memo documents any conflict analysis, and that the daughter's videos and statement were not gathered until the defense raised the issue — grounds it argued require disqualifying the office under a capital case's heightened reliability standard (see [[Defense Strategies and Degrees of Liability]]).

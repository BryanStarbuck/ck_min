## Summary
**What happened:** [[Judge Tony Graf]] took up [[The Defense Team|the defense's]] motion to disqualify [[The Prosecution|the Utah County Attorney's Office]], which rests on the allegation that a line prosecutor's adult child attended the Turning Point USA event at Utah Valley University where Charlie Kirk was shot and was in the crowd that fled. The defense (Richard Novak) opened by asking the court to strike the office's self-authored opposition and to refer the motion — at least for this limited purpose — to the Utah Attorney General under *State v. Jimenez* and Utah Code § 17-68-304, arguing the office cannot litigate a conflict allegation against itself; the state (Jeffrey Gray and Ryan McBride) objected, calling the request untimely and warning that alleging conflicts can become a forum-shopping tactic. After a recess and questioning, the court found the defense had not, on the briefing alone, made the threshold showing needed to compel a referral, and ruled the evidentiary hearing would proceed. The court denied a blanket request to close the entire hearing (agreeing to consider closure witness-by-witness), then found a violation of paragraph 48 of its December 23 decorum order after the pool camera captured [[Tyler Robinson|Robinson]] and counsel with their lips visible; as a sanction it barred the video camera from showing Robinson for the rest of the day. The defense then called Jeffrey Gray as its first witness and examined him at length about the prosecutor ("Prosecutor A"), the child's presence and location relative to the alleged shooter, whether any screening was done, and measurements Gray helped take at UVU; the day recessed mid-examination with the court asking counsel to confer on whether to finish the remaining witnesses that day or in a later setting. This is Part One of a continued evidentiary hearing (the matter carried over to a subsequent date; the motion was ultimately denied February 24, 2026).
**For the prosecution:** The state prevailed on the day's threshold questions — the court declined to refer the motion to the Attorney General on the briefing alone, refused to close the whole hearing, and let the Utah County Attorney's Office proceed to defend itself.
**For the defense:** The defense kept its conflict theory alive and put the elected County Attorney on the stand under oath, drawing testimony that no formal screening was set up and that Gray personally helped gather the "line of fire" measurements it says shows he made himself a witness — though the court rejected, for now, its bid to install conflict-free counsel for the state.

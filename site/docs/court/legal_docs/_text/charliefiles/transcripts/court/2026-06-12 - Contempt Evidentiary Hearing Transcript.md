# Contempt Evidentiary Hearing — Transcript (June 12, 2026)
An omnibus pretrial hearing in *State of Utah v. Tyler James Robinson* before [[Judge Tony Graf]] whose centerpiece was an evidentiary hearing on [[The Defense Team]]'s motion for an order to show cause, alleging that two prosecutors made extrajudicial statements to the media about the ballistics evidence in violation of the court's pretrial-publicity order. The same session also disposed of a defense motion to stay the case, heard argument on a constitutional challenge to hearsay at the preliminary hearing, and handled scheduling. · video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ws3jBiVuh5U

## Summary
**What happened:** The court first read its order **denying** [[Tyler Robinson]]'s motion to stay all proceedings — including the July 6 preliminary hearing — while the Utah Supreme Court considers his interlocutory appeal of the court's electronic-media (camera) ruling, finding the asserted threat of prejudice too contingent for that extraordinary relief. It then held the contempt evidentiary hearing: the defense called prosecutors Christopher Ballard and elected Utah County Attorney Jeff Gray as sworn witnesses and argued they violated the September 16, 2025 pretrial-publicity order by giving TMZ, USA Today, PolitiFact, Fox and Friends and other outlets statements about the disputed [[Chain of Custody Problems|bullet-fragment analysis]] after a defense filing generated headlines that the recovered bullet did not match Robinson's rifle. The defense asked the court, as the remedy for the alleged contempt, to bar the state from seeking the death penalty; the state responded that its statements were permitted by Rule 3.6(b) and (c) (public-record and reply-to-prejudice exceptions) and that no intentional violation occurred under the clear-and-convincing standard. The court took the contempt matter and a separately argued defense motion in limine to exclude hearsay at the preliminary hearing **under advisement**, setting a June 22 ruling date, and declined to change its briefing schedule for future media-coverage requests. (No contempt finding was made and the death penalty was not stricken at this hearing; those questions were reserved for the June 22 ruling.)
**For the prosecution:** The state prevailed on the day's one dispositive ruling — the stay was denied and the preliminary hearing stayed on track — and both prosecutor-witnesses testified they acted deliberately within Rule 3.6 to correct what they called false reporting, leaving the contempt and death-penalty questions unresolved rather than decided against them.
**For the defense:** The defense put the prosecutors' own media statements and admissions on the record and pressed its strongest theory — that talking to the press about "this bullet" while the case is pending was at least extreme recklessness warranting striking the death penalty — and preserved its stay, hearsay, and subpoena challenges for appellate and later review, though it won no ruling in its favor on June 12.

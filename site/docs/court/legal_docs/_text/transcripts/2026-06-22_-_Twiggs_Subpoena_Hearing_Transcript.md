A pre-preliminary-hearing motion hearing in *State of Utah v. Tyler James Robinson* at which Judge Tony Graf read his rulings on the defense's constitutional challenge to hearsay and on the defense subpoena seeking to compel ex-roommate Lance Twiggs to testify live. ## Summary
**What happened:** Judge Tony Graf denied the defense's motion in limine, holding that Article I, Section 12 of the Utah Constitution and Rule 1102 permit the state to establish probable cause through reliable hearsay at the preliminary hearing, and that the relaxed application of confrontation and related constitutional rights recognized in *State v. Timmerman* extends to this stage. He then denied the defense's application to certify an out-of-state subpoena compelling Lance Twiggs — Robinson's alleged ex-roommate and the source of a video-recorded interview — to testify in person, and granted the state's motion to quash, finding under Rule 14(a)(2) that the subpoena was unreasonable because the defense's proffer aimed at credibility and impeachment rather than at evidence reasonably likely to negate probable cause. The denial was entered without prejudice and subject to renewal. The court also set a follow-up hearing for June 26 and announced that no electronic devices would be permitted in the courtroom for the public and press at the preliminary hearing. (The defense later petitioned the Utah Supreme Court.)
**For the prosecution:** The state prevailed on both rulings — its planned use of Twiggs's recorded interview as reliable hearsay was upheld, and it will not have to produce Twiggs for live cross-examination at the preliminary hearing.
**For the defense:** This ruling cut against the defense — the court declined to force Twiggs's live testimony and permitted the state to proceed on hearsay, though the denial was without prejudice and the defense preserved its objections for later review.

## Summary
**What happened:** Judge Tony Graf issued rulings on two outstanding matters in *State of Utah v. Tyler James Robinson*. First, he granted the defense's motion for an order to show cause, setting an evidentiary hearing (during the June 12, 2026 proceeding) on whether Utah County Attorney Jeff Gray and deputy Chris Ballard violated the court's pretrial-publicity order by making statements to news outlets — while declining to compel the county attorney to produce the requested discovery. Second, he denied the defense's motion to close portions of the preliminary hearing and seal its exhibits, holding that the public and media enjoy a presumptive right of access that the defense had not overcome, though exhibits presented at the hearing may not be inspected or copied except as published in court.

**For the prosecution:** Faces an evidentiary hearing on the contempt/show-cause question, but the court declined to order it to produce the discovery the defense sought.
**For the defense:** Lost its motion to close the preliminary hearing; the court pointed to jury-selection safeguards (an enlarged venire, questionnaires, and voir dire) as less-restrictive alternatives, and reserved ruling on the pending motion to preclude rule 1102 evidence.
**For the media:** A win — the press and public keep access to the preliminary hearing and cameras are allowed, subject to the limit that hearing exhibits cannot be copied except as published in court.

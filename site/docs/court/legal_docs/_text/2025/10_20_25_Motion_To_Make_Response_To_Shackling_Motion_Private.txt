      On October 13, 2025, this Court emailed counsel an Interim Order ordering

the State and the Utah County Sheriff’s Office (“Sheriff’s Office”) to respond to

Defendant’s Motion to Appear in Civilian Clothing and Without Restraints. The

October 13th Interim Order permits the Sheriff’s Office to file its response “as a
sealed document” if it contains “secure information.” See October 13, 2025

Interim Order.

      The response does include information from the Court’s Security Director,

Chris Palmer, regarding specific security measures in this case. The Court should

therefore limit access to the response.

      Although the Court’s October 13th Interim Order contemplated classifying

as “sealed,” a response that contains “secure information.” The State and the

Sheriff’s Office believe that the more apt classification should be “private,”

because not even counsel for the parties can assess a “sealed” court record

without a court order. See Utah R. Jud. Admin. 4-202.03(2). Therefore, pursuant

to Rule 4-202.04 of the Utah Rules of Judicial Administration, the State and the

Sheriff’s Office request that the Court classify the response as “private.”

      On October 14, 2025, this Court entered on the docket a modified Interim

Order ordering the State the Sheriff’s Office to respond to Defendant’s motion.

The docketed October 14 Interim Order omits the reference to allowing the

response to be filed under seal. The State and the Sheriff’s Office believe that this

omission was a mere oversight. But even if this omission were intentional, the

Court should nevertheless classify the response as “private” because it contains

secure information that might endanger “personal and public safety” if

disclosed. See Utah R. Jud. Admin. 4-202(2)(B) (recognizing that interests of
“personal and public safety” justify restricting access to certain court records).

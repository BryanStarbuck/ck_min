# Demonstration of a Shaped Charge

This page presents a video demonstration of what a large **shaped charge** looks like when it detonates. The clip was circulated by defenders of the government's rifle narrative as a challenge to the [exploding mic](./overview) conclusion that most citizen investigators have reached: if a shaped charge had detonated on Charlie Kirk, they asked, why does the event footage not show a smoke and debris cloud like this one? The page records that question and the geometry questions that followed it, and sets out how investigators have answered each one. The short answer is scale: a breaching-size demonstration charge is not the gram-scale device investigators describe inside a lavalier transmitter.

## The Question Raised

The video was originally posted by **@Trillion0x**, who used it to push back on the exploding-mic theory as it went viral again. According to @Trillion0x:

> "CK exploding RODE mic is going viral again, bc it's wrong. More absurd CK theories go more viral. It would take less than an afternoon to demonstrate the theory. Jon Bray has been promising to do a simulated test of his theory for over 6mo. Where's that at? Shaped charges are [as shown]."

The clip was then quote-shared by **@troofevades**, who added:

> "Well, thats a lot of smoke that I dont see at the CK event. Holy fuck."

The question is straightforward: the charge in the demonstration produces a fireball, a substantial smoke cloud and scattered debris, and no cloud of that size is visible in the Utah Valley University footage. Investigators answer that the comparison is one of scale, not of kind. The demonstration uses a charge sized to breach material; the device investigators describe is a gram-scale micro-charge or explosively formed penetrator (EFP) inside a transmitter body, pressed against the body under a shirt, which would vent far less visible smoke into open air. And the UVU footage is not clean of a blast signature: [video frame analysis](./mic-explosion-video-analysis) shows the shirt expanding outward before the wound appears, and slow-motion clips show dark fragments falling from under the shirt.

## How Investigators Answer It

The rest of the [Microphone section](./overview) documents the case made by [Jon Bray's video analysis](./mic-explosion-video-analysis), the [RØDE Wireless PRO mic](./rode-wireless-mic), the [exploding pendant variant](./exploding-pendant-shaped-charge), and the [Navy SEAL testimony](./rob-oneill-navy-seal-testimony) of Rob O'Neill. Investigators conclude the device was very small — a micro-charge or explosively formed penetrator (EFP) rather than a large breaching charge — and would not produce the smoke cloud seen in this demonstration.

Asked whether even a small charge would leave a visible signature, investigators point to the signature the footage does show — the outward fabric motion, the [Canon XA55 audio](./Canon_XA55_Audio) separating a louder event at the tent from a distant report, and the [debris in the SUV](./burnt-object-rode-transmitter). On @Trillion0x's challenge that the theory could be tested in "less than an afternoon," investigators note that the decisive test was always available to the authorities: a residue swab of the shirt, the necklace, the SUV and the soil — none of which has ever been publicly released (see the [residue cover-up hub](./Explosive_Residue_Coverup/overview)).

## The geometry questions, and investigators' answers (2026)

Smoke was not the only question raised. In late June 2026, veteran commentator **[@angertab](https://x.com/angertab)** (Matt Tardio) posted a graphics-heavy thread arguing that a **shaped charge that fits a RØDE Wireless–class envelope** fails on **stand-off and priming geometry**: the detonator must be rear-center-primed; penetration through steel at optimal stand-off is a fraction of cone diameter; at shirt-to-neck distances far beyond optimal stand-off, the jet **dissipates**; and even a “directional” charge still produces **outward** blast that, in his view, would tear a large hole in the shirt and damage pectoral tissue. He attached a small-mass PETN demo as a scale reference and concluded the mic-shaped-charge kill claim is physically **impossible** as framed.

A related free-body argument (circulated by handles such as **@i11umin8ed**) holds that a shaped charge needs **rigid attachment** to the target; otherwise the device itself is launched opposite the jet “like a rocket.” A clip-on lav without metal strapping, in that view, cannot deliver a focused jet into the neck.

These posts are **citizen analyses**, not peer-reviewed ballistics papers, and they assume a classic Monroe-liner charge that investigators do not propose. Investigators answer that an **EFP / micro-charge** works under different stand-off rules, that the transmitter was [routed under the shirt](./AV_Team_Mic_Setup) and held against the body rather than hanging loose, that the [video analysis](./mic-explosion-video-analysis) shows fabric motion consistent with overpressure, and that the energetic may have been a [LiPO runaway](./lipo-battery-short-charge) rather than a lined charge at all. A "rocket" recoil of the device is also what investigators say the falling fragments and the [burnt object in the SUV](./burnt-object-rode-transmitter) reflect. The government's narrative remains a rooftop rifle shot.

## Public commentary

The large majority of citizen investigators on X have concluded the exploding mic killed Charlie Kirk; this demonstration is one of the challenges they have answered:

- Shaped-charge demonstration videos circulated by handles such as **@Trillion0x** and **@troofevades** show large smoke and fireball signatures and ask why the UVU footage lacks a comparable cloud.
- Investigators answer that a **micro-charge / EFP** would not resemble a breaching demo, and that the footage does show outward fabric motion and falling fragments.
- **@alexboge** (July 2026) elaborates Monroe-effect geometry requirements (liner, standoff, rigid fixation) and argues a 32 g clip-on RØDE cannot satisfy them without inventing a second “primer” charge — labeled by that author as epicycle reasoning.
- No court has yet examined residue evidence; none has been publicly tested.

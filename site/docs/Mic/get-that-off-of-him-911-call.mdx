# "Get That Off Of Him" — Alleged 911 Call Audio Tip

On July 27, 2026, [@DiligentDenizen](https://x.com/DiligentDenizen) posted a short clip in which
Candace Owens presents what is described as an **audio tip** — a line
reportedly captured on an **alleged 911 call** placed by Charlie Kirk's
security team while they were en route to the hospital on September 10, 2025. The line, repeated
twice on the clip, is: **"get that off of him."**

## What the post said

According to [@DiligentDenizen](https://x.com/DiligentDenizen/status/2081877698306048459), posted
July 27, 2026 (477 likes, 96 reposts, 8,453 views):

> ‼🇺🇸: @RealCandaceO reveals "GET THAT OFF OF HIM (Charlie)" audio tip of alleged 911 call from
> Charlie Kirk's security team on their way to the hospital on 9/10 👀
>
> Get WHAT off of him? 🤔
> His t-shirt?
> His MICROPHONE? 🤔
>
> I'd love to see TPUSA answer this?!

The poster is not asserting what "that" refers to — the post is framed as an **open question**
addressed to TPUSA.

## Transcript

The clip runs 23 seconds. Full transcript, as spoken:

> "Allegedly — allegedly not for long. The statement, *get that off of him. Get that off of him*,
> is captured on this alleged 911 call. Now, *get what off of him* is the first thing I think.
> Get what off of him?"

The speaker's own framing is important: the word **"allegedly"** is used three times in 23 seconds,
and the phrase "allegedly not for long" indicates the speaker expects further material to be
released rather than claiming the recording is already authenticated.

## Why this matters to the microphone thread

The clip is short and carries no independent documentation, but the question it raises sits
directly on top of the [exploding-mic theory](./mic-explosion-video-analysis):

- Charlie Kirk was wearing a [RØDE Wireless PRO lavalier transmitter](./rode-wireless-mic),
  reportedly routed **under** his shirt rather than over it — see [AV Team and Mic Setup](./AV_Team_Mic_Setup).
- If a device was **removed from his body** before he reached the hospital, that would be a
  chain-of-custody event that no public record currently documents.
- The same chain-of-custody question is raised independently on the
  [Table Jumpers & Device](./Table_Jumpers_Device) page and by the June 2026
  [SUV interior photos](./suv-transporting-charlie), where a burnt object was photographed in the
  vehicle used to transport him.

Proponents of the mic theory read "that" as the transmitter. That is an **interpretation**, not
something the audio establishes. Either way, the words describe people trying to help Charlie in a
moving vehicle; nothing indicates anyone on the security or AV team knew of any device.

## Other readings of the phrase

Other readings of the same three words have been offered:

- **Clothing.** Emergency responders routinely cut and strip clothing away from a trauma patient to
  expose the wound. "Get that off of him" is standard language in that context, and the post itself
  lists "his t-shirt" as the first possibility.
- **Equipment generally.** A lanyard, badge, jacket, or the mic could all be in the way of chest
  compressions or a wound assessment without any of it being sinister.
- **A bystander or a blanket.** The phrase does not specify an object at all.

None of these readings implies wrongdoing by anyone. Investigators note that none of them answers the
question the tip raises: where the RØDE transmitter went, since no public inventory of it has been released.

## How the tip travelled in later commentary

By early August 2026, public posts were already treating the phrase as settled evidence that a **RØDE pack** was stripped in the vehicle. Other speculative replies mapped the audio to an **OnStar / open-door automatic 911** path or to a **heart-rate monitor** rather than a microphone. None of those elaborations authenticate the underlying recording. They show how a **two-second alleged tip** becomes a Rorschach for every device theory in this section — lapel pack, [pendant](./exploding-pendant-shaped-charge), [earpiece](./exploding-earpiece-iem), or medical gear.

## What has not been established

- The 911 recording has **not been publicly released** by Utah County dispatch or any agency, and
  is not in the public court record.
- The **speaker is not identified**, and it is not established who was on the call.
- The clip presents an audio tip **secondhand**; the underlying file has not been independently
  authenticated, and this website has not verified it.
- **No wrongdoing** by any member of Charlie Kirk's security detail, by TPUSA, or by any other
  named person or organization is established by this clip. Nothing on this page is a finding of
  fact.

## Open questions

1. Will the full 911 audio be released, and by which agency?
2. Does the UVU GRAMA record set or the criminal discovery file
   contain the dispatch recording?
3. Does the timestamp of the call match the SUV's departure and arrival times documented on the
   [SUV photos](./suv-transporting-charlie) page?
4. Has TPUSA responded to the question the post raises?

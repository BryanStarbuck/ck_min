# FBI Seized Clothing From Charlie's Security Team

On **July 30, 2026**, Candace Owens said on her show that she had been told
the FBI took the clothing of certain members of Charlie Kirk's security detail
after September 10, 2025 — in her words, **"down to their boxer briefs."** The segment was clipped and
posted the same night by [@ProjectConstitu](https://x.com/ProjectConstitu/status/2082978765991293333),
which reached roughly 25,600 views, 862 likes and 194 reposts within hours.

The claim is **secondhand and unverified**. No agency has confirmed it, and Owens herself does not
name a source. It is documented here because, if accurate, it bears on a specific forensic question:
whether the clothing worn by the people closest to Kirk in the minutes after the shot was ever tested
for **explosive residue** — see [Removing Explosive Residue Cover-Up](./overview).

## The named individuals

Three living people are named in the clip. None of them is accused of a crime here, and nothing on
this page is a finding of fact about any of them.

- **Frank Turek** — Christian apologist and a Kirk mentor who was in the SUV.
  Owens questions his account of receiving scrubs; that is her **speculation**, not an established fact. Turek
  has publicly given his own account of the day and has publicly refuted conspiracy claims alleging
  TPUSA involvement — see his profile page. Receiving scrubs after being
  covered in blood has an obvious innocent explanation.
- **Brian Harpole** — founder of Integrity Security Solutions, present
  in the vehicle. The clip reports his account as differing from Turek's on who performed CPR. Two
  people recalling a chaotic ninety seconds differently is **ordinary**, not evidence of deception.
- **Rick Cutler** — described in the clip as holding Kirk in the vehicle.
  No agency has named him a person of interest and no criminal involvement is asserted. He is not
  accused of anything, and nothing indicates he knew of any device.

The conflicting-CPR-account question is documented separately and in more detail on the
[SUV transport](/Mic/suv-transporting-charlie) and Security Team pages.

## Why this matters to the residue question

If the clothing was seized and **preserved**, it is the single best surviving surface for an
explosive-residue test. Anyone performing chest compressions on Kirk in the back of the SUV had their
hands, forearms, and shirt front in direct contact with the wound area within a minute or two of the
shot. Post-blast residue transfers readily to fabric and survives for extended periods on stored
items.

That gives the seizure a testable consequence:

- If the clothing was **bagged and tested**, a result exists. Releasing it would answer the
  [exploding-mic](/Mic/overview) question directly — which is why its absence is what investigators
  keep pointing at.
- If the clothing was **bagged and never tested**, that is a documented decision not to run a
  standard forensic examination — the same category as the items on the
  Forensic Tests Skipped page.
- If the clothing was **destroyed**, the test can never be run. Owens raises this possibility and
  immediately labels it as her own belief that she cannot verify.

None of those three has been ruled out by any public document. **No evidence inventory, lab request, or residue result for any clothing item has been made
public.**

## The routine-procedure reading, and investigators' answer

The routine-procedure reading, stated plainly:

- **Seizing clothing is textbook procedure.** In any homicide, investigators collect the clothing of
  everyone who had physical contact with the victim. Blood, transfer patterns, and trace evidence all
  live on those garments. Taking undergarments as well is not unusual when a person's clothing is
  saturated.
- **Scrubs are the normal replacement.** A hospital hands out scrubs precisely because the people
  involved now have nothing to wear. That sequence — clothing collected, scrubs issued — is what you
  would expect to see whether or not anything else happened.
- **Non-disclosure is also normal.** Evidence inventories in a pending capital case are typically not
  public. The absence of a released test result is not, by itself, proof that no test was run.
- **Investigators' answer:** the clothing of the people who held Charlie Kirk is exactly the substrate
  an explosive-residue test works on. Collecting it is routine; never releasing a residue result for
  it is what leaves the question open. No wrongdoing by anyone is asserted here.

## What has not been established

- **No agency has confirmed** the seizure. The claim is secondhand and the source is not identified.
- **No wrongdoing** by the FBI, by any of its employees, by any member of Charlie Kirk's security
  detail, or by TPUSA is established by this clip. Nothing indicates anyone on the
  security or AV team knew of any device; the people whose clothing was reportedly taken were the
  people who carried and helped him.
- **Owens does not claim to know** what happened to the items; she says explicitly that it is "an
  unknowable thing" absent disclosure.
- **The government's narrative is a rifle gunshot wound.** Most citizen investigators have concluded
  the cause was an explosive at the microphone (see [the Mic hub](/Mic/overview)); this page documents
  why a residue test of this clothing matters to that question.

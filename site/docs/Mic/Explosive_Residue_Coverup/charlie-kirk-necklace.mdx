# Charlie Kirk's Necklace — Residue Search and Custody

:::caution Legal Disclaimer
Nothing on this page is a claim of fact that any living person or organization destroyed evidence, committed a crime, or acted with improper motive. Collecting a homicide victim's jewelry is ordinary procedure. This page documents **attributed public claims** about what happened to Charlie Kirk's necklace and why some investigators treat it as a residue surface. All named persons are presumed innocent.
:::

The silver box chain and cross pendant Kirk wore on September 10, 2025 sits at the center of the residue question. Investigators conclude a **device on his chest** flung it — and report that federal personnel were **urgently searching for the necklace because it would carry explosive residue**. The government-side reading attributes the snap to a **rifle bullet's cavitation**. No published lab result has been released for the chain.

This page is only about **custody and residue**. The competing device physics live on
[Exploding Pendant](/Mic/exploding-pendant-shaped-charge), [Mic vs Pendant](/Mic/Mic_vs_Pendant), and the
Necklace and Residue (Open Questions) page.

## What Candace Owens and amplifiers said

In a widely clipped segment amplified by
[@ProjectConstitu](https://x.com/ProjectConstitu/status/2067372027623715212) (June 17, 2026),
Candace Owens argued that the **rigged-microphone theory** makes several oddities line up at once. One of those items was the necklace:

> *"Another thing that sort of would lock into place: why the feds were searching for Charlie Kirk's necklace — same reason, explosive residue."*

In the same monologue she said the necklace was **"sitting directly on his chest when the device detonated,"** so it would be an obvious transfer surface for energetic compounds. Citizen lists of "anomalies a rigged mic would explain" — including the site's own
Rigged Mic Explains Anomalies page — put the same line as item three:
**feds searching for the necklace because it would carry residue.**

Owens is a living public commentator. Her statements are **reported claims**, not agency admissions. No FBI press release reviewed for this page has confirmed an "urgent residue hunt" for the pendant.

## Frank Turek's recovery account

A separate, first-person-style narrative comes from **Frank Turek**, Kirk's mentor, who was in the transport SUV. Circulating accounts of his interviews describe this sequence:

1. The necklace went **missing in the chaos** after the shot.
2. Federal or local personnel were **looking for it**.
3. Turek later found the cross **draped over his computer bag** inside the SUV used to rush Kirk to the hospital.
4. He retrieved it — in some retellings **after police cleared the vehicle**, or **before the vehicle was impounded** — and got it to **Erika Kirk**.

X commentary has stressed the tension: if the chain was **"violently flung"** in the blast (the device-camp reading), how does it end up **neatly on a bag** in the rear cabin? Posts from [@MsMaraboubou](https://x.com/MsMaraboubou), [@living4HisGlory](https://x.com/living4HisGlory), [@alleytopfiles](https://x.com/alleytopfiles/status/2024238574795620714), and [@ProjectConstitu](https://x.com/ProjectConstitu/status/2080108801068273973) flag that contradiction without resolving it.

**No wrongdoing by Turek is alleged here.** Finding a personal effect in a vehicle you just rode in is ordinary. What investigators want is the **evidence log**: when was the item logged, by which agency, and was it swabbed?

## Erika Kirk and the public necklace narrative

Within days of the killing, the necklace became a **public emotional centerpiece**:

- Erika was filmed **holding the necklace out a vehicle window** during a motorcade.
- She appeared at a studio podium **clutching the cross**.
- A New York Times interview and other early coverage included the line that **medics ripped the necklace from his body** while trying to stop the bleeding — a detail [@ProjectConstitu](https://x.com/ProjectConstitu/status/2080101268190597281) and others later challenged as inconsistent with the flung-off / bag-recovery accounts.
- Commentators including [@baroncoleman](https://x.com/baroncoleman/status/2006731899654516984) described **feds and Turek** as both "hysterically searching" for the piece, and asked why.

Citizen investigators (e.g. [@JosephLTrahan](https://x.com/JosephLTrahan/status/2070505693296087191)) argue the necklace and related personal items were **turned over to the widow too fast** for residual testing. Others note the opposite: **family keepsakes after a homicide are normal**, and displaying a cross is not evidence of a cover-up.

## Why residue investigators care

If a small charge detonated on or near Kirk's chest, the pendant and chain were **within centimeters of the event**. Post-blast residue (PETN, RDX, and related compounds in standard libraries) transfers to metal and fabric and can persist for long periods on stored items. That makes the necklace, in the device theory, a **cheap, decisive swab**:

| Outcome | Consequence for the exploding-mic / pendant theories |
|---------|------------------------------------------------------|
| Tested **negative** | Severely undercuts "residue must be on the chain" claims |
| Tested **positive** | Forces a forensic reopening of cause-of-death assumptions |
| **Never tested / never disclosed** | The question stays open indefinitely |

**No public residue result — positive or negative — has been released for the necklace.** That absence is the fact this section records. See also the
Forensic Tests Skipped page.

## The cavitation reading, and investigators' answer

Dr. Chris Martenson and others attribute the snap to **hydrodynamic cavitation** from a high-energy projectile: overpressure expands the neck and breaks the chain with **no explosive at all**. That reading supports the government's rifle narrative.

Investigators answer that frame-by-frame work by Jon Bray and others shows the shirt and necklace moving **before** visible blood, that the [six forensic physics phenomena](/Mic/six-forensic-physics-phenomena) thread finds no cavitation signature in the footage, and that collecting personal effects as a matter of course would still leave the chain available for a residue test — one that has never been made public.

## What has not been established

- That the FBI or any agent **destroyed** the necklace or ordered it untested.
- That Candace Owens, Frank Turek, Erika Kirk, or TPUSA committed any crime or obstruction.
- That any explosive residue exists on the chain, pendant, shirt, mic, or soil.
- Whether residue would confirm the explosion investigators have concluded killed him — no test has been released.

## Open questions

1. Was the necklace entered on any **evidence inventory** with a chain-of-custody number?
2. Was any **GC-MS / IMS explosive-residue panel** run, and does a lab report exist under seal?
3. Which account of recovery is accurate: **medics ripped it off**, **blast flung it**, or **found on Turek's bag** — and are those reconcilable?
4. When did custody pass from law enforcement to family, and under what authorization?
5. Would [Charlie Kirk Investigation Laws](/Fix/overview) compel disclosure of residue testing on personal effects?

Blaine Holt circulated on X on August 21, 2026, posted by [@tricia_dejong](https://x.com/tricia_dejong/status/2090891396265791653). The [shorter clip already covered on this site](./rob-oneill-navy-seal-testimony) carries the headline quote. ## Why This Cut Is Worth Its Own Page

The widely shared version of this interview is roughly a minute long and ends on the quotable line. The four-minute version is where O'Neill is asked to justify it — "What made you arrive at that conclusion? Explain that." — and answers with a specific physical argument rather than an impression. It also contains his account of the [lapel microphone](./rode-wireless-mic) after the shooting and his description of what happened when he began asking questions in public. Both bear directly on the [exploding-mic theory](./overview) and on the [cover-up questions](./Explosive_Residue_Coverup/overview) this section tracks.

## The Physical Argument About the Shirt

Pressed by the host to explain his conclusion, O'Neill reportedly gives a force argument rather than a wound-pattern argument:

> "Your shirt doesn't move like that. His shirt was moved by a force. What was it? The vapor trail behind a bullet is not carrying enough force to do that. Why did it blow out that way?"

The claim is narrow and testable in principle. A supersonic rifle bullet drags a low-pressure wake behind it; O'Neill's assertion is that this wake carries far too little energy to displace fabric the way the footage appears to show. He is not, in this segment, making a claim about what device was present — only that the observed fabric motion, in his experience, requires a nearby energy release rather than a passing projectile.

That claim is the same one examined frame by frame on the [video frame analysis](./mic-explosion-video-analysis) page, and from the acoustic side on the [Canon XA55 audio](./Canon_XA55_Audio) page. The one question defenders of the rifle narrative raise most often — why no breaching-size smoke and flash signature appears — is answered on the [shaped-charge demonstration](./Demo_of_shape_charge) page: a gram-scale charge under a shirt is not a breaching charge.

## "That Was Not a Bullet"

On the wound itself, O'Neill describes an immediate reaction rather than a considered analysis:

> "I'm a guy that has shot people at a closer range than that camera was to Charlie Kirk. And I've seen a lot of people get shot at that range. As soon as I saw the blood coming out of his neck, that was off. That was wrong. That was not a bullet."

He is explicit that this is instinct first: "I'm talking my immediate reaction. So my gut instinct is that's wrong. And then you start to further, you come out with what actually happened, and then you look at the responses from the security team."

He also frames his own credibility carefully: "It's not like I'm out there saying big lie or a conspiracy, or someone did it. I'm just saying, I've been in a lot of shootings." This is opinion testimony from an experienced combat veteran, offered on a television broadcast. It is not sworn testimony, it has not been cross-examined, and no forensic finding has been published that either confirms or contradicts it.

## The Security Response — "Gathering Equipment"

O'Neill's description of the seconds after the shooting is the part of this segment most relevant to the [residue cover-up](./Explosive_Residue_Coverup/overview) thread:

> "It doesn't appear to me it's stop the bleeding, stop the wound, where we take him, get him off the X. It really looked like they were gathering equipment, not trying to stop bleeding to me. And I'm talking about a combat veteran. I've been to 400 combat missions — that's not what you do."

He lists what he says he saw prioritized: retrieving SIM cards from cameras, removing cameras, and moving Kirk away from the site. He adds that he has been told the ground was paved over a couple of days later — a claim this site tracks separately on the [dirt replaced with cement](./Explosive_Residue_Coverup/dirt-replaced-with-cement) page.

O'Neill is describing priorities in the chaos as he saw them on video. Nothing indicates anyone on the security or AV team knew of any device, and this site makes no such claim about the people who reached Charlie.

## The Lapel Mic Question

The exchange that does not appear in the shorter clip is O'Neill's list of open questions, which begins with the microphone:

> "Because the lapel mic came off, went to someone else that ran off. What do they do with the SIM card? Did someone take that camera that was right behind him down? Why did they contaminate the crime scene immediately? Is it paved over? Did they rebuild it? Is all of that true? I don't know."

O'Neill does not allege that any individual acted improperly, and explicitly says he does not know whether the claims he is repeating are true. He is asking where the device went, not accusing whoever carried it; nothing indicates anyone on the security or AV team knew of any device. That question is the whole subject of this section: the [RØDE Wireless PRO](./rode-wireless-mic) page covers the hardware, the ["Get That Off Of Him" 911 call](./get-that-off-of-him-911-call) page covers the claim that something was taken off Kirk's body, and the [burnt object](./burnt-object-rode-transmitter) page covers a charred item reported in the transport vehicle. No public chain of custody for the transmitter has been released.

## What Happened When He Asked

The final exchange in this cut is about the response to the questions rather than the questions themselves:

> "But the second you ask — I'm anti-Semitic. I'm like, I didn't even bring up Israel. Why are you bringing up Israel? Is someone getting paid to defend it, for something I'm not even talking about? And all of a sudden I'm a conspiracy theorist?"

He closes:

> "I've killed a bunch of people for the country. A great American was killed in front of everyone in America. He was for this country. I would like to know why, and stop bullshitting us."

This site records the exchange as O'Neill described it on air. It does not follow from his account that any particular person or organisation is paid to respond to critics, and no such finding is made here. The broader pattern of question-asking being reframed as bigotry is tracked on the censorship pages.

## On Erika Kirk and Candace Owens

O'Neill also repeats, in this cut, the comparison that appears in the shorter clip:

> "And then you get Erika Kirk, behind all the razzle dazzle, and she's really quick to forgive the alleged shooter — which technically says that was him, because I forgive him — but you can't forgive Candace Owens for having an opinion or asking questions. And they mock people by saying, 'Oh, I'm just asking questions.' Yeah, I'm a SEAL Team 6 sniper. I'm asking a lot of questions. No one wants to answer them."

Erika Kirk has not responded publicly to this characterisation. Forgiving an accused person is not an admission about that person's guilt, and O'Neill's inference on that point is his own reading rather than an established fact. It is recorded here because it is what he said on the broadcast.

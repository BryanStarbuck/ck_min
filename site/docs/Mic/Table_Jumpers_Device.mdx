## The device-grab question

If the lavalier transmitter **detonated**, investigators expect:

- Fragment scatter on clothing, stage, and transport vehicle ([SUV photos](./suv-transporting-charlie))
- Absence of an intact TX unit in evidence photos
- Possible **magnetic clasp** ejected as projectile per [video analysis](./mic-explosion-video-analysis)

If it did **not** detonate fully, analysts ask where the unit went in the rush, before law enforcement
secured the scene. People who went over the table were reaching Charlie; nothing indicates anyone on the
security or AV team knew of any device.

Either way the question is the same: where is the transmitter, and why has no inventory of it been released?

## Stage chaos and security gap

Multiple threads describe people **on or over the table** within seconds:

- All-black-clad figures near Charlie (Suspects — Black Clothing)
- Other named persons in public commentary (Patsies & Distraction Actors)
- TPUSA staff moving toward cameras and SD media (UVU Tent)

## Camera-side chaos claims (related, not the same grab)

Separate from “hands on the transmitter,” public clips argue that **camera / recording gear** was moved or powered down within minutes — e.g. packages claiming that a camera was taken down and a chip removed before the area was treated as a sealed crime scene. Those are **evidence-handling** claims, not proof a mic pack was stolen, but they sit in the same post-shot chaos window as the table-jumper wish-list item. Nothing here establishes that any named cameraman committed a crime; ordinary panic and equipment rescue also occur after a shooting.

## Connection to AV team timeline

[Terryl Farnsworth](./AV_Team_Mic_Setup) was filming from a rock pile; timeline notes quote him
declaring the shooting and death within ~73 seconds. Investigators ask whether routine **equipment recovery** by staff
overlapped with the table-jumper window. We assume that every TPUSA employee and every audio-visual contractor working at UVU that day had no knowledge of any explosive that may have been in the mic, even if they handled the mic. Nothing indicates anyone on the security or AV team knew of any device.

## What evidence would answer it

- Unedited TPUSA stage-facing cameras from 6 feet in front of Kirk (publicly requested)
- Inventory of all wireless TX units returned after the event
- Photographs of Kirk's torso **before** body movement by responders
- Explosive-residue swabs on stage decking and clothing

## Public commentary

- Table-jumper and near-stage movement analyses shared on X are used by some commentators to argue **secondary device placement**; others say the same footage shows ordinary panic and people rendering aid.
- No public court filing has been reported to authenticate a recovered second device from the table area.
- These remain unverified theories, and timestamped primary video carries more weight than social-media captions.

**Also in the first seconds:** a hand pressing something under a security man's shirt at the table. We don't know if it was a mic, a radio, or anything being turned off — see *Video: What Was Pressed Under the Shirt?*

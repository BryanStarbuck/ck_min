# AV Team and Mic Setup

The exploding-mic theory depends on **how the microphone was routed** on
Charlie Kirk's body and on the chain of custody of the hardware. Investigators focus on TPUSA's AV chain at the September 10 UVU event.
We assume that every TPUSA employee and every audio-visual contractor working at UVU that day had no knowledge of any explosive that may have been in the mic, even if they handled the mic. Nothing indicates anyone on the security or AV team knew of any device.

## AV staffer — under-shirt placement

**An AV staffer** is described in project materials as the AV team member who mic'd
Charlie at UVU. The flagged anomaly:

- The [RØDE Wireless PRO](./rode-wireless-mic) transmitter was placed **under** Kirk's shirt
- The unit already had a **furry windscreen** designed for **external** outdoor clip-on use
- Prior TPUSA outdoor debates consistently show Kirk's lavalier on the **outside** of his shirt

Under-shirt routing positions the hardware against the torso. Running a lavalier under a shirt is ordinary
broadcast practice, and wind, wardrobe, or a quick change are all routine reasons for it. Nothing indicates
the AV staffer or anyone on the security or AV team knew of any device; a device built into the transmitter
would look identical to the real equipment to the person clipping it on.

## Terryl Farnsworth — early "He's dead" timeline

Timeline accounts in public discussion place **Terryl Farnsworth** on a rock pile filming
and quote him saying **"They just shot Charlie"** at ~00:52 and **"He's dead"** at ~01:06 —
roughly **73 seconds** after the shot. These are the words of someone reacting to what he saw;
nothing indicates he or anyone on the AV team knew of any device.

Farnsworth is also discussed on the UVU Tent page regarding SD-card handling claims
(unproven).

## Cooper Brown — standing mic / witness accounts

Cooper Brown appears in multiple conflicting social-media identifications
— event volunteer near Charlie, standing-mic role, and the
man seen steering a young TPUSA attendee away from a camera. The Witnesses page preserves the discrepancy
rather than resolving it.

### “Heard the mic explode” vs. “silencer” identifications

Early investigation notes in the master file record a **proponent claim** that a junior event worker identified in posts as Cooper Brown **heard an explosion from the microphone direction** and was **steered away from the camera** by another person before saying so. A widely shared Fox-segment clip amplified by [@DiligentDenizen](https://x.com/DiligentDenizen/status/1976419242666021122) (October 2025; later re-circulated) shows a man putting hands on a young Turning Point–adjacent person while covering his own ears — public discussion splits on **which face is Cooper Brown** and whether the blocked speech was about a **mic boom**, a **gunshot**, or ordinary panic.

Fox voiceover language in that package describes Brown as an **event volunteer ~15 feet** from Charlie when the scene unfolded — a softer affiliation than “TPUSA AV staff.” **None of these posts is a sworn statement.** They matter to the exploding-mic thread only as **attributed acoustic witnesses**: if someone next to the stage reported a **direction of sound consistent with the body pack** rather than a distant rooftop, that would parallel the [Canon XA55 two-event audio](./Canon_XA55_Audio) argument. Until the person on camera is stably identified and interviewed, treat “Cooper Brown heard the mic explode” as **unverified social-media narrative**, not a finding.

## Public commentary — AV / mic placement

Public discussion on social media has focused on equipment configuration.

- Citizen investigators continue to focus on **under-shirt** transmitter placement attributed in commentary to **an AV staffer** and timeline notes on **Terryl Farnsworth** — as **who configured what**.
- **@jonaaronbray**’s RØDE MagClip GO / bulge analysis is frequently cited as independent of who clipped the mic: if housing integrity failed, placement becomes forensically relevant.
- Defenders of the government's narrative say a wireless lav under clothing is common for outdoor PA redundancy. Investigators agree the routing itself is ordinary; what matters is that it put the transmitter against his chest and neck, where the [frame analysis](./mic-explosion-video-analysis) places the epicenter.

**Other under-shirt electronics:** a separate clip shows a security man's hand pressing something under a colleague's shirt after the hit. It is unknown whether it was a mic, a walkie-talkie, or neither — see *Video: What Was Pressed Under the Shirt?*

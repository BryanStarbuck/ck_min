On **September 2, 2026**, **Stew Peters** posted the Google Trends screenshot
shown here and attached claims to it about **Burton "Butch" Hibbs** and Fort Huachuca. This site does not repeat
or adopt those claims. The **screenshot** is a different and much narrower thing, and the two should not be read as
the same statement. This page separates them.

## What the screenshot actually shows

Every value below is read directly off the image. Nothing is inferred.

| Field | Value as shown |
|---|---|
| Search term | `"Burton Hibbs Calvalry Church"` — spelled *Calvalry* in the screenshot |
| Region | **Tucson (Sierra Vista) AZ** |
| Date range | 1/1/25 – 9/9/25 |
| Category | All categories |
| Search type | **Image Search** |
| Result | A **flat line at zero** across the entire eight-month window |
| Single spike | **September 7, 2025**, value **100** |

The chart is therefore an unusually clean shape: **nothing at all for eight months, one day of interest, nothing
after.** On a Google Trends chart, `100` is not a count of searches — it is the **normalised peak of whatever
happened in that window**. When a term is otherwise dead, a very small number of searches on one day will render as
a spike to 100. The chart shows the *timing* of interest, not its *volume*.

## Fort Huachuca is inside this geography — and that does not carry the claim

**Fort Huachuca** — the U.S. Army's intelligence and counterintelligence school — sits next to the town of
**Sierra Vista, Arizona**, and Sierra Vista is named in the Trends region on this chart. That geographic fact is
real, and it is why the screenshot was posted at all.

**It does not do the work the claim needs it to do.** Google Trends reports **metro-level buckets**, and the
Tucson (Sierra Vista) AZ bucket covers **most of southern Arizona and roughly a million people** — the city of
Tucson, its suburbs, Sierra Vista, and the surrounding counties. A spike in that bucket tells you the search
happened *somewhere in southern Arizona*. It cannot tell you:

* that any search came from **the base**, rather than from anywhere else in a metro of a million people;
* that any search came from a **government network**, or from anyone in uniform;
* **who** searched, or **why** they searched;
* anything at all about **Butch Hibbs**, who is the person **being searched for**, not the person searching.

That last point is the one most easily lost. **Being searched for is not an act you perform.** A Trends spike is
evidence about **whoever typed the words in**. It is not evidence about the man whose name they typed, and it
places him nowhere.

**This site does not claim that Butch Hibbs was at Fort Huachuca, travelled to Fort Huachuca, or was expected at
Fort Huachuca.** No manifest, visitor log, gate record, or travel document supporting any such thing has been
published by anyone, and until one is, there is nothing here to stand on.

Elsewhere on this site, an object in his front pocket is noted. We do not claim that he knew anything ahead of time or that he was involved in any way — only that whatever was in his pocket could continue to be investigated.

## Why the search term is the interesting part

The term is not his name. It is **`"Burton Hibbs Calvalry Church"`** — his **legal first name**, his surname, and
a misspelling of what is almost certainly **Calvary Chapel**, the denomination of his brother, Pastor
**Jack Hibbs**.

That shape is worth noticing, and it is the one genuinely odd feature of the screenshot:

* It uses **"Burton"**, not the **"Butch"** he is publicly known by — which suggests the searcher was working
  from a **written record** rather than from having heard the name spoken.
* It reaches him **through his brother's church**, not through his own public footprint — the search of someone
  trying to **identify or place a person** they had a name for but not a face.
* It was run as an **Image Search** — consistent with wanting to know **what he looks like**.
* It happened **once**, on **one day**, and never again in eight months.

None of that establishes who ran it or why. A journalist, a researcher, a churchgoer, a curious neighbour, or
someone who had just seen the name on a page all produce the same chart. It is recorded here because the
**date** is the part a reader can check against everything else on this site.

## How to check this yourself

Trends screenshots are cheap to make and easy to mis-frame, in both directions. The chart is reproducible while
Google keeps serving the data:

1. Open Google Trends → **Explore**.
2. Enter the exact quoted term `"Burton Hibbs Calvalry Church"`, including the misspelling.
3. Set region **Tucson (Sierra Vista) AZ**, range **1/1/25 – 9/9/25**, search type **Image Search**.
4. Compare against a **control region** — run the identical query for a metro with no connection to the case.
5. Compare against a **control term** — a similarly obscure name, same region, same window.

Steps 4 and 5 are the ones that matter and the ones the original post does not show. Without them there is no way
to tell a meaningful signal from the ordinary noise a near-zero search term produces. Note also that
[Trends terms have been claimed to vanish after going viral](./trends-deletion-suppression) — archive what you
find, with the date visible, before it changes.

## What would settle it

* **Base visitor and gate logs** for Fort Huachuca covering early September 2025, obtainable by FOIA or by the
  disclosure powers proposed in the [Charlie Kirk Investigation Laws](/Fix/overview).
* **Travel records** for the dates in question — which would speak to where anyone actually was, as opposed to
  what was typed into a search box.
* **Raw Trends data with a preserved before-state**, rather than a single screenshot taken by the party making
  the claim.

Until at least one of those exists, this remains a screenshot of a search chart and a claim built on top of it.

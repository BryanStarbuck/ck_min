**country/region filter**, not a verified list of Mossad workstations. Posts allege **months-early** interest in
case subjects. **Attribution is disputed; intent is unknown.**

## Citizen Investigator Claims on X (Attributed)

Google Trends / search-pattern claims remain among the **most contested** OSINT threads.

**Pro-pattern voices** (Baron Coleman ecosystem; **@ZachCostello_**; **@denton_doyle**) argue pre-event Trends activity on names, hospital, surgeon, rifle-drop locations, and Israel/DC/Alabama geos is irregular enough to investigate.

**Skeptic voices** (**@paramounttactcl**, **@MaxNordau** summarizing Tim Pool) argue Trends is **sampled noise**, unreproducible, and that Google has told media it is not a historical record—especially at single-digit volumes. Joel Webbon-style "Trends prove Israel killed Kirk" claims are treated by skeptics as statistical malpractice.

**Israel IP** claims are the flashpoint: proponents see coordinated pre-event queries; **@MaxNordau**/**@paramounttactcl** call them noise. No court has authenticated IP-level Trends attribution.

**Rule used on this site:** Trends anomalies are **leads**, not identification of a living searcher, and never proof of foreign-state murder.

Whether leads like these ever received formal follow-up is the question raised by former NCTC director Joe Kent, who has stated on the record that his center's parallel review of potential foreign ties was halted before such threads could be run to ground.

*Commentator coverage:* Citizen journalist Ian Carroll has cited the Google Trends foreign-IP search-pattern data in his public commentary as one of the anomalies he argues deserves formal investigation, while cautioning — in his words — that raising the Israel question is "a perfectly reasonable question to ask" and does not mean any state carried out the killing.

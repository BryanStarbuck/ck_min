# Journalists Kept Outside

Even the press could not get near the **Serena Hotel** during the April 2026 talks. Journalists are usually the group with the most access short of officials. Accredited reporters were kept in a separate building and did not see the delegations arrive or leave. This shows how tight the cordon was.

## Accreditation and entry to the country

* Pakistan's Ministry of Information set up a **Media Facilitation Centre** at the **Jinnah Convention Centre**, a government venue in the Red Zone. Information Minister Attaullah Tarar said it gave journalists all facilities "under one roof" ([Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2602182/media-facilitation-centre-set-up-for-journalists-covering-islamabad-talks)).
* About **50 foreign journalists** came. **Visa on arrival** was offered only to the U.S. and Iranian delegations and to journalists covering the talks ([Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2602182/media-facilitation-centre-set-up-for-journalists-covering-islamabad-talks)).
* A government photo shows the inside of the media centre ([APP photo](https://www.app.com.pk/photos-section/an-interior-view-of-the-modern-media-center-established-at-the-jinnah-convention-centre-to-facilitate-national-and-international-journalists-covering-the-historic-islamabad-talks-2026-in-the-federal-c/)).

## Held half a kilometre away

* AFP reported that the hub was about **half a kilometre** from the talks. Journalists were held at checkpoints for **about an hour** while convoys passed. There were no briefings, and news came as **WhatsApp statements** ([AFP via France 24](https://www.france24.com/en/live-news/20260411-buffets-baristas-but-no-briefings-journalists-frozen-out-of-iran-talks)).
* Dawn reported that reporters were gathered at a hotel and **bussed in by the Information Ministry**, with **no access to the venue**. Reporters also complained of weak mobile internet at the centre ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1990894)).
* Al Jazeera's media institute reported that journalists **could not see delegates entering or leaving** the Serena ([Al Jazeera Media Institute](https://institute.aljazeera.net/en/ajr/article/3634)).
* AFP recorded a guard at the hub telling a reporter, "Foreign media is here, and they are watching" ([AFP via France 24](https://www.france24.com/en/live-news/20260411-buffets-baristas-but-no-briefings-journalists-frozen-out-of-iran-talks)).

## The second round

Before the planned second round, *The News* reported that **special passes** would go only to foreign media, Pakistani television anchors and reporters on the Foreign Ministry beat ([The News](https://www.thenews.pk/print/1411133-can-war-return-as-pakistan-preps-to-host-the-final-round)). Residents along the route from **Nur Khan Airbase** to the Serena were told **not to film official convoys** ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/04/19/evacuations-start-at-marriott-serena-hotels-for-next-round-of-us-iran-talks/)).

## The one outside view

The only reported look inside the cordon came from **One Constitution Avenue**, the residential tower next to the Serena. Journalist Talat Hussain said the delegations were filmed from there ([MM News](https://mmnews.tv/what-actually-is-the-one-constitution-avenue-scandal/)). That was a view from outside, not a way in. See [Islamabad's Red Zone](./red_zone).

## Why it matters

Most of what the public knows about who was inside the Serena comes from official statements and a few reporters who were escorted in afterward, such as *The National*'s walk-through ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2026/04/13/inside-us-iran-talks-venue-in-islamabad-where-remnants-of-tense-diplomacy-linger/)). No independent observer logged who came and went. The only full record of who passed through the gates is held by the Pakistani state and the U.S. Secret Service.

# Who Could Get Inside the Serena

The argument that makes the Serena claim important is simple. If four phones from the UVU set were really inside the **Islamabad Serena Hotel** on the eve of the talks, the people carrying them were not tourists. Contemporary reporting shows that by then an ordinary person could not book a room, walk into the lobby, or even drive down the road to the gate. This page collects that reporting.

## The hotel was handed to the government

* **Guests were evicted.** Paying guests were asked to check out days before the talks. Access was "restricted to official delegations" ([Hospitality Career Profile](https://hospitalitycareerprofile.com/islamabad-serena-hotel-us-iran-talks-diplomatic-hub)). Al Jazeera's reconstruction: "Guests were asked to check out, floors secured, staff vetted" ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/4/13/how-the-us-iran-talks-in-islamabad-unfolded)).
* **Government control.** The property was "placed under full government control for the duration of the talks, with operations temporarily suspended" ([Hospitality Career Profile](https://hospitalitycareerprofile.com/islamabad-serena-hotel-us-iran-talks-diplomatic-hub)).
* **"Diplomatic fortress."** A Pakistani security official used that phrase to *The National*, which reported that "nearly all the staff were restricted to certain floors or kept off the property for the entire period" ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2026/04/13/inside-us-iran-talks-venue-in-islamabad-where-remnants-of-tense-diplomacy-linger/)).
* **The second round repeated it.** On April 19, ahead of an expected second round, the hotel told remaining guests they had to leave "due to a government event" and stopped taking reservations "until further notice" ([Al Arabiya](https://english.alarabiya.net/News/middle-east/2026/04/19/islamabad-ramps-up-security-near-hotels-as-preparations-appear-under-way-for-usiran-talks)). Pakistan's foreign minister **Ishaq Dar** later thanked the public for its patience while the Serena and the Red Zone stayed closed, and the hotel reopened on Monday, April 27.

## Staff were vetted and kept on site

Al Jazeera reports the staff were "vetted." Circulated accounts of the talks add that the staff working the event went through expedited background checks and ate and slept on the premises during the roughly 20-hour negotiation. *The National* reports that the rest were confined to certain floors or kept off the property ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2026/04/13/inside-us-iran-talks-venue-in-islamabad-where-remnants-of-tense-diplomacy-linger/)). Either way, the staff inside were a known, cleared list.

## Inside, the building was partitioned

Operational staff told Reuters that the talks ran across "two separate wings and one common area": one wing for the U.S. side, one for the Iranians, and a common area for trilateral meetings with the Pakistani mediators ([The New Arab, citing Reuters](https://www.newarab.com/news/us-iran-leave-door-open-dialogue-after-tense-islamabad-talks)). *The National* walked the building afterward and described:

* Vance and Iranian speaker Ghalibaf meeting face to face in "a small conference room at one end of the lobby," with the usual furniture replaced by lounge seating.
* A **Pakistan Foreign Ministry** office opposite the negotiating corridor, and the **Army Chief's** office nearby, with stairs leading up to a **U.S.-secured area**.
* Vance's private office, restricted to senior U.S. officials and the **Secret Service**.
* A banquet hall with a triangular table and three flags, prepared for a joint announcement that never happened.

The U.S. delegation **slept at the Serena**. The Iranians were driven over from the Marriott ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2026/04/26/pakistan-trying-to-broker-us-iran-deal-but-prospects-keep-slipping-away/)).

## Phones were restricted

On April 14, *The Daily Jagran* reported that "no phones were allowed inside the Serena Hotel" during the talks. Other accounts are narrower: phones were banned in the **negotiating room**, and delegates stepped outside during breaks to report to their governments. The narrower version fits better with delegations that were in constant contact with their capitals. Al Jazeera notes the U.S. side was repeatedly in touch with Washington and the President ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/4/13/how-the-us-iran-talks-in-islamabad-unfolded)). Either way, phone use inside the building was controlled, and the controllers knew whose phones were there.

## Outside, the city was locked down

* More than **10,000** police and security personnel were deployed, along with Rangers, paramilitary forces and army coordination ([Wikipedia: Islamabad Talks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamabad_Talks)).
* Pakistan declared public holidays on **April 9–10** to empty the streets.
* Roads to the hotel were sealed and traffic diverted. Barbed wire and heavy police presence surrounded the hotel ([Al Arabiya](https://english.alarabiya.net/News/middle-east/2026/04/19/islamabad-ramps-up-security-near-hotels-as-preparations-appear-under-way-for-usiran-talks)).
* Journalists were kept at the **Jinnah Convention Centre** and shuttled over. They did not stay in the hotel.
* The hotel sits inside the [Red Zone](./red_zone), which is closed to the general public even on ordinary days.

## Why this matters for the phone claim

Taken together, the reporting leaves a short list of people who could have been inside the Serena on the evening of April 10:

1. Pakistani government, military and police personnel running the security operation.
2. Foreign **advance teams**: the security and logistics staff who arrive before a delegation. The U.S. principals did not land until the next afternoon.
3. Vetted hotel staff.
4. From April 11, the accredited delegations themselves ([Who Was There](./delegations)).

That is why citizen investigators treat the Serena half of the claim as the stronger half. A UVU crowd of about 3,000 is hard to account for, but the access list for a sealed diplomatic hotel was short and fully written down. As the X account @JGlasseir put it, the authorities who could "reasonably have had access to the Serena Hotel" to identify such devices "would have been Pakistani authorities." So if the four phones existed, the Pakistani state already knows whose they were.

This page does not say that any delegate, official or staff member carried one of the phones. The claim itself has not been verified ([The Phone Claim](./phones_uvu_to_serena)). The point is narrower: if the devices were there, their owners were on a short, recorded list.

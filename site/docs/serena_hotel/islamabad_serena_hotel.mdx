# The Islamabad Serena Hotel

<img src="/img/serena_hotel/serena_islamabad_interior_corridor.jpg"
  style={{float:'right', width:'48%', marginLeft:'1.5rem', marginBottom:'1rem'}}
  alt="Arched interior corridor of the Islamabad Serena Hotel, photographed in 2010. Photo: atifullahkhan, CC BY 3.0, Wikimedia Commons." />

The **Islamabad Serena Hotel** is a five-star hotel in Islamabad's government district. Pakistan's security services already know how to seal it, which is why the government uses it for its most sensitive guests. In April 2026 it hosted the first direct high-level U.S.–Iran talks since 1979.

## Facts about the property

* **Opened:** March 12, 2002. President **Pervez Musharraf** inaugurated it in the presence of the **Aga Khan** ([AKDN press release](https://the.akdn/en/resources-media/whats-new/news-release/aga-khan-joins-president-musharraf-inauguration-islamabad-serena-hotel)).
* **Ownership:** part of the Serena Hotels chain. The Aga Khan Fund for Economic Development is the largest shareholder, and it is managed through Tourism Promotion Services ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/pakistan/inside-islamabad-serena-hotels-transformation-from-luxury-hideaway-to-geopolitical-hotspot-in-pakistan-1.500506208); [Hospitality Career Profile](https://hospitalitycareerprofile.com/islamabad-serena-hotel-us-iran-talks-diplomatic-hub)). A post on X by Zahir Rahimtoola said the Aga Khan hosted the summit free of charge. That is unconfirmed.
* **Size:** 387 rooms at opening, "more than 400" today, on **5.5 hectares** (about 13.6 acres) of gardens, with a grand ballroom, several conference halls, and an office complex ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2026/04/13/inside-us-iran-talks-venue-in-islamabad-where-remnants-of-tense-diplomacy-linger/)).
* **Design:** traditional Pakistani architecture, with open-air terraces, pavilions, hand-hewn marble, and thousands of square feet of hand-painted wooden ceiling panels, facing the Margalla Hills and Rawal Lake.
* **Location:** on Constitution Avenue next to the **Ministry of Foreign Affairs**, inside the [Red Zone](./red_zone), within reach of the Diplomatic Enclave and the U.S. Embassy. The 22-storey **One Constitution Avenue** tower is next door.

## Why governments use it

The Serena works as a diplomatic venue because it can be isolated. It stands in its own grounds, can be entered only through gates set well back from the main buildings, is next to the Foreign Ministry, and sits inside a district that is already walled off from ordinary traffic. The same qualities made it the venue again when a second round was planned for late April 2026: the hotel told guests to leave "due to a government event" and stopped taking bookings ([Al Arabiya](https://english.alarabiya.net/News/middle-east/2026/04/19/islamabad-ramps-up-security-near-hotels-as-preparations-appear-under-way-for-usiran-talks)).

## Security history of the chain

Serena hotels in the region have been attacked before, which explains the chain's hardened posture. The **Kabul Serena** was attacked in 2008 and again in 2014 ([Wikipedia: Serena Hotel attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serena_Hotel_attack)). The **Quetta Serena** was bombed in 2021 ([Wikipedia: Quetta Serena Hotel bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quetta_Serena_Hotel_bombing)). Some summaries of the Islamabad talks say the *Islamabad* property itself was attacked in 2008. We found no record of that. The well-known 2008 attack in the city was the truck bombing of the **Islamabad Marriott**, the hotel where the Iranian delegation stayed in 2026.

## The rooms used for the talks

After the talks, *The National* walked the building ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2026/04/13/inside-us-iran-talks-venue-in-islamabad-where-remnants-of-tense-diplomacy-linger/)):

* The face-to-face session took place in a small conference room at one end of the lobby, refurnished with lounge seating.
* A Pakistan Foreign Ministry office stood opposite the negotiating corridor.
* The Army Chief's office was nearby, with stairs up to a U.S.-secured area.
* Vance's private office sat at the foot of those stairs, open only to senior U.S. officials and the Secret Service.
* The Iranian delegation had its own office area.
* A banquet hall held a triangular table with U.S., Iranian and Pakistani flags for an announcement that never took place. By 11 PM the setup had been cleared, leaving "discarded US and Iranian placards" and three Iranian flags in a spare room.

A white box truck was parked on the street across from [UVU](./overview) on **September 10,
2025**. Citizen journalist @DiligentDenizen has called it a
**"command center"** since first pointing out an antenna array on its roof. On **August 29,
2026** he published a side-by-side arguing the identification is now firmer: a
**special-operations SWAT vehicle** photographed in **St George, Utah** that day carries, in
his reading, **the same two roof units**.

This is a **visual equipment comparison**, not a document. What follows is his claim, the
image, and the limits.

## The post

According to [@DiligentDenizen](https://x.com/DiligentDenizen/status/2093546357999243481),

## What the image actually shows

The graphic is two stacked panels with hand-drawn annotation.

**Top panel** — a ground-level photograph of a **white box truck marked S.W.A.T.** with a
police-star emblem on the flank, a marked pickup either side, and an operator in fatigues
walking past. **Three roof features are circled in red** with arrows drawn up to them: a low
dome toward the front, a larger white rectangular housing at the centre, and a small dark
protrusion toward the rear.

**Bottom panel** — an **overhead frame** of a white box truck parked in a lot beside a
building, with heavy equipment and other white vehicles around it. **Two roof features are
circled in red and labelled in yellow**: **"AC"** on the rectangular housing, and
**"COMS ANTENNA"** on the small dark protrusion.

The argument is that the AC housing and the antenna in the lower frame match the same two
features in the upper one. **The labels are the poster's, added to the image** — they are his
identification of what the shapes are, not markings on the vehicle.

## What it would establish, and what it would not

**If the match holds**, it says the vehicle across from UVU was of a **type** carrying
incident-command communications gear — which is what "command center" would mean in the
ordinary sense.

**It does not follow** that the two trucks are the same vehicle, the same unit, or the same
agency. Boxed public-safety vehicles are built out by a small number of upfitters from a
short list of catalogue parts; a rooftop A/C unit and a whip or dome antenna in comparable
positions is what that industry produces by default, across agencies and states. **Common
components are the expected result of a common supplier, not evidence of a common operator.**

Three specific limits apply to this particular comparison:

* **No plate, no fleet number, no agency marking** is legible on the UVU vehicle in the
  overhead frame — the identification rests entirely on roof silhouettes.
* **The two frames are at completely different angles and resolutions** — a ground-level
  side view against a compressed overhead. Matching small roof shapes across those two
  viewpoints is exactly the comparison that is easiest to get wrong.
* **The St George photograph is from August 2026**, roughly a year after the assassination,
  and its own context is not established here.

Neither the vehicle's owner nor its purpose has been confirmed by any public record this
investigation has seen.

## Why it is kept

A marked, equipped vehicle sitting across the street from an event where a man was killed is
a reasonable thing to want identified, and it is identifiable in principle: **fleet records,
a plate, a dispatch log, or a mutual-aid request would settle it outright.** That is the sort
of record the proposed [disclosure laws](/Fix/overview) are aimed at, and it is a far better
route than matching silhouettes.

It also sits next to a separate surveillance-equipment claim already on this site — the
[Stingray/IMSI-catcher allegation](./Stingray-Device) — and the two should not be merged.
They are different claims about different equipment from different sources, and neither is
established.

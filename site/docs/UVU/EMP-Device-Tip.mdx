# The EMP-Device Tip UVU Received the Morning of September 10

At about 7:30 a.m. on September 10, 2025, roughly five hours before Charlie Kirk was killed, UVU received an anonymous tip. It warned of an **electromagnetic-pulse (EMP) device** that a suspect might use at the event. This is not a rumor. It is written in UVU's own commissioned [After-Action Report](./After_Action_Report). This page sets out what the report says, then what commentators on X connect it to, and the questions nobody has answered.

## What the After-Action Report says

The report (published September 25, 2026) states:

> "Chief Long also contacted SIAC by e-mail at approximately 7:30 a.m. on September 10 to share an anonymous tip UVU received regarding a potential threat involving an electronic-magnetic-pulse device that a suspect might use at the event. Chief Long was seeking to determine whether any threat-related intelligence update was available regarding the tip."

According to the report, the Utah SIAC (the state intelligence fusion center) answered that the threat was not credible, because the tipster "had provided similar information in the past that was determined to be unreliable" (report p. 42). The report does not say who sent the tip, what exactly it described, or whether anyone searched the courtyard, the stage, or the vehicles for a device.

The same report describes UVU Police Chief Jeff Long's first security call with TPUSA Security Supervisor Dan Flood: "Discussion focused on arrival, departure, and parking logistics." The report also says Flood did not respond to the review team's calls, voicemail, or certified letter. You can <a href="/reports/uvu-after-action-report/">read the full report page by page</a>.

### Key statements from the video

From the video by @JOKAQARMY1, September 27, 2026:

* **The tip.** "UVU released their third-party independent security review of Charlie Kirk's assassination today, and it turns out that the morning of September 10th at 7:30 AM, UVU received an anonymous tip regarding a potential threat involving an electromagnetic pulse device that a suspect might use at the event." (This matches the report text quoted above.)
* **What an EMP does.** "An EMP is a short burst of electromagnetic energy that can affect the power grid, cause disruptions with electronic equipment, and disrupt communications."
* **Electronics that went dark.** He cites Candace Owens's reporting that some UVU security cameras blacked out about two minutes before the shot. He also points to the gap in Officer Bagley's body-cam footage when Bagley reached the [Losee Center](./Losee_Center) roof.
* **Security's priorities.** He says the report shows Charlie's head of security "was not worried about the rooftops." Instead, Flood focused on "the arrival, the departure, and the parking logistics," and on the SUV having "a clean exit." (The report confirms the arrival, departure, and parking focus. The "not worried about rooftops" wording is his.)
* **Where EMP devices are carried.** "Manmade EMPs that are used by the military are often built into trucks, trailers, aircraft, because they often need a bulky power source." He adds that compact "suitcase versions" exist too. He asks whether one could ride in an SUV.
* **Suitcases and the SUV.** He notes that footage released by Candace Owens shows Charlie's security unloading suitcases from the SUVs after returning to campus from the hospital. He also notes that the SUV that carried Charlie was stripped and sold at auction.
* **The tipster.** He asks who sent the tip and why.

The video also names people connected to EMP work by profession. It says Erika Kirk once appeared as an interviewer in an EMP documentary, and that her mother, Lori Frantzve, runs Scottsdale, Arizona companies focused on EMP hardening. **This site has not verified either claim.** EMP hardening is lawful, protective work, and neither woman is accused here of any wrongdoing or of any knowledge of the tip. We include the claim only because it is part of what the post says.

The video itself adds a caution on screen: "Real EMP or high-power microwave systems that can affect electronics at any useful distance are generally large. Many are built into trucks, trailers, aircraft, or missiles because they need bulky power sources, capacitors or generators, and antennas. Compact 'suitcase' or easily SUV-portable versions that work over a wide area are mostly fiction or highly specialized military equipment whose details are not public."

It also shows a nighttime photo of a black SUV with its trunk open and people standing around it, and a still of a man near the "Prove Me Wrong" tent, circled.

## The report, hour by hour: what else surrounds the tip

The EMP line does not sit alone. Here is what UVU's After-Action Report puts around it, in the report's own words.

**Weeks before: an anonymous caller.** The report's planning timeline (p. 18) records: "An anonymous caller demanded cancellation." It does not say whether this caller and the EMP tipster were the same person.

**September 8, 3:12 p.m.: the roof text.** The report prints this exchange verbatim:

> **Dan Flood:** Hello Chief Long — We received this message today from the student group. FYSA "there is student roof access pretty close to where CK will be set up at Utah Valley (The Sorensen Student center has a couple stair cases that go up to the walkways on the roof)"
>
> **Chief Long:** You want access to the roof?
>
> **Dan Flood:** I was told students have access above us, if this is true, it would be nice to either have it controlled access or allow one of my guys to be there as well. If possible.
>
> **Chief Long:** I got you covered
>
> **Dan Flood:** Thank you, Sir.

The report says this exchange "reveals the primary security focus for TPUSA was the Hall of Flags outdoor pedestrian walkway above the canopy," and that Long's "We got you covered" referred to that walkway. **That is not the [Losee Center](./Losee_Center) roof**, where charging documents place the shooter. So the one roof both sides discussed in writing was not the roof that mattered. This correction matters for anyone who reads the video's line that security "was not worried about the rooftops." Flood did raise a roof, just a different one.

**September 10, 7:23–7:30 a.m.: the EMP tip.** The incident-day timeline gives the time as 7:23 a.m. The report's narrative section gives it as "approximately 7:30 a.m." and adds that "Long was advised e-mail was the best method to request information from the Utah SIAC. SIAC responded shortly thereafter to Chief Long that it was not a credible threat, as it appeared the tipster had provided similar information in the past that was determined to be unreliable." Posters have asked a fair question: how did SIAC tie an *anonymous* tip to a specific past tipster?

**About 9:00 a.m.: the security team arrives, with no introductions.** "The TPUSA security team arrived at UVU with Dan Flood at approximately 9:00 a.m. Chief Long reported that he was not formally introduced to the members of the TPUSA security team upon their arrival, as he did not reach out to them to meet, nor did TPUSA security staff reach out to him to do so." Flood texted Long at 9:04 a.m. that he had arrived.

The report records no step between 7:30 a.m. and the event that follows up on the EMP tip. It does not say the tip was passed to TPUSA's team, to Orem police, or to anyone sweeping the venue.

The EMP line was one sentence in a 158-page report. Within 48 hours it became one of the most shared details in the whole document.

The same post says EMP equipment is tested at Fort Huachuca's Electromagnetic Environmental Test Facility.
* [@nnnnnevaeh](https://x.com/nnnnnevaeh/status/2103659676286210180), September 26 (**5,601 views, 262 likes**): Erika Frantzve "previously appeared in Black Start, a documentary about threats to the U.S. power grid, alongside EMP expert Peter Pry…"
* Posts and the video's own overlay describe Lori Frantzve as founder and longtime principal of Scottsdale companies in EMP hardening and critical-infrastructure protection, including AZ-Tech International. The overlay cites ZoomInfo as a source.

Read these for what they are. A family's professional work on *protecting* equipment from EMPs is lawful and public. It is not evidence that anyone used an EMP device or knew about the tip. Several posts ask why the tipster chose "EMP" in particular. That question is about the tipster, not about the family.

## Open questions

* Who sent the tip, and what exactly did it say about the device, the suspect, or the location?
* Earlier tips from the same person were called "unreliable." What were they about, and were any of them later confirmed?
* After the tip, did anyone sweep the tent, the stage, the sound gear, or the vehicles?
* Did the camera outage and the body-cam gap have a technical cause that has been documented? If so, where is that record?
* Why did TPUSA's security supervisor not answer the review team?

An EMP disables electronics. It does not by itself explain the wound. Citizen investigators on X mostly conclude that Charlie died from an explosive, the [exploding-microphone theory](/Mic/overview). An EMP-type device, if one existed, would fit best as a way to blind cameras and radios at the key moment, not as the cause of death.

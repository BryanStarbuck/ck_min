BEFORE THE GOVERNMENT RECORDS OFFICE OF THE STATE OF UTAH

KLAYTON INKLEY, Petitioner, v.

UTAH VALLEY UNIVERSITY, Respondent.

DECISION AND ORDER

Appeal No. 2025-148

By this appeal, Petitioner, Klayton Inkley, seeks access to records allegedly held by Respondent, Utah Valley University ("UVU").

BACKGROUND

On September 18, 2025, Mr. Inkley submitted a request for records pursuant to the Government Records Access and Management Act ("GRAMA") to UVU. Mr. Inkley requested any contracts, purchase orders, invoices, memoranda of understanding, or agreements with private security providers related to the Charlie Kirk Turning Point event held at UVU on September 10, 2025. Mr. Inkley also requested any procurement documentation identifying the private security company or agency used for the event. Charlie Kirk was fatally shot during the event at UVU and Tyler Robinson has been charged in relation to the shooting.

On October 10, 2025, the UVU Records Officers denied Mr. Inkley's records request. The denial stated that the requested records are records regarding security measures designed for the protection of people or property, which are not subject to GRAMA pursuant to Utah Code § 63G-2-106. Alternatively, UVU classified the records as protected under Utah Code § 63G-2-305(10) because disclosure would interfere with the ongoing criminal investigation into the shooting death of Mr. Kirk.

Mr. Inkley appealed to UVU's Chief Administrative Officer for GRAMA, Vice President Val Peterson, asserting that the security exemptions "were applied too broadly." Mr. Inkley argued that identifying a vendor is a "routine procurement fact" and not a tactical security plan and disclosure of such information "would not interfere with any ongoing enforcement proceeding." On October 24, 2025, Vice President Peterson denied Mr. Inkley's appeal stating:

There is an ongoing criminal investigation and prosecution related to the Turning Point event that occurred on UVU campus September 10, 2025, the same event for which you seek after-action reports, incident reports, coordination logs, and communications referencing private security. Releasing any records relating to the security for, and response to, this event, whether provided by University police or by private security, could interfere with those proceedings. As such, even if the records were not excluded under § 63G-2-106, they would remain protected and not publicly releasable under Utah Code Ann. § 63G-2-305(10)(a-c).

Mr. Inkley filed an appeal of this decision with the Government Records Office ("Office") on October 27, 2025. After the appeal was filed, Utah County submitted a Request for Intervention pursuant to Utah Code § 63G-2-403(6), which was granted on January 13, 2026. Tyler Robinson also filed a Request for Intervention, which was granted on January 13, 2026. On January 22, 2026, the Director of the Office ("Director") held a public hearing at which the parties were allowed to present their evidence and arguments. Having carefully considered the parties' presentations and the applicable law, the Director issues the following Decision and Order.

STATEMENT OF REASONS FOR DECISION

1. GRAMA specifies that a "record is public unless otherwise expressly provided by statute." Utah Code § 63G-2-201(2). Records that are designated as "private," "protected," or "controlled," are not public. See Utah Code §§ 63G-2-201(3)(a), -302, -303, -304 and -305.

2. Records of a governmental entity or political subdivision regarding security measures designed for the protection of persons or property, public or private, are not subject to GRAMA. Utah Code § 63G-2-106(1).

3. Records created or maintained for criminal enforcement purposes, are protected non-public records if properly classified by a governmental entity, and release of the records: (1) Reasonably could be expected to interfere with investigations undertaken for enforcement; (2) Reasonably could be expected to interfere with enforcement proceedings; or (3) Would create a danger of depriving a person of a right to a fair trial or impartial hearing. Utah Code § 63G-2-305(10)(a-c).

4. Counsel for UVU argued that the police coordination records are security measures not subject to GRAMA pursuant to Utah Code § 63G-2-106. Counsel further argued that release of the records regarding the security at the Turning Point event "would reasonably impair the ongoing police investigation into the incident" and therefore, should be classified as protected records pursuant to Utah Code § 63G-2-305(10)(a-c). Counsel for Intervenor Tyler Robinson similarly argued that the records are properly classified as protected records pursuant to Utah Code § 63G-2-305(10)(a) & (c).

5. The Director finds that the requested "incident reports, coordination logs, after-action reports, or communications referencing private security" are intrinsic to UVU's security procedures. Because these records contain information regarding how UVU Police coordinate with external assets, their response posture, and deployment strategies, they fall squarely within the exclusion from GRAMA under Section 106.

6. Mr. Inkley contends that the requested procurement contracts and vendor names are financial records, not records of security measures, and that such standard procurement materials should be releasable with limited redactions (e.g., account numbers or signatures). However, UVU counters that revealing such records would expose "patterns, preferences, or operational gaps" that could be exploited by bad actors.

7. The Director disagrees with UVU's assertion that the requested contracts and related procurement records qualify as records of security measures which are entirely exempt from GRAMA under Section 106. Under Utah Code § 63G-2-301(2)(j) "documentation of the compensation that a governmental entity pays to a contractor or private provider" is public. Moreover, under Utah Code § 63G-2-301(3)(b)-(d) contracts entered into by a governmental entity, as well as records documenting services provided by a contractor or private provider and compliance with the terms of a contract, are normally public. However, access to such records may be restricted under Subsection 63G-2-201(3)(b), Section 63G-2-302, 63G-2-304, or 63G-2-305 to the extent that a record is expressly exempt from disclosure.

8. Although the procurement records requested here are subject to GRAMA, the Director finds that they are properly classified as protected under Utah Code § 63G-2-305(10)(a)-(c) because their release reasonably could be expected to interfere with investigations and enforcement proceedings and would create a danger of depriving Mr. Robinson of his right to a fair trial or impartial hearing.

9. After consideration and weighing of the various interests and public policies pertinent to the classification and disclosure or nondisclosure of the procurement records, the Director finds that the public interests favoring access are not greater than or equal to the interests favoring restriction of access at this time. Thus, the Director declines to order release of these records pursuant to Utah Code § 63G-2-403(11)(b).

ORDER

THEREFORE, IT IS ORDERED THAT the appeal of Petitioner, Klayton Inkley, is DENIED.

RIGHT TO APPEAL

A party to this proceeding may seek review of the Director's order or decision by filing a petition for judicial review in District Court as provided in Utah Code § 63G-2-404. Utah Code § 63G-2-403(14). A petition for judicial review "shall be filed no later than 30 days" after the date of the order or decision pursuant to Utah Code § 63G-2-404(1)(a), except as provided in Utah Code § 63G-2-404(1)(b). The petition is a complaint governed by the Utah Rules of Civil Procedure and shall contain the required information listed in Subsection -404(2). Utah Code § 63G-2-404(2). The court shall make its decision de novo but shall allow introduction of evidence presented to the Director, determine all questions of fact and law without a jury, and decide the issue at the earliest practical opportunity. Utah Code § 63G-2-404(6). In order to protect its rights on appeal, a party may wish to seek advice from an attorney.
